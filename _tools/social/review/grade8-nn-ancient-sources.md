# Grade 8 NN ancient history and art corrections

Reviewed 24 November 2026. Existing textbook extracts adapted; original media retained. Answer positions and worksheet completions reconciled.

- UNESCO Aksum, https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/15/ : kingdom mainly first to eighth centuries CE; surviving stelae third to fourth centuries. Replaces 400 BCE founding and blanket attribution to Ezana.
- UNESCO Harar Jugol, https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1189/ : three mosques date to the tenth century, but capital status was 1520–1568. Separates these claims.
- UNESCO Great Zimbabwe, https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/364/ : Shona trading centre of eleventh to fifteenth centuries. Removes unsupported precise Kalanga founding.
- Boston University African Studies Center, https://www.bu.edu/africa/research/projects/african-ajami/ : modified Arabic scripts used for African languages. Removes single-origin assertion in Ancient Ghana and changes the question accordingly.
- Archaeology archive, https://archive.archaeology.org/9901/newsbriefs/giraffe.html : Dabous dating is approximate. No exact 6500 BCE or earliest-African-art assertion retained.
- Metropolitan Museum, https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/the-age-of-iron-in-west-africa : possible Nok smelting and forging by sixth century BCE. No continent-wide 1500 BCE Iron Age.
- Metropolitan Museum, https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/igbo-ukwu-ca-9th-century : copper-alloy/bronze vessels and regalia, not simply iron smelters.
- Britannica, https://kids.britannica.com/scholars/article/smelting/68259 : extraction from ore with heat and reducing/oxidizing agents. Glossary, worksheet, keys and recap no longer say melting rock.

Other source-specific dates and quotations remain attributed to the textbook and are not claimed independently verified. Teacher cover notes name the corrected sources. Recap scripts and visible text synchronized. Local print and slide inspections recorded in the changes manifest. Existing recap fit repaired for the art lesson and checked in all ten scenes.
