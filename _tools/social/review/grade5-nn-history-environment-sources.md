# Grade 5 English-track history and environment fact checks

Verified 24 November 2026. Original textbook passages and media retained. Added visible qualifications, and synchronized authored definitions and printed supports.

- UNCCD, Article 1: desertification is degradation of land in dry regions, not a literal universal advance of deserts. https://www.unccd.int/article-1-use-terms
- Emirates Natural History Group, Tribulus 14(2), 2004: explosives fishing decree announced during the falconry conference at the end of 1976. The National retrospective dates it to 1977. Preserve the conflict visibly instead of presenting a falsely certain year; both describe explosives, not a ban on every form of fishing. https://enhg.org/Portals/1/trib/V14N2/TribulusV14N2Searchable.pdf and https://www.thenationalnews.com/opinion/comment/how-the-uae-got-to-be-a-nation-of-nature-lovers-1.1129712
- EAD, 21 December 2023: Arabian oryx recovery involved joint regional efforts. Do not erase Sheikh Zayed's role, but avoid sole-credit history. https://www.ead.gov.ae/en/Media-Centre/News/Arabian-Oryx-2023
- UNESCO, Çatalhöyük: earliest eastern mound Neolithic occupation about 7400 BCE. https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1405/
- Met Museum regional chronologies: Neolithic dates are regional, and farming predates Sumerian urban development. Fertile Crescent extends through the Levant and northern Mesopotamia toward southwestern Iran. https://www.metmuseum.org/toah/ht/02/wae and https://www.metmuseum.org/toah/ht/02/wam
- Smithsonian Human Origins, May 2023 event: dogs were the first domesticated animals; do not teach goats as the earliest of all domesticated animals. https://humanorigins.si.edu/about/events/hot-human-origins-today-topic-chasing-tales-humans-dogs-and-evolution-online
- Egyptian Museum Cairo: Narmer Palette discovered in 1897–1898; the interpretation of the unification scene is qualified. https://egyptianmuseumcairo.eg/artefacts/narmer-palette-collection/
- Met Museum, Adela Oppenheim, 24 November 2015: bread and beer were Egyptian dietary staples, fish were also eaten. https://www.metmuseum.org/perspectives/food-and-feasts-in-middle-kingdom-egypt
- Smithsonian National Museum of Asian Art: multiple Neolithic cultures in different Chinese river basins, millet or rice diets and region-dependent dates. https://asia.si.edu/education/educator-resources/teaching-china-with-the-smithsonian/explore-by-dynasty/neolithic-period/
- W.W. Norton, Worlds Together Worlds Apart, chapter 2: millet in the northern Yellow River basin and rice in the southern Yangzi basin, with the qualifications of regional farming rather than an exclusive no-exceptions rule. https://nerd.wwnorton.com/ebooks/epub/worldstogether6/EPUB/content/2.5-chapter02.xhtml
- British Museum collection description: Emperor Yang belongs to Sui, not Zhou. No unsupported identification of a textbook portrait added. https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/image/1613317078
- Harappa Archaeological Research Project: seals made impressions in wet clay on bundles. https://www.harappa.com/slide/seal-impression
- British Museum model boat description: lashed papyrus reeds, not papyrus paper sheets. https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/Y_EA21805

Research procedure: hypotheses must be tested and may be revised or rejected. This is an authored classroom instruction, not a changed quotation attributed to the textbook.
