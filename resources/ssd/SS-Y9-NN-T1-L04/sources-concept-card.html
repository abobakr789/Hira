Page 40–41 · Reading 1

Why is it important for organisations to protect their ideas? How is the UAE tackling climate change? Activity 1: Express your opinion on the moral challenges shown in the two pictures in the book, then answer the questions. (1) Are we entitled to revive animal species? (2) Are we entitled to change our genetic map so that future generations do not inherit diseases or disabilities?

Page 42–43 · Reading 2

Activity 2: Read about the international organisations working to protect ideas from piracy. The book describes two WIPO systems. It says an industrial design is the decorative or aesthetic appearance of a piece, and that the owner of a registered design is entitled to prevent others from making, selling or importing copies for commercial purposes. It says a brand, or trademark, is a mark that distinguishes the goods or services of one company from those of others, protected by intellectual property laws at national and regional level and, internationally, through a country’s trademark office or the WIPO Madrid system. A trademark can be a word, letters, numbers, symbols, shapes, sounds, smells or colours, and its registration can be renewed without a time limit for a fee.

Questions: (1) Explain why pirating ideas is a moral challenge, and the importance of intellectual protection in combating piracy. (2) Logos are of fundamental importance in developing a brand globally. Draw a new logo for your school to introduce it to students from Western countries. (3) Protect your intellectual product (your school’s logo) by researching the necessary steps in the UAE.

Page 43–44 · Reading 3

Activity 3: Learn about the measures the UAE is taking to face climate change and the global challenges that hamper sustainable development. Read the article about Masdar City. The book says Masdar has committed more than US$ 1.7 billion to renewable energy projects, which generate nearly one gigawatt of clean power inside and outside the UAE. Its projects in the UAE include the 100-megawatt Shams 1 solar power station in western Abu Dhabi, a 10-megawatt solar photovoltaic plant, rooftop solar installations, and carbon capture and sequestration projects. Internationally, Masdar has invested in Torresol Energy in Spain, which builds concentrated solar power plants, and in the London Array, a 650-megawatt offshore wind farm in the Thames Estuary.

Questions: (1) Define the term sustainable environment. Compare how the UAE, France and India work to accomplish the objective of sustaining the environment. (2) Design a poster to introduce classmates to different types of renewable resources. (3) Prepare a report on the most important environmental and health indicators used to study the global environment and health status. Use these indicators to study the environmental and health status of your neighbourhood.

Page 44–47 · Reading 4

Activity 4: Read the report about the contribution of morals to public health in the World Health Organisation bulletin. The book says health ethics goes back to Hippocrates, San Si Miao and Ibn Sina, and that modern bioethics expanded after World War II. The word bioethics was used by Fritz Jahr in 1927, so it is not accurate to say the field had no earlier roots. Public health ethics looks at measures to monitor and improve the health of whole populations, not only the doctor-patient relationship. The book lists main issues: differences in health between social groups and their access to care; addressing infectious disease, such as isolation and quarantine to stop the spread of tuberculosis and influenza; the exploitation of people in low-income countries in medical research; health awareness, since unhealthy behaviours raise the question of when authorities may step into personal health; and participation, transparency and accountability.

WHO’s Constitution entered into force on 7 April 1948. The textbook’s phrase “60 years ago” belongs to an older source, not the present year. Ethics is part of WHO’s public-health work; the discussion below examines ethical choices in research and health policy. Questions: (1) Take the role of an official at the WHO and discuss with classmates two ethical health issues: using individuals to conduct medical experiments, and the use of chemical weapons. (2) What ethical health issue is set to be the subject of research? Browse the website of the UAE’s Ministry of Health and make a presentation on the laws concerning health morals.

Page 48–50 · Reading 5

Activity 5: Read about the ethical problems in journalism around the world and how to reduce them. The book says a report from the Ethical Journalism Network looks at challenges in a “post-truth era”, where facts have been replaced by propaganda and misinformation. The report defines hate speech as statements calling for discrimination and violence, which differ from controversial statements that others may find offensive. Journalists must tell them apart. The book says media coverage of such statements may raise viewing figures but may also give them legitimacy they do not deserve.

The book says the guide advises journalists to have an honest and transparent intent with a source, to make sure a person at risk understands the consequences, to take care with anonymous sources, and to verify social media content: check that a picture or video has not been edited, and contact the original source to confirm accuracy and credibility.

Questions: (1) Write a report explaining how the UAE regulates journalism so that it remains a cornerstone of truth, not falsehood. (2) Show the importance of verifying the source of information before making judgements. (3) Look for an example of global propaganda and explain its consequences. Activity 6: With a classmate, choose one global ethical challenge. Make your classmates aware of its seriousness and suggest ways to overcome it.
