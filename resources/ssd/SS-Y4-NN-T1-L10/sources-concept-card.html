CONCEPT CARD

Addressing changes and losses

A change is when something becomes different. A loss is losing someone or something that matters to us. Adaptation means getting used to a new situation. We can cope by talking to someone we trust, crying when we need to, resting, eating healthy food and asking for help.

With care and support, we can get used to change and cope with loss.

Words to know

changesomething becomes differentfeelingshow we feel inside, like sad or worried
losslosing someone or something that matters to usrestsleep and time to relax
adaptationgetting used to a new situationhealthy foodfood that is good for our body
supporthelp and care from other peopleindividualone person, different from others

📖 Book text

Student Book Vol. 1. Activity 4 is adapted with the 2013 UNICEF comparison corrected; classroom discussion suggestions are not claims about all Dutch families.

Page 101 · Activity 1

Work in groups. Following the steps below, talk to each other about times when change and loss happened in your lives. Make sure that everyone in the group gets a chance to speak.

Steps: 1. Talk about a change or a loss that happened to you. 2. What did you think and how did you feel? 3. Did this feeling last for long and do you still feel that way now? 4. Did someone help you to deal with your change or loss? Who? How did they help you? 5. How do you behave when change or loss happens to you?

Page 102–103 · Activity 2

After you have talked about times when change and loss happened to you, read the story about Johnny. Then answer the questions that follow.

Johnny and his parents recently moved from America to Abu Dhabi. When they arrived in Abu Dhabi, Johnny’s parents began looking for a good school for him. But Johnny was very quiet and didn’t seem to be interested in finding a new school. He stopped speaking to anyone.

His father noticed that Johnny was unhappy and asked what was wrong.

Questions. • What do you think Johnny said when his father asked him what was wrong? • Johnny’s father will probably tell Johnny not to worry about starting a new school. What do you think Johnny will say? • How big a problem is it if people don’t want to accept change or get used to a new situation? • What could you say to Johnny to help him get used to his new life?

Page 104 · Activity 3

Work in groups. Look at this list of things we can do at times when change or loss happen to us. Do you agree with these ideas? Explain your answer, giving examples.

• Talk to a friend, someone in your family, or a doctor or nurse • Don’t be afraid to cry • Get a lot of sleep and rest • Eat healthy food • Ask for advice and help, especially when you are feeling unhappy or worried about something

Page 105–106 · Activity 4

Read the text about happy children in The Netherlands. Then, work in groups and write five ways we can ensure children are healthy and happy. Share your ideas with your classmates.

In 2013, a UNICEF report ranked the Netherlands first overall for child well-being among 29 wealthy countries, including the United Kingdom and the United States of America. The report looked at the following important areas of a child’s life: material well-being, health and safety, education, behaviours and risks, housing and environment. The Netherlands ranked in the top five in all these areas, but not first in every area.

The lesson encourages families to treat children as individuals. Children are not all the same, and their feelings and needs deserve care.

The lesson suggests regular meals, rest and support as ways to care for children. Think about how a calm family breakfast could help a child start the day.

Spending time together gives family members a chance to talk and listen to each other.

Families can enjoy simple activities together without buying expensive things. Choose an activity that would help a child feel included.

Page 107 · Activity 5

Read about and role-play each situation to show how you can help the people involved.

• Yusuf crashed his bicycle today. It was badly damaged and he won’t be able to ride it again. • Janna’s father has found work abroad. He will only be able to visit his family once every three months. • In the middle of the school year, the science teacher had to leave because he was sick and a new teacher has arrived to teach science.

Let’s talk

① What problem did Johnny have? ② Name two ways to cope with change or loss. ③ What does it mean to adapt?
