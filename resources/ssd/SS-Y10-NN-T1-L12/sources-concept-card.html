CONCEPT CARD

Helping others to overcome addiction

Stigma can stop people asking for help. Help includes counselling, cognitive based therapy, detox, rehabilitation and a support network. Families help with understanding. The UAE focuses on prevention, treatment and rehabilitation.

Recovery is possible with support.

Page 182–184 · Reading 1

Do you know of any professional organisations in the UAE which help people with addiction problems? What advice would you give to someone whose brother or sister is suffering from an addiction problem?

In many countries worldwide, there is a strong social stigma attached to those who suffer from addictions, which the World Health Organisation highlights as a “major cause of discrimination and exclusion.” This attitude is gradually changing as people are becoming more aware of the need to help addicts to overcome their cravings and addiction lifestyles. This has been supported by scientific research that has proven addiction to be a brain disease, rather than a lack of willpower.

The first step for those suffering from any addiction is to admit that there is a problem. This can be very challenging. It may be difficult for people to ask for help, as denial of an addiction problem is common. […]

People suffering from addictions do need personal and professional support to overcome their problems. A counsellor is a trained professional who focuses specifically on providing therapy to people by giving advice on their personal problems. […] Open communication and a feeling of trust and confidentiality are very important […].

Page 184–185 · Reading 2

Psychotherapy is a more specialised field than counselling. One of the most widely used techniques is cognitive based therapy (CBT). CBT is a type of therapy that can help people manage their problems by looking at ways they can change their thinking and behaviour. It is commonly used to treat anxiety and depression, but can be useful for other mental and physical health problems. […] This may involve writing a journal about their mood, what made them feel the way they do and how they responded to it, and practising relaxed breathing and relaxing muscles until the whole body feels relaxed.

Research Task (p. 184): use reputable websites to find three commonly used CBT tools. Make notes why each of the tools may be useful for an addict.

Thinking Task (p. 185): why is it important that dialogue between an addict and a counsellor or psychiatrist is confidential?

Page 185–187 · Reading 3

One way in which people can get treatment for their addiction is to enter rehabilitation. Rehabilitation is generally a supervised procedure overseen by medical practitioners and professionals in psychology. In the UAE, for example, the National Rehabilitation Center (NRC) was set up under the direction of Sheikh Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan (May Allah have mercy upon his soul) in 2002.

Before people enter a rehabilitation programme for an addiction to a substance or substances, they may need to go through a period called ‘detox’. This is the period where the addictive substance or substances is/are given the chance to exit from the body. The body and mind suffer withdrawal symptoms as the substances in the body decline. […]

Once the detox phase is complete, rehabilitation can begin. This involves the patient entering a programme to remove their dependency on the substance that they are addicted to. The main goal of any rehabilitation program is to change the patient’s attitudes towards a specific substance. […] Rehabilitation and counselling can happen at the same time […]. Once addicts are aware of the triggers, the focus can move to developing the skills to deal with these triggers when they appear. This involves the development of a set of personal coping skills […].

Page 186–188 · Reading 4

There are several organisations to support specific addictive behaviours. For example, The Cabin Dubai, provides treatment programmes for people with gambling issues, internet usage addictions, shopping addictions and compulsive eating. These outpatient programmes are confidential, and allow patients to stay at home in order to maintain their regular routines for work and family, while attending treatment at the centre.

“Work therapy is the latest means to overcoming mental illnesses and problems that plague humanity nowadays.” Sheikh Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan (May Allah have mercy upon his soul)

Family members take a central role in the rehabilitation process. Rehabilitation involves mending broken relationships. […] Family members can also learn about the coping skills, the different addiction triggers, and how to show support and understanding, rather than anger and judgement, which can help the addict to recover. […] family skills training programmes have been found to be more effective than only providing families with information about substance abuse.

Page 188–191 · Reading 5

After rehabilitation, the process is far from over. The patient is likely to pay regular visits to the clinic, a doctor, a counsellor and/or a support group to build a network of support and people they can turn to. It is important to note that recovery from addiction is a lifelong process […].

The UAE has acknowledged the issues of drug addiction and is actively encouraging drug awareness programmes. […] The focus is on prevention, treatment, and rehabilitation, giving addicts the opportunity to re-enter society. To encourage more addicts to seek the help they need, the UAE changed the law. […] The law will not prosecute those who ask for help for drug addiction. Parents, brothers and sisters of the addict can also come forward to seek help for them as well, without fear of legal consequences.

The National Rehabilitation Centre (NRC) in Abu Dhabi promotes treatment and rehabilitation as the tools to support addicts in their recovery. […] The NRC is seen as a model for tackling substance abuse and treatment throughout the UAE. It recognises that addiction can occur at any level of society.

The NRC provides both inpatient and outpatient programmes. […] The process involves a week of detox, followed by a rehabilitation programme of approximately 10 weeks. The patient then has outpatient treatment for up to two years. […] Support and guidance for families is provided […].

Page 191–194 · Reading 6

It can be very difficult to know what to say or do if you know someone […] who you think is suffering from an addiction. […] Try to arrange a time to meet with the person in a quiet place where there are no distractions. Speak to the person about something they care about […]. Be clear that their addiction is a problem. Letting the person know that you are there to listen and support them is very important, as they may feel isolated and overwhelmed. […] but rehabilitation is confidential. Encourage the person to take the first step of going to see a doctor or the NRC. […]

Remember to listen, encourage, share, inform and support.

“One who is strong yet doesn’t help his weaker brother will regret it later […].” Sheikh Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan (May Allah have mercy upon his soul)
