CONCEPT CARD

The First Hundred Years’ War

The book says the First Hundred Years’ War lasted from 1159 to 1259 CE, mainly between England and France. The Plantagenets ruled most of England and the Capets ruled France. In 1215 King John signed the Magna Carta, a document that protected the rights of wealthy landowners. The Treaty of Paris ended the war on December 4, 1259.

Activity 1: A war in Europe lasted one hundred years. What are some reasons you think wars happen? Write your ideas in the causes of war web.

Page 141 · Reading 2

The 12th century CE was another period of instability and conflict in Europe, particularly between England and France. This period occurred from 1159 CE to 1259 CE and was known as the First Hundred Years’ War. At the time, there were two major kingdoms in England and France. The Plantagenet family ruled most of England and owned a large amount of land in France. […] The French kings of the Capet family, Louis VII and, later, Philip Augustus, wanted to regain control of their own lands. They began fighting the English kings. […] When Henry died in 1189 CE, Richard became the new King of England.

Page 142 · Reading 3

John became King of England in 1199 CE after Richard died from injuries in battle. […] In 1215 the king signed the Magna Carta, a document that protected the rights of wealthy landowners. King John did not follow the rules of the Magna Carta and so the landowners fought with the king. […] The First Hundred Years’ War lasted until 1259, exactly one hundred years after it began. It ended with the signing of the Treaty of Paris on December 4, 1259. The treaty was signed by King John’s son, Henry III, and the King of France, Louis IX. By signing the treaty, Henry III agreed to give up many of his lands in France, and officially ended the First Hundred Years’ War.

Indicate who benefitted from each event and briefly explain your answer.

Activity 7: The picture shows an original signed copy of the Magna Carta, kept in the British Library. Why do you think it’s important to save documents like this for hundreds of years? What can we learn from the past? And what kinds of objects should we save?
