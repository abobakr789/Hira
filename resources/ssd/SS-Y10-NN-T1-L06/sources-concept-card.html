CONCEPT CARD

Socially responsible investing

Investment means doing something today for a return in the future: education, health, savings, business and government projects. A share is part ownership; a bond is an IOU. Socially responsible investing takes ethical factors into account, such as corruption, tobacco, harm to the environment and poor working practices.

Investment is about the future; responsible investment adds ethics.

Page 92–94 · Reading 1

Do you think about your future? What can you do now that will help you in the future?

The term ‘investment’ generally means doing something today in the hope of a future benefit; financial returns are not guaranteed. We use the term in different ways; for example, by going to school, you are investing in your future. The hope is that by getting an education, you will be in a better position to get a job in the future.

The UAE Centennial 2071 plan is an investment in the UAE. His Highness Sheikh Mohammed bin Rashid Al Maktoum (May Allah protect him), Vice President and Prime Minister of the UAE and Ruler of Dubai, said of the plans: “[…] We must plant the seeds today for future generations to grow and prosper tomorrow. […]”

“The real asset of any advanced nation is its people, especially the educated ones, and the prosperity and success of the people are measured by the standard of their education.” Sheikh Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan (May Allah have mercy upon his soul)

Page 95–97 · Reading 2

We can also ‘invest in a healthy lifestyle’. In this context, what we eat today, how we exercise and live our lives can have a bearing on how we will be able to live our lives in the future. […]

In many parts of the world, people will refer to investment in terms of setting aside some money now through saving. […] The expectation is that the money saved will grow over time and be used for funding retirement, children’s education or some other planned spending in the future. […] If an individual saved 50 000 AED for a year at an interest rate of 5%, they would have 52 500 AED at the end of the year.

In Islamic finance, interest (riba) is not allowed. […] Saving in an Islamic bank can generate a return through the bank using the money to invest in beneficial projects and businesses which it is hoped will generate a profit. The profits are then shared with savers, giving them a return on their investment. The base principle of Islamic banking is one of shared risk and sharing of profits and losses.

Page 98–100 · Reading 3

In business, investment means the purchase of capital items which are not bought for their own sake but for the contribution they will make to production. For example, if a business buys a computer system which helps process payments from customers, this is classified as capital investment. If the Abu Dhabi National Oil Company decides to set up a new refinery, it is investing in the future productive capacity of the oil industry in the UAE.

Many governments oversee large investment projects in a range of services. Hospitals and schools, for example, are major investments for many governments. […] As part of the UAE Vision 2021 National Agenda, the government is investing in the reform of education in schools and universities. […] governments will invest in infrastructure projects […] roads, bridges, ports, telecommunications systems, transit systems and so on.

“No matter how many buildings, foundations, schools and hospitals we build, or how many bridges we raise, all these are material entities. The real spirit behind the progress is the human spirit, the able man with his intellect and capabilities.” Sheikh Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan (May Allah have mercy upon his soul)

Page 100–103 · Reading 4

For businesses, this money might come from the revenue they receive from selling their goods and services or from financial institutions in the form of loans. […] At the heart of the financial system is the capital market. The capital market is the general term used to describe the markets where people who wish to save money can be put in touch with people who wish to access money.

Stock exchanges exist to enable the trade in stocks and shares of businesses. A share or stock is a part ownership in a business and a claim on the future profits the business may make.

A bond is a means of raising funds for businesses and governments. A bond is essentially an IOU. For example, the real estate company Manazel might issue bonds each valued at 10 000 AED. This 10 000 AED is called the ‘principal’. […] In return for lending the 10 000 AED to the business, Manazel will pay the bond holder an interest rate called the ‘coupon’. The coupon for our fictional bond example here might be 3.5%. This means that the bond holder will receive 3.5% of 10 000 AED (350 AED) each year for 5 years in interest. At maturity the issuer promises to repay the principal, but default can cause losses. Bonds are not risk-free.

Page 103–106 · Reading 5

Investment choices have financial, social and environmental risks. The textbook’s company examples and percentage returns are historical or hypothetical classroom prompts, not current offers or recommendations. A high promised return can come with high risk. Verify any real investment independently; returns and capital can be lost.

Action Task: Which of the options would you choose and why? […] Discussion Point: Is investing in companies that manufacture tobacco products ethical?

According to the 2017 Transparency International Index, countries at the bottom end of the index have police and justice systems that do not work properly. […] The World Health Organisation (WHO) report that around 50% of tobacco users will die of tobacco related illnesses. […] Agro-chemical companies create different types of herbicides, pesticides and fungicides, which, critics argue, can be harmful to human health and damage ecosystems and biological diversity.

Page 106–109 · Reading 6

Socially responsible investing involves assessing investments to take into consideration ethical factors. A socially responsible investment will not give funds to governments that have poor human rights records, are classified as corrupt, or who are known to be linked to terrorism. Similarly, a socially responsible investment will not give funds to businesses associated with certain products or services. These include alcohol, tobacco, gambling, or goods which have a negative impact on the environment. It will also avoid giving funds to businesses which support poor working practices.

In addition, socially responsible investing might deliberately target investment funds at governments and businesses where there is a clear social, environmental or economic benefit in addition to providing a financial return on the investment.

The Emirates Investment Authority (EIA) was set up in 2007. Its role is to: “[…] strategically invest funds allocated by the Federal Government to create long-term value for the UAE and contribute to the future prosperity of the country.” […]
