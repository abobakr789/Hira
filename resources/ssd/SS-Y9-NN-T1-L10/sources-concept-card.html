📖 Book text

Student Book Vol. 1 · Original text from the Student Book.

Page 107–108 · Reading 1

What characteristics do you need to become an entrepreneur? What things do you need to create a successful start-up business? Activity 1: Add characteristics to the mind map in the book. As you know, entrepreneurs have a number of characteristics that make them successful. Suppose you were going to build your own start-up. What characteristics do you think you would need?

Page 109–110 · Reading 2

Activity 2: Read the article about Jack Ma. The book says Ma Yun, known as Jack Ma, is a Chinese businessman and technology entrepreneur, mostly known for his repeated failures before he achieved success. After graduating with a master’s degree in business administration, he could not find a job, so he became an English teacher at a local university, which led him to start a translation service. In 1995 he discovered that the world was using the internet but it had not yet reached China, and he saw an emerging business opportunity. He launched a website listing Chinese products and companies, called Chinapage.

The book says he partnered with the Chinese government, but the partners limited his ideas, so he sold his share and created an online retail shop called Alibaba, which received a $1 billion investment from Yahoo. It says he launched the company by convincing friends and family to invest, and that today Alibaba is worth over $200 billion and poses a threat to big bricks-and-mortar retailers because people can buy online.

Questions: (a) What lessons can we learn from the text? (b) What characteristics do Jack Ma and Elon Musk share? How are they different?

Page 110–112 · Reading 3

Activity 3: Read the article about business models. The book says building a start-up is very challenging and needs a blend of creativity, leadership, vision, determination and financial and social responsibility. Start-ups start small and have the potential for scalability. To create a successful start-up you need three cornerstones in place: an innovative business strategy; a coherent operational strategy to implement it; and an information technology (IT) strategy to support both. The book calls this a business triangle that must be in alignment.

When you decide on a business idea, ask: what business are you in, what is your source of revenue, and which operations drive it? You must also consider costs: set-up costs, also called capital costs, and the costs of running the business, called operating costs, such as wages and utilities. The book gives XYZ Co., a fictional company, as an example, in which trend data guides what is manufactured, distributed and sold, and the IT system records sales so the store manager can reorder.

Task: With your group, come up with an idea for a start-up, using the three cornerstones.

Page 113–114 · Reading 4

Activity 4: Read the article about Zaha Hadid. The book says she was a British-Iraqi architect and creative entrepreneur who died in 2016 at the age of 66 and is remembered for her unprecedented creative designs. She founded Zaha Hadid Architects and was an ambassador for the Creative Entrepreneurs movement. The book says a creative entrepreneur is a person who sets up a business in a creative field such as architecture, photography or cinematography.

The book says she is considered a leader of innovation and a role model in Britain and the Arab world, and that in 2015 she became the first woman to win the Royal Gold Medal. It says she studied mathematics at the American University of Beirut, then at the Architectural Association School in London, and that in 2010 she completed the Sheikh Zayed Bridge in Abu Dhabi. Her winning designs include the Guangzhou Opera House, a museum of 21st-century art in Rome and the London Olympics Aquatic Centre. The book adds that she is famous for her leadership in founding her own company and competing in an occupation previously dominated by men.

Question: What are the similarities and differences between Zaha Hadid and Jack Ma and Elon Musk?

Page 115–116 · Reading 5

Activity 5: Go back to your start-up groups. The book says a business plan is a detailed outline for your business, a blueprint for turning your idea into reality. It describes what your product or service is, what market need it meets, who the target market is, how you will finance the business, and how you expect it to become profitable. Prepare a detailed business plan outlining how you would bring your product or service to market. The book’s table lists: product or service, market need it addresses, target consumers, financing, growth schedule, expected expenses and expected income.

Present your business plan to the class and express your thoughts on the question: what are the important moral values that you should keep in mind when doing business?

Activity 6: Now that you have imagined all the effort that goes into building a start-up, think of the lessons you have learned. Do you think you have the personality needed to set up a business? What financial skills do you need to learn? Would you be able to cope with the pressures and challenges? Write a ten-line paragraph about the lessons you have learned from the start-up exercise.
