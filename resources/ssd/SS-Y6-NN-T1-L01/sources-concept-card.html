CONCEPT CARD

Aspects of equality among people

Equality means people have the same rights and respect. In the story, the cats’ dispute left them with nothing. In his Message of Tolerance, Sheikh Mohammed says Sheikh Rashid’s and Sheikh Zayed’s majlis welcomed everyone regardless of origin, tribe, sect or religion. The Declaration of Human Rights says all people have the same rights and the right to education.

Tolerance helps a community stay fair.

Page 6–9 · Reading 1

Unit questions: How do we commit to distributive justice in our daily lives and on the community level? How do we achieve equality among people? What is the relationship between equality, fairness and justice? Are resources and funds distributed fairly? As a community, how can we achieve distributive justice through what we have learnt?

Lesson questions: What does it mean to have equality among people? How is equality created in a community? How is equality created in a country?

Activity 1: With your classmates, brainstorm the concept of equality in the community. Then, on a sticky note, write two things you know about equality and place it in the first box of the Knowledge Table. On a second sticky note, write two things you’d like to learn in this lesson. After you complete the lesson, on a third sticky note, write two things you have learned.

Page 10 · Reading 2

Activity 2: What is the best way to solve a dispute? Read the story of ‘The Monkey and the Scale’. Once upon a time, there were two cats named Fulla and Yasmina. […] Fulla saw a sardine in front of them. Yasmina picked it up in her mouth and the two friends hurried back home. Once they were home, Yasmina said, ‘I will eat the whole sardine myself, as I was the one who carried it back home and I’m very hungry’. ‘I should eat the sardine because I found it,’ objected Fulla, ‘and I am hungry, too!’ Soon they were screeching and hissing and snarling at each other […]. Unable to find a solution, the cats decided to tell their story to the monkey. […] he said, ‘You should go and see the judge. He holds court under the giant fig tree.’ Then, the monkey hurried to the tree. He quickly put on a judge’s robe and sat behind the table waiting for the cats to arrive.

Page 11–12 · Reading 3

[…] From a box under the table, the monkey brought out a small scale. Holding the sardine, he cut it into two uneven pieces. Then, he placed each piece of fish on each side of the scale. […] The monkey shook his head and said, ‘This is unfair! The two pieces should be exactly even.’ So, he took a bite of the largest piece […]. The judge continued to eat from each piece while shaking his head and exclaiming that the two pieces should be exactly even. Soon, only one small piece of fish was left. The monkey took the last piece […]. He then put the fish in his mouth and said, ‘This is the judge’s fee!’ The cats stood in silence. They realised that their disagreement had left them both with no fish and empty stomachs!

Questions: (a) In your group, discuss what happened between the monkey and the cats. Write down your ideas. (b) How could the cats have solved their dispute? Rewrite some of the story so that the cats come to an agreement.

Page 14 · Reading 4

Activity 3: Tolerance is the key to a diverse and fair community. Read the Message of Tolerance by His Highness Sheikh Mohammed bin Rashid Al Maktoum. […] I vividly remember my late father Sheikh Rashid’s majlis, over four decades ago. Citizens, young and old, from the cities and Bedouins from the desert used to attend the majlis. The people that came were from various tribes and sects, and people of different backgrounds and races. But, to Rashid, they were all citizens. They were all given the same respect, appreciation, rights and considerations. […] The residents and visitors who attended Rashid’s majlis were treated exactly the same as citizens. They were from different religions, nations and cultures, and all were welcome in Rashid’s majlis and were welcome in Dubai.

Page 15–16 · Reading 5

After our union, I came to know Sheikh Zayed (may God have mercy upon him) more closely. […] Sheikh Zayed welcomed everyone into his majlis, regardless of their origin, tribe, sect, denomination or religion. Sheikh Zayed was generous with everyone; he taught everyone and loved everyone. […] This is the legacy of Sheikh Zayed and Sheikh Rashid. Sheikh Zayed’s greatest legacies were his values, spirit and morals.

Questions: (a) What does Sheikh Mohammed say is the value that governs the relationship between the nation and its people? How is it important for the UAE community? (b) How can we contribute to spreading the values that are mentioned in this message?

Page 16–17 · Reading 6

Activity 4: Read the text adapted from the Universal Declaration of Human Rights. Write down a country where this is true. (a) Article 2: All people have the same rights and freedoms, regardless of their race, gender, language, or religion. (b) Article 23: Everyone has the right to equal pay for equal work. (c) Article 26: Everyone has the right to education.

① Why did the cats end up with no fish? ② Who did Sheikh Rashid’s majlis welcome? ③ What does Article 26 say?
