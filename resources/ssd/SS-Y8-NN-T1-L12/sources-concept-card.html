Page 126–127 · Reading 1

What ancient African civilizations can you name? What contributions have ancient African civilizations made to human history? Warm up: Do you know the names of any ancient African civilizations? List as many as you can. The book’s key words include ethnic group, obelisks, delegation, Harar Jugol, Ajami, Abyssinia, Ancient Aksum, Great Zimbabwe, Ancient Ghana and predecessor.

Page 128–130 · Reading 2

Modern day Ethiopia is located in the Horn of Africa and is populated by many ethnic groups. In ancient times, Ethiopia was called Abyssinia and its most prominent kingdom was called Aksum. The book says the kingdom of Aksum was established in 400 BCE. As a powerful empire, Aksum served as a major hub between African, Arab, European and Indian trade communities. The people of Aksum had their own written language called Ge’ez and printed their own coins. The remains of Aksum are still visible in northern Ethiopia, and archaeologists have discovered large structures called obelisks, erected by Negus Ezana in ancient times.

Another powerful kingdom from ancient Abyssinia is Harar Jugol. The book says the city was founded as early as the 10th century as the capital of the Harari Kingdom, an important trading society that connected merchants from the Ethiopian coast to those in the highlands. It had five walled gates that served as protective barriers and divided the city into five neighbourhoods. Activity 1: List five facts from the text about Ancient Aksum and Ancient Harar Jugol. Explain how they were similar and different.

Page 130–132 · Reading 3

The Kingdom of Zimbabwe: located in Southern Africa, it was established about 1,000 CE by the Kalanga people of present-day Zimbabwe, Botswana and South Africa. The name comes from the stone architecture present throughout the kingdom, including stone houses and massive walls that surrounded the kingdom as a means of artistic design and protection. The kingdom lasted till about 1500 CE. The book says present-day Zimbabwe took its name from the original kingdom after independence from the British in 1980. The kingdom became an important regional power through trade. One of its major trading partnerships was with the Kingdom of Mapungubwe, in the present-day Limpopo area of South Africa. Through this relationship Great Zimbabwe controlled the ivory and gold trade that crossed from the interior to the south-eastern coasts, and it established its own gold, copper and iron mines.

Activity 2: Answer in complete sentences. Why was ancient Zimbabwe referred to as “Great Zimbabwe”? Give two examples. What was another kingdom that traded with Great Zimbabwe? Which products were traded?

Page 133–136 · Reading 4

The Kingdom of Ancient Ghana, or Wagadou, was a large and powerful West African kingdom located in present-day Mauritania and Mali. Its influence spread across the West African coast and across the Sahara Desert. Like Great Zimbabwe, present-day Ghana took its name from its ancient predecessor. The book says Ancient Ghana appears in written records of travellers in 830 CE and is mentioned again in an 11th-century book called The Book of Routes and Kingdoms. It was known as the Land of Gold. Merchants traded gold, copper, iron, ivory, salts, cola nuts and other resources across the Sahara and into Arabian, Asian, European and Indian markets. The book says a common rule was that all gold nuggets belonged to the king, while miners and other citizens could freely collect gold dust.

Early travellers described Ancient Ghana as a complex civilization with a heavy Islamic and traditional West African culture. The book says Islam was introduced to Ghana through trans-Sahara trade as West African and Arab merchants traded resources and culture, and that scholars began to write oral stories using Arabic script, which is called Ajami. Activity 3: (a) Which present-day countries made up the ancient Kingdom of Ghana? (b) What was a major product of Ghana, remembered in history? (c) What is Ajami? How was it important in recording the history of the Ancient Ghanaian people?

Page 137–138 · Reading 5

Activity 4: Answer the questions. Which ancient African kingdom was located in Western Africa? Which ancient African kingdom was located in Eastern Africa? (The book repeats the second question; the kingdoms of Eastern Africa in this lesson are Aksum and Harar Jugol.)

Activity 5: Each of the ancient African kingdoms has a very unique cultural history. After learning about these places, explain some of the similarities that they shared. Explain how they differed. Write your answers and discuss with a partner.
