CONCEPT CARD

Challenges to health and getting help

Health has challenges such as a sedentary lifestyle and fizzy drinks. The textbook describes some UAE colleges serving healthy meals and banning soft drinks. Obstacles like access to knowledge and to health care services lead to consequences. We give kind advice and ask for help.

Know the challenge, ask for help.

Words to know

challengesomething difficult that needs effortfizzy drinka soft drink with bubbles
consequencewhat happens as a result of somethingknowledgewhat a person knows
diseasean illness that harms the bodyhealth care servicesplaces and people that look after health
sedentarysitting down for most of the dayadvicea helpful suggestion

📖 Book text

Student Book Vol. 1 · Original text from the Student Book.

Page 76–77 · Reading 1

How do people stay healthy? Why should individuals care about the health of others? What is a health problem that is common in your community?

Activity 1: What do you think is meant by this quote? “If we could give every individual the right amount of nourishment and exercise, not too little and not too much, we would have found the safest way to health.” Hippocrates.

Page 77 · Reading 2

Activity 2: Read about colleges in the UAE. Some colleges in the UAE are leading by example when it comes to facing the challenge of global obesity and promoting healthy living. Students are being taught about health and wellbeing right from the basics of food intake and energy levels. Healthy living is reinforced by the college. The cafeteria serves only healthy meals and soft drinks have been banned on campus.

Page 78 · Reading 3

A sedentary lifestyle, in which young people spend most of the day sitting either at school or at home, has been blamed for the rise of obesity. As one college representative says, “It’s important that we educate this generation and the ones that come after.” […] Another cause of increased weight among young people is fizzy drinks. A recent study found that many young people consume more than one 330ml can a day. Results showed more tooth decay among soda drinkers. […] The government of the UAE is working closely with other organisations to address the problem and efforts are ongoing to ensure the health of the population.

Page 78–79 · Reading 4

Questions: (a) Why do you think a ban on soft drinks was introduced? (b) What reasons can you give for the sedentary lifestyle mentioned in the article? (c) In your group, discuss the responsibilities of the individual, family, and society in ensuring that we live full and healthy lives. (d) Obesity has been referred to as “a global health problem.” What do you think this statement means?

Activity 3: Consider the challenges and the consequences people encounter in their lives while trying to maintain their health. The table has two columns, Obstacles and Consequences, and rows such as Access to knowledge and Access to health care services.

Page 80–81 · Reading 5

Activity 4: In pairs, role play the scenarios below. One person is a student with a problem, the other is an adult giving advice. Nadia is 11 years old. She finds it difficult to sleep at night. […] Fahad is 11 years old. He is overweight and is often teased in school because he can’t run easily […]. Sophie is 12. She feels that she eats quite healthy and gets enough sleep, but when she has to walk up stairs she finds herself getting out of breath very easily. […] Adnan is 12 years old. […] He hasn’t got many friends and feels sad and lonely a lot of the time. […] Safety: unusual breathlessness needs a trusted adult and medical advice, not just more exercise. Severe breathing difficulty needs emergency help.

Page 82 · Reading 6

Activity 5: Visit one of the following facilities in your neighbourhood: a sports club, a gym, a swimming pool, a health club. Write a report on how this facility contributes to the health of the people who go there regularly.

Let’s talk

① What was banned on campus in the article? ② What lifestyle has been blamed for the rise of obesity? ③ What is the aim of the role plays?
