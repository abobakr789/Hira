Page 150–151 · Reading 1

What were the ancient kingdoms of Mali, Islamic cities and civilizations in West Africa like? What contributions have the ancient African kingdoms made to human history? The book’s key words are Mali kingdoms, Mansa Musa, Timbuktu, Djenné and the gold trade. Warm up: You learned about the Sahel region in Africa in an earlier lesson. What was it located next to? What was it like? Write a description of the landscape.

Page 152–153 · Reading 2

The Spread of Islam in Africa, 715–1500 CE (the title of the book’s map). The book says Islam spread into Africa through several separate phases. From 685 CE to 715 CE, it spread to Egypt and across North Africa. Islam spread across the Sahara to the Sahel, and through the West African states and East Africa through merchant contacts and long-distance caravan routes to Arab Muslim cities of northern Africa. By the 11th century, Islam reached the Senegalese coastal region and vast areas of the Sahel across to Sudan. By the late 13th and 14th centuries, well-established cities in the Sahel and Mali had large Muslim institutions. Mosques at Timbuktu and other cities in Mali reflect this development. Cities in the Sahel prospered from trade in gold, salt and other metal wares produced in the region.

Activity 1: List the progression of Islam across Africa as stated in the text on the timeline. Write the area that corresponds to the time period that Islam reached the area: the 7th and 8th centuries, the 11th century, and the 13th and 14th centuries.

Page 154–155 · Reading 3

Timbuktu: the book says Timbuktu was an important city by the 15th and 16th centuries and the cultural centre of Mali and the Sahel region. It had three great mosques, Djingarey Ber, Sankore and Sidi Yahia, and Sankore University. Timbuktu became wealthy from the gold trade of the 14th century. The Great Mosque, also known as Djingarey Ber, was originally built in 1327, in a building style typical of the people of Sudan and the Sahel of that time. The book says the style began with the King of Mali, Mansa Musa, who went on the Hajj in Mecca and brought back a famous architect to design the mosque.

The book says Mansa Musa was the great nephew of Mari Jata (Sunjata), the legendary king who established the kingdom of Mali, and was famous for his long caravan expedition across the Sahara to Cairo with great amounts of gold. In Cairo, he spent so much gold that the value of gold fell throughout Egypt. The library of Arabic manuscripts in Timbuktu shows how its cultural heritage has been connected to the Arab Muslim world, with evidence of visits of Muslim scholars crossing the Sahara since at least the 14th century. One of the famous visitors was the traveller Ibn Battuta, who wrote about his visit.

Page 156–157 · Reading 4

Djenné: the book says Djenné has been occupied since 250 BCE and became a market centre along the trans-Saharan gold trade routes of the 13th to 14th century. Its traditional mud houses and large mosques make use of the local material of mud from the nearby river. Each year these traditional mosques are refreshed. With a community effort, teams of builders bring fresh mud plaster to reinforce the walls of the structure.

Activity 2: Answer the questions. (1) Mali is a kingdom located in which region of Africa? Circle your choice. (2) Where do the Blue Nile and White Nile meet? Circle your choice. (3) Mansa Musa was related to which of the following famous Africans: Ibn Battuta, the famous North African traveller; Ibn Khaldun, the famous North African historian; or Sunjata (also spelled Sundiata), the king who founded Mali?

Page 158–161 · Reading 5

Activity 3: Fill in the gaps of the sentences with the key words in the box: mosques, library, education, Niger River Delta, Djenné, Timbuktu, Mansa Musa and Songhay. The sentences say that one of the most famous rulers of the Malian and Songhay Empire was Mansa Musa, who led a caravan across the Sahara to Cairo and to Mecca for the pilgrimage, or Hajj; that the city of Timbuktu in Mali has a great library of manuscripts where Arabic books and Qur’ans are preserved for scholars; that education is a major achievement of Timbuktu; and that the city of Djenné, located in the Niger River Delta, features many mosques made of traditional mud architecture.

Activity 5: Describe five geographical features that Mansa Musa would have crossed during his journey.
