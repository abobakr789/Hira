CONCEPT CARD

Discrimination

Discrimination is treating a person badly because they are different. Idris was a new student that some people did not accept. Rajeef was left out of the game. We stop discrimination by treating everyone equally and speaking up.

Everyone deserves a welcome.

Words to know

fairtreating people in the right and equal waydiscriminationtreating a person badly because they are different
justicetreating people in a fair and right waynew studenta child who has just joined the school
unfairnot right; not equalwheelchaira chair with wheels for people who cannot walk easily
equalityeveryone is treated the samewelcometo greet someone in a kind way

📖 Book text

Student Book Vol. 1 · Original text from the Student Book.

Page 18–19 · Reading 1

Have you ever been treated differently because of the way you look? What would you do if people didn’t want to play with your friend or talk to him/her because he/she looks different? How can we make people who are different from us feel welcome or comfortable?

Activity 1: Act out this scene with a group. Dialogue: Idris is a new student. He joined the class one month after the school year started. Idris changed schools because his father changed jobs. His father moved from another country to the UAE. When Idris first arrived at the school, some people didn’t talk to him. Others just walked away from him. One group didn’t accept him. How would you act in this situation?

Page 20 · Reading 2

The referee thought about the situation for a moment. Then he walked over to Rajeef, put his hand on his shoulder and said to everyone, ‘May I have your attention? As the referee, I have decided that today’s game will be a “friendly” between the two teams. And to be fair, Rajeef will play on an equal basis. If you don’t agree with this decision, the game will be cancelled, and no one will play. Come on, what do you think? We don’t want to waste any more time.’

The referee blew his whistle, but, sadly, everyone went away. They chose to cancel the game rather than play with Rajeef. I stood there shocked. I couldn’t believe what I was seeing. Then, I turned to Rajeef and noticed that his face had changed. I was ashamed. We had to do something. We had to solve the discrimination. But how?

Page 22 · Reading 3

Questions: (1) The referee decided to let Rajeef participate in the game. What does this mean? (2) What expression do you think Rajeef had on his face when the other boys went away? (3) Was the decision to cancel the match a fair decision? (4) What issue do you think the writer is raising?

Let’s think about the question from the story, ‘How do we solve this?’ Suggest a solution.

Page 23–24 · Reading 4

Activity 3: Imagine a situation where a group of yellow birds didn’t want to accept a blue bird because of its different colour.

(a) Work with your classmates to write a dialogue for this scene. Write the dialogue between the blue bird and the yellow birds. Use the vocabulary and concepts you have learned in this lesson.

(b) Write a conclusion that shows equal treatment.

(c) Take roles and act out the dialogue in front of the class.

Page 25 · Reading 5

Activity 4: Tell a classmate about an unfair situation you were a part of or saw.

Recall a time when someone treated you unfairly even though you were being fair to that person. Then tell the story to a classmate and recall how it made you feel.

Let’s talk

① What is discrimination? ② What did the other boys do at the end of Part 2? ③ What did the referee decide?
