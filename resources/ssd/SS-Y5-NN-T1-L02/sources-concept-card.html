CONCEPT CARD

Moral dilemma

Moral standards are rules about what is right and wrong. A moral dilemma is a hard situation where it is difficult to decide what is right. Sometimes the law and our morals are not the same. Fresh water shortage can cause moral dilemmas between countries.

Good morals help us choose what is right, even when it is hard.

Page 15–16 · Activity 1

Key Vocabulary: kindness, sympathy, generosity, empathy, moral standards.

Work in groups. Discuss this quote from Sheikh Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan (May God have mercy upon him) on the importance of being raised with good morals.

“Without morals, good behaviour and knowledge, nations cannot bring up their generations nor perform their duties. Civilizations were created by knowledge, good manners, magnanimity, of understanding the past, belief in the present and anticipation of the future.”

Why do you think Sheikh Zayed placed such emphasis on the importance of moral behaviour?

Page 16 · Activity 2

Should the law always be the same as our moral values? Can you think of a situation when the law is more important than our morals?

Read the story In the Name of the Law. Then do the activities that follow.

It was the eve of the feast and the people of the neighbourhood were celebrating. At the same time, four men sneaked into an old lady’s small, modest room and stole the few possessions she had. When the lady discovered the theft, she felt completely helpless. She sat down and began to cry. On account of the theft, the lady could not pay her rent for five months. The landlord took her to court — her remaining possessions were taken from her and she was evicted from the room.

Passersby watched and cheered as repossession agents came for her property, while she sat there helpless, quietly crying, not knowing what to do.

Page 17 · Activity 2

The men worked together to take the biggest items of furniture from the room. They took the bed where she slept, the table where she ate her food, and the chair where she often sat and looked out her window. The old woman breathlessly ran up to them. She was very sad to see her possessions being taken away.

With much sorrow, she gently touched each item. She felt a mixture of emotions — feeling thankful and sorry at the same time. But these things no longer belong to her. How will she replace them?

Adapted from ‘Darkness and Rays’ by Mai Zyada.

1. Work in pairs. Discuss the following: The owner’s situation. The legal situation. The old woman’s situation.

2. What is another way to solve this situation? Is it possible to still follow the law and also have a happy result for everyone?

Page 18–19 · Activity 3

Work in pairs. Are the situations below moral or immoral? How do you decide whether something is moral or immoral?

1. During class tests, Sami does not let Jane copy his answers. As a result she gets bad marks. This causes bad feeling between them.

2. Faten gives her classmate Samantha sweets so that Samantha does her homework for her.

3. Electronics salesman, Abu Majid, sells goods for reasonable prices that are affordable for people on low incomes.

4. Sara’s younger sister often takes Sara’s things. This makes Sara angry and she hits her younger sister.

5. Michael knows who broke the classroom glass, but he keeps it a secret to save his classmate from punishment.

Page 20–21 · Activity 4

Think about a moral dilemma that can be caused by not having enough fresh water. Read the text about how different countries deal with water resources.

All countries suffer from climate change, but some countries suffer from water shortage, or drought, as well. Although countries such as the United Kingdom have high average rainfall, other countries are affected by a lack of fresh water reserves. This can cause problems between neighbouring countries that share fresh water resources, such as rivers and lakes. In some places, shortage of water has resulted in protests, and even violence. Countries that do not prepare for a water shortage may not be able to help their citizens. This will be a big problem in rural areas where the lack of water will be disastrous for agriculture.

Some countries, such as the UAE, have invested in sea water desalination to help secure their water needs. During the 5th Global Water Summit, held in 2012 in Abu Dhabi, HH Sheikh Mohammed bin Zayed al Nahyan, may God save him, stated, ‘For us (the UAE), water is now more important than oil.’ Fact check: WAM reported this water-priority statement after a lecture in December 2011, not at the summit named here.

Work in groups. Discuss the problem of fresh water shortage around the world. Think about the different opinions on this moral dilemma. How can this problem be solved?
