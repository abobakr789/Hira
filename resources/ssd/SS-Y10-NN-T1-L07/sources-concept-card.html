CONCEPT CARD

A moderate lifestyle

A moderate lifestyle means avoiding excess. Its habits include a healthy work-life balance, a balanced diet, an average of at least 60 minutes a day across the week (ages 5–17), being positive, good relationships, being responsible for yourself, your family and your community, and good personal hygiene.

Avoid excess and build healthy, positive habits.

Page 112–114 · Reading 1

What is a healthy lifestyle? How do you think you could improve your lifestyle?

The UAE government is focused on providing a high quality of life for all, where everyone can enjoy rich and fulfilling lives. At the core of this policy is the idea of everyone in the UAE living moderate and healthy lifestyles.

There are many different aspects involved in leading a moderate lifestyle, but the key element is the idea of avoiding excess. Excess means too much of something, for example continuing to eat when your stomach is already full, […] using more water than is necessary, buying more physical goods than we really need, working too many hours, seeking more and more pleasure, seeking to amass more wealth than we need to live, and so on. […] Avoiding excess can mean that you have more of a balance in your life, both mentally and physically.

Having a balance between work and study, and other important areas of life such as spending time with your family and friends, […] and helping other people, can be challenging in today’s dynamic world. […] This idea doesn’t mean that you divide your time in half between work or study, and your life, but that you find the right work-life balance that suits you.

Page 115–116 · Reading 2

Think back over the past week and all the things that you have eaten and drunk. Do you think you eat healthily or not? All research indicates that eating healthily is one of the cornerstones of leading a moderate lifestyle. […] A healthy balanced diet means eating a wide range of foods, but in the right proportions to maintain a healthy body. Fresh fruit and vegetables are part of a healthy and balanced diet. They provide vitamins and minerals and fibre for the body and health experts suggest it should make up around a third of what we eat every day. Foods that are high in starch, for example, bread, rice and pasta, should make up another third of your diet. Beans, pulses, fish, eggs and meat are all good sources of protein. The body needs protein so that it can grow.

Checkpoint: 1. Do you think that you have a balanced diet? Why or why not? 2. What foods would you remove from or add to your current diet to improve the balance? 3. What drinks would you remove from or add to your current diet to improve the balance?

Action Task: Write a short summary of what a balanced diet should include.

Page 117–119 · Reading 3

WHO recommends that children and adolescents aged 5–17 average at least 60 minutes a day of moderate-to-vigorous, mostly aerobic activity across the week. Include vigorous activity and muscle- and bone-strengthening activity at least three days a week. Start gradually and adapt activities to ability and health advice.

Plan an enjoyable week of safe, ability-adapted activity. For ages 5–17, aim toward the WHO weekly average of at least 60 minutes a day of moderate-to-vigorous activity. Some activity is better than none. Ask a trusted adult or health professional about restrictions. Keep any personal health notes private.

“Happiness and positivity are a lifestyle and government’s commitment and a true spirit that unites the Emirati community. […]” His Highness Sheikh Mohammed bin Rashid Al Maktoum (May Allah protect him)

The UAE government has a Ministry of State for Happiness. One of its main goals is to promote a culture of happiness and positivity for all Emiratis […]. Being positive in life centres around the idea that you look for the good in any situation. Thinking positively is linked to success in life and a healthy life.

Page 119–121 · Reading 4

The first place to begin is how we think and how we approach life. Being grateful, for example, can change the way that you see things in life, particularly the small things that can be taken for granted. […] A smile can also be a sign of positivity. […] Being kind to other people is a sign of a positive outlook and being prepared to give.

Discussion Point: Choose three people that you are grateful for. They could be members of your family, or friends. Discuss with your small group why you feel grateful to have these people in your life.

Having positive relationships. The people that you spend time with and the influences that support you provide a solid basis in life. Having close and dependable relationships that provide love and security are essential for a caring society to develop. Parents need to spend time with their children so that children can learn good behaviour and values. […] Grandparents can be excellent role models for children as well, and give a sense of cultural heritage and family tradition. […] It is important for all family members to spend time together, to care for each other and be kind to one another.

Page 121–123 · Reading 5

Being responsible means that you do the right thing at the right time so that people can depend on and trust you. It is very important to develop this characteristic because it is a sign of good behaviour and responsible people usually do well in life.

We can learn how to be responsible from the role models that our parents provide when we are young. […] Being responsible also means making sure that you practise good health and hygiene habits by eating healthily, exercising every day and avoiding illicit drugs and medicine misuse; take prescribed medicines as directed. […] One of the most important things is to be honest with your parents and ask for their advice, as they will have wise suggestions for you. Being kind to family and friends and showing empathy towards other people helps you to develop emotionally.

“It is my duty as the leader of the young people of this country to encourage them to work and to exert themselves in order to raise their own standards and be of service to the country. The individual who is healthy and of a sound mind and body but who does not work commits a crime against himself and society.” Sheikh Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan (May Allah have mercy upon his soul)

You must complete your homework, but leave it until the last minute to finish. 4. You lost your phone. Rather than blaming other people, you are honest with your parents and tell them the truth. […]

Personal hygiene. […] It is very important to develop good personal hygiene habits so that you can look and feel great. […] This includes having a shower or bath regularly, brushing your teeth, washing your hands properly to help stop the spread of bacteria and disease, keeping your nails trimmed and getting a good night’s sleep. […] It is also important to have regular check-ups at the doctor and dentist.

Self-Assessment Task: In the ‘SELF’ circle, write notes about the different ways you are responsible for yourself. Next, draw another circle around the ‘SELF’ circle, and make notes on the different ways you show responsibility towards your family. Draw another circle around the family one and make notes on the different ways that you show responsibility to your community.
