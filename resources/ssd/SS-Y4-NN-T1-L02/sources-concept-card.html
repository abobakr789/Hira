CONCEPT CARD

Mercy, sympathy and empathy

Sympathy means feeling sorry for someone who has a problem. Empathy means understanding and sharing the feelings of another person. Acts of compassion are actions that show we care and want to help. Recognising the feelings of others helps us show mercy and kindness.

Understanding others’ feelings leads to acts of compassion.

Page 21 · Activity 2

A two-year-old boy is playing with a broken toy. His father, Chris, is talking to the person in charge of the city shelter. Chris and his son do not have a home; they’re homeless. Chris is trying to talk this official into letting him and his son stay at the shelter. But the shelter doesn’t allow children to stay. Chris doesn’t know what to do. It’s his responsibility, as a parent, to take care of his son. And he doesn’t want to put his son into foster care or send him to a children’s home. He has to find a solution. Chris listened to the official talking but didn’t say a word. What’s the point of all these words when Chris and his son now have nowhere to live?

Chris used to live in the shelter, while his wife took care of their son. A few days ago, however, she brought their son to Chris, and said she didn’t have the money to care for him. Chris was not the kind of man who would leave his son, so he took the child. However, the shelter administrator found out about the boy and asked Chris to leave. But where could he go? Nowhere.

He could sleep in stations, on the streets or in a nearby park, but how would this little boy live such a hard life? While he was thinking of all this, he felt a hand grabbing his old trousers. The little boy said in a language that only his father could understand, ‘I’m hungry.’ Chris picked up his son and left the building. He looked around but didn’t know where he should go. Page 24–25 · Activity 3

Differentiate between sympathy and empathy. Work in pairs. Discuss the pictures and situations below. Decide whether each situation is an example of sympathy or empathy. Explain your answers.

1. Samira saw a tired cat next to her house. She gave the cat food and water every day until it got better.

2. Nahid watched a documentary about earthquake victims in Kamalpur, Nepal in 2015. She felt very sorry for them, especially when she saw they were homeless after their houses had been destroyed.

3. Saif and Mohammed are childhood friends. They live in the same neighbourhood and go to the same school. One day, Mohammed’s father falls ill and is taken to hospital. Saif stays with his friend. He talks to him about the time his own father was in hospital and the feelings that he had before his father got better.

Page 25–26 · Activity 4 and 5

Activity 4. William Faulkner (American novelist, Nobel Prize winner in 1949) said: “Never be afraid to raise your voice for honesty, truth and compassion against injustice, lying and greed. If people all over the world would do this, it would change the Earth.” Define in your own words sympathy, empathy and compassion.

Activity 5. Can you recall a situation when someone empathised with you and did something that made you happy? Write a short message of appreciation to that person.
