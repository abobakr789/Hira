Page 58–59 · Reading 1

What does diversity and equality mean? How diverse is your community? Activity 1: Read this text and express in your own words the meaning of the words in the bubbles. The textbook uses an older world-population estimate of almost 7.5 billion people, and we are all different! We all look different, act differently, and have different beliefs, values, cultures and different ideas of right and wrong. We all have different tastes in clothing, food, music and so on. Imagine a world where we all were the same: it would become monotonous. The United Arab Emirates is host to a multi-cultural population from around the world. To ensure that we live in a peaceful and prosperous world, we must respect diversity and equality.

Page 60–61 · Reading 2

Activity 2: We are all diverse. Diversity, or difference, brings a richness to life. Many societies are diverse today. Travel is much easier now than a generation ago, and the Internet has turned the world into a global village. In the same way the various ingredients contribute to the rich taste of a meal, various cultures can add exciting new flavours to a society. People are able to interact with people from other cultures, which gives everyone a chance to learn new ideas. A diverse society also has access to a wide range of skills and knowledge. By learning about other cultures, we can learn about, and share, our own.

Of course, diversity also brings its challenges. Where there is difference, there is potential for conflict. Some people respect difference, but others fear it. This can lead to prejudice and misunderstandings. However, with a committed effort, people can work together so that difference is respected. After all, we are all human and we have to share this planet.

Questions: (a) What would life be like in a society that is not diverse? (b) In what ways can our differences influence the learning environment? (c) Do you agree that with a committed effort people can work together for the common good? Why? (d) Do you believe that encouraging people to talk about their differences would lead to greater mutual respect?

Page 62–63 · Reading 3

Activity 3: Now think outside the classroom. The UAE is a very diverse country, with people from different cultures and nationalities living side by side. This creates an opportunity to bring people together to share and learn about each other’s cultures. Many groups were formed so that residents come together on a shared platform of tolerance and understanding. They organise events, trips to the desert, outings to art shows and cooking classes. Questions: (a) In what ways do you see diversity around you? (b) Sometimes communities of a particular background live in enclaves within a larger community. What are the possible advantages and disadvantages? (c) Write at least one other question to help you think about the diversity of your community.

Activity 4: Many people who live in the UAE come from different countries. The book says a large number of Arabs from other countries live in the UAE, as do people from south Asia and south-east Asia, and many Europeans and Americans. They came primarily for the job opportunities. Thanks to the promotion of tolerance by the UAE government and the welcoming attitude of its people, the book calls the UAE a great example of a society that is both diverse and unified. Questions: (a) Does being multicultural make us diverse? (b) How does this influence life in the UAE?

Page 64 · Reading 4

Activity 5: Read the text and answer the question. A student has studied in the UK for three years and is often asked “where are you from?”, because the accent sounds different at times. The student’s father is Emirati and the mother is Canadian with Asian roots. The student was born in Canada and lived there until the age of six, and then moved with the father, an ambassador. The longest period at home in the UAE was during high school. The student hopes to reconnect with many people from all the countries they can call home, who live in the UAE. In the text, the student says the UAE is home, and also home for many people from around the world who share the same story.

Question: What makes the UAE your home? Discuss with your partner.

Page 65 · Reading 5

Activity 6: Read this information about the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR). The UDHR was adopted in 1948 to try to ensure that all people are treated equally and with dignity and respect, in order to promote an equal society. The UDHR affirms that everyone is entitled to equal opportunities, regardless of race, gender, disability, age, beliefs and values, or ethnicity.

Task: In groups, find examples of ensuring equality in each of the categories mentioned in the declaration. Share your examples with the rest of the class. The book adds that in our schools and community we can bring more awareness, be more considerate of others, accept difference and try not to discriminate, and we must demonstrate these values in our everyday lives.
