📖 Book text

Student Book Vol. 1 · Original text from the Student Book.

Page 83–85 · Reading 1

Why are diseases considered a global problem? What can countries do to stop diseases from spreading? What is one health issue that needs more awareness?

Activity 1: Discuss what the following statement means. “All over the world, health concerns are the same.”

Page 86 · Reading 2

Activity 2: Read about malaria and answer the questions that follow. Malaria is an infectious disease that is caused by a parasite carried by mosquitoes. The symptoms of the disease include fever, chills and flu-like illness. If it is not treated, people can die. Although this is a preventable disease, “bad environmental sanitation, stagnant water, poor access to healthcare and lack of information are still contributing factors to its existence” says one health expert. […] Statistics show that a child dies every two minutes from the disease and about half the world’s population is at risk of catching it. […] there is hope that one day malaria will also become a disease of the past.

Page 86–87 · Reading 3

Malaria was introduced to the UAE by people travelling abroad who then came to the country and spread the disease. The UAE has worked hard to stop the spread of malaria regionally and internationally. Thankfully, the efforts of the UAE government have kept the country free from the disease since 2007. […] The UAE made a large contribution to the Roll Back Malaria Partnership, the global platform in which more than 500 partners worked together against malaria.

Questions: (a) What is an infectious disease? (b) How is malaria different from a typical flu? (c) Why do countries need to work together to fight infectious diseases like malaria? Key Fact: World Malaria Day is on April 25th. On this day, people help to spread awareness about the disease.

Page 88 · Reading 4

Activity 3: Read the following short articles about the challenges of malaria and malnutrition today. Tanzania: Malaria risk is high in Tanzania, especially in rural areas. However, there are few prevention measures in poor areas. One woman, who knew her son was ill with malaria, went to a clinic to get free medicine, but the clinic did not have any. Medications were usually supplied at the beginning of each month and due to high demand, there was none available by the end of the first week. […] She was then advised to go to a nearby town where she might find some for free. […] Will she be luckier here?

Page 89 · Reading 5

Guatemala: Guatemala has a very high rate of malnutrition. In 2015, around 46% of the population was stunted. This means people are shorter and thinner than normal. […] The main cause of stunted growth, experts say, is lack of vital nutrients during the first few years of life. But stunting is not just about height. Malnourishment also means that these children get sick more easily and their brain function does not develop properly. Stunted kids are more likely to drop out of school and grow up to be unskilled workers with less economic success later in life.

In your group, consider the following questions: (a) What is the main health challenge in each article? (b) What is the cause of these two health challenges? (c) What could the global community do to help these people?

Page 90–91 · Reading 6

Activity 4: In your groups, write down one idea for solving the problem of malnutrition. Then discuss why this is a good strategy. Share your ideas with another group. Give reasons why you agree or disagree with the other group.

Activity 5: Choose a health issue that affects young people today. This can be a local issue or a global issue that you want more people in the UAE to be aware of. Work together as a group to create a public health awareness campaign to highlight the issue. You can create a poster, a flyer, a website, or a short visual or audio ad. Ideas: obesity, awareness against smoking, physical exercise, how healthy is your diet?

Let’s talk
