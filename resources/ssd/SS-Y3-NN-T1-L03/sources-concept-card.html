📖 Book text

Student Book Vol. 1 · Original text from the Student Book.

Page 26–27 · Reading 1

Do you know of any real-life situations of discrimination? What can you and your classmates do to stop discrimination in your classroom or school? Why is discrimination bad?

Activity 1: I walk around the room with my classmates and answer questions that are on the walls. What is the meaning of discrimination? Give examples of discrimination. Where can discrimination happen?

Page 28 · Reading 2

Rajeef and I went up to the classroom, feeling sad and disappointed. […] I said to the class, ‘I want to ask you to think about what happened in the playground. Look at Rajeef. He is part of this class, working with us and taking part in all classroom activities. We cannot exclude him from our activities outside the classroom.’

Bassem suddenly broke the silence, saying, ‘I feel that I have been unfair towards Rajeef’. Amer added, ‘I feel that too. We are all the same in some ways and different in other ways. And we have to respect that.’

Page 29 · Reading 3

‘After class, let’s go back and finish the game, letting everyone participate.’ I was excited and we went back to the playground. Rajeef went quickly and took his place in midfield. All through the game, he played very well. He stopped the other team from scoring. He passed the ball very well too. He played fairly and made everyone very happy. Rajeef’s team won the match.

As soon as the match was over, we all ran to Rajeef. We carried him on our shoulders and ran with him across the playground. We shouted, ‘Go Rajeef, go!’

Page 30–31 · Reading 4

Questions: (1) Does the writer solve the discrimination or just sympathise with Rajeef? Explain your answer. (2) If Rajeef’s team had lost the game, do you think his classmates would have wanted to play with him next time? Explain your answer. (3) What rights does Rajeef have as a member of the class? (4) Does discrimination only happen among children?

Let’s Think: Let’s think about the lines, ‘We are all the same in some ways and different in other ways. And we have to respect that’.

Activity 3, Case Study: (1) Haidy’s classmates tease her and make her feel bad every day because she has red hair. (2) Shouq and Alia didn’t let Amal eat with them because she does not eat meat. Write what type of discrimination is happening, and how you could solve each situation as an individual.

Page 32–33 · Reading 5

Activity 4: A plan everyone can use to solve discrimination at school or at home. Work in groups. Think about something that is unfair and discriminatory at school or at home. Work with your group to come up with a plan to solve the discrimination. The form below will help you.

The form has these parts: Subject of Discrimination · Objectives · Success Criteria · Required Budget · Schedule · Actions to be Implemented.
