Page 139–140 · Reading 1

What materials do you think traditional African art was made out of? What impact do you think traditional African art has had on culture? Art has always played an important role in culture. Not only does it serve as a form of expression and creative design, it can also be used to show elements of the culture that are deemed important and significant. When you think of traditional African art, what comes to your mind? Use your imagination and draw a picture that represents your interpretation of African art.

Page 140–141 · Reading 2

Traditional African art combines stone, wood, clay, iron, brass, gold and other materials. The items were used for different purposes in each culture that created them. Examples include masks, statues, paintings and fabric. Rock art is one of Africa’s ancient art traditions. The Dabous giraffe engravings in Niger are estimated to be thousands of years old. Their exact date is uncertain; they should not be presented as the earliest known African stone art.

Traditional wooden art: wood was used to make expressive masks, figures, doors and ornaments. In West Africa, many ethnic groups created wood carvings used in ceremonies, which represented animals, loved ones and important cultural figures. The book says some cultures, such as the Dan of present-day Ivory Coast, created ceremonial masks made of solid wood and decorated with gold, copper, silver and cowrie shells, and that most of the time the masks were worn by special people like the king or other important people.

Page 142–143 · Reading 3

In addition to masks, traditional African wooden art included sculptures, plaques and decorative doors. Traditional sculptures represented something similar to the masks. The book says that during the 17th through 19th centuries the ancient Kuba Kingdom was well known for creating artwork and decorations. Around 1700 CE, the Kuba King introduced a new type of wooden sculpture called Ndop. Ndops are wooden carvings created to represent the king and elements of his personality. Later kings had their own Ndops made to create their own legacy.

Activity 1: Answer the questions. List two traditional art forms from the text. What were they made from? What are Ndops? Who had them made? For what purpose?

Page 144–147 · Reading 4

Traditional metallic art used materials such as gold, copper, iron and other metals. The Nok culture of present-day Nigeria is associated with early ironworking. The Metropolitan Museum describes possible Nok iron smelting and forging by the sixth century BCE, not a continent-wide Iron Age beginning in 1500 BCE. Metal technologies developed at different times in different African regions. Smelting uses heat and chemical reactions to extract metal from ore; it is not simply melting rock.

Igbo-Ukwu is known for technically accomplished copper-alloy and bronze castings, including vessels, ornaments and regalia. It should not be described only as a community of iron smelters. Bronze artwork was used throughout the Kingdom of Benin in the 16th and 17th centuries, with detailed representations made through metal casting. The Yoruba civilization, including the Ife, created metal art with detailed facial features. Activity 2: Why do you think so much detail went into the artwork of ancient Africa? Choose one of the African art pieces shown in this lesson, identify it, describe it in one or more sentences, and talk about who or what you think it represents.

Page 148–149 · Reading 5

Activity 3: Art can be used for creative expression, beauty or as cultural descriptions. Choose one of the African art examples from this lesson and explain how you would categorise it. Do you think it should be labelled as art for beauty, creative expression, a cultural description or something else? Explain your answer in detail.

Activity 4: African art has very specific characteristics that set it apart from art forms from different parts of the world. What do you think makes African art different from other art forms in different parts of the world? Research traditional art from another region outside of Africa and compare it to some of the African art you have seen in this lesson.
