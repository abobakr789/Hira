CONCEPT CARD

Responding to emergencies

The official UAE portal u.ae lists 999 for police, 998 for ambulance and 997 for the fire department (civil defence). The book says a fire needs heat, oxygen and fuel; if your clothes are on fire, stop, drop and roll; and if a door is hot, do not open it. Smoke alarms have helped reduce the home fire death rate, says the book.

Be prepared.

Safety adaptation of Student Book pp. 75–77. UAE emergency number checked with u.ae; escape priorities checked with London Fire Brigade. Pupils must not fight fires. Follow local civil-defence instructions.

Student Book Vol. 1 · Student Book readings with clearly marked health/safety adaptations.

Page 70–71 · Reading 1

What different emergency services are there? What should you do when there is a fire? Activity 1: Find the keywords. Look back at the key words from the previous lesson and the key words from this lesson, and find them in the word search in the Student Book.

Page 73 · Reading 2

Activity 2: Karim is a very brave boy. When Karim came home to find his father lying on the floor, he knew something was wrong. He gently shook his father and called his name, but he got no response. […] he knew this was a medical emergency. Without hesitating, Karim took out his phone and dialed 998. Soon, an ambulance was racing to his home. “We learned about first aid in school,” Karim explains. “I know how to handle small injuries and cuts. But I also know what I can’t do! Sometimes all you can do is call in the emergency services in the United Arab Emirates.” […] Our teacher taught us that we have to be prepared for emergencies. We all memorised the emergency phone number.

Page 73–74 · Reading 3

Questions: (a) Why does the writer say Karim was brave? (b) How did Karim know it was a medical emergency? Would you have known it was an emergency? (c) Why does Karim say he’s no hero? (d) What do you think might have happened if Karim had not known the number of the emergency services?

Activity 3: Look at the images in the Student Book and decide which emergency service should be called. Write the name and number of this service and the reason for it. UAE numbers (u.ae): police 999, ambulance 998, fire department (civil defence) 997.

Page 75–77 · Reading 4

Activity 4, safety-adapted reading: If fire or smoke is in your home, alert others, use a safe escape route and stay outside. In the UAE call civil defence on 997 as soon as it is safe. Do not delay escape to collect belongings or fight the fire. Follow the school fire drill and emergency instructions. Keep low if there is smoke. Do not open a hot door or use a lift. If trapped, close the door, block smoke gaps if possible, call 997 and tell the operator where you are. Signal from a window and follow the operator's advice. Do not jump or try an unsafe escape. A fire needs heat, oxygen and fuel. If clothes catch fire: stop, drop and roll. Questions: (a) What can supply heat or fuel? (b) Why should you not run with burning clothes? (c) Why leave a hot door closed? (d) How can a safe escape plan help?

Page 77–79 · Reading 5

Activity 5: Look at the diagrams in the Student Book. In the UAE, electrical malfunctions, cooking appliances and gas leakages are the most common cause of fires. In Dubai, the majority of fires occurred in motor vehicles, followed by residential homes. Since smoke alarms were first introduced in the 1970s in Ireland, they have helped to reduce the home fire death rate by one half. Questions: (a) Why do you think the number of fires that occurred between 1977 and 1995 has decreased? (b) How important is it to have a smoke detector? (c) Why do you think older people are more at risk of dying in a fire? (d) What could you do in your home to protect yourself from a fire?

Activity 6: Consider an emergency that hasn’t been discussed in this lesson. Write what the emergency is, what the risks are, and what you should do in the situation.
