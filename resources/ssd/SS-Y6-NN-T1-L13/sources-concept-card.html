The Republic of Venice

Venice is a city of over one hundred small islands. In the 12th century CE it controlled much of the Adriatic and Aegean Seas. Its large ships, called galleys, helped Alexios I in 1082 in exchange for free trade without tariffs. Venice became Egypt’s exclusive European trading partner for spices and other goods.

📖 Book text

Student Book Vol. 1 · Original text from the Student Book.

Page 126–128 · Reading 1

What can be learned from the Republic of Venice? Why might someone break a promise they made? Have you ever broken a promise? What was the reason you didn’t keep it? (Personal sharing is optional.)

Activity 1: During the Middle Ages, people often travelled from one country to another by sea. Write four advantages of travelling by sea instead of land.

Page 130 · Reading 2

Venice is a small city in Italy made up of over one hundred small islands. Instead of streets and cars, the city uses small rivers and boats to move around. Nowadays, Venice is a popular tourist attraction. But in the High Middle Ages, Venice was a wealthy and influential maritime power. In the 12th century CE, Venice controlled much of the Adriatic and Aegean Seas. Venice was able to grow wealthy and powerful because of the trading relationship it established with Egypt and other Middle Eastern countries.

Page 130–131 · Reading 3

The Venetian maritime fleets were useful in many ways. They were called galleys. They were very large, with more than one hundred oarsmen moving it through the water. Armed soldiers stood on the main decks of the ships, ready to battle pirates, enemy ships, and other dangers. […] These ships were so powerful that, in 1082, the Byzantine Emperor, Alexios I Komnenos, signed a treaty with Venice. In exchange for the Venetians’ help against the Normans, a fierce group of warriors from France, the Venetians enjoyed free trade throughout the empire, without tariffs. […] They developed such a strong relationship with Egypt that they became Egypt’s exclusive European trading partner for spices and other goods.

Page 133–135 · Reading 4

Activity 2: Are these statements about Venice true or false? (1) Venice was a province of the Roman Empire. (2) Alexios I Komnenos was the leader of the Normans. (3) Venice helped the Roman Empire defeat the Egyptians. (4) Venice grew rich on trade with Egypt. (5) Venice was wealthy and powerful for less than one hundred years.

Activity 3: Answer questions about the Venetian navy: what seas, who did Alexios ask for help against, what was special about Venice and Egypt, what did Venice ask for in return, what was a Venetian ship called. Activity 4: Circle Venice, Egypt and Constantinople on the map and draw lines. Activity 5: Research the masks of Venice and draw each mask under its description.

Page 136–137 · Reading 5

Activity 6: Venice promised to help Alexios fight the Normans, but they did not keep their promise. However, they did get the benefits of the deal. Do you feel this is fair? Write your ideas.

Activity 7: Write about a time when you or someone you know didn’t keep a promise. How did you feel about it?
