CONCEPT CARD

Benefits of a moderate lifestyle

Moderation brings calm, focus and productivity. A work-life balance and boundaries help; healthy eating and exercise strengthen body and mind and build self-confidence; problem-solving skills, positivity, positive relationships (a support network), responsibility and personal hygiene all bring benefits.

A moderate lifestyle benefits body, mind and community.

Page 129–131 · Reading 1

How can you develop positive relationships with others? What do you do to maintain good personal hygiene?

Leading a moderate lifestyle shows respect for yourself and for others. It means that you have balance in your life and can bring a sense of calm to everyday living. Practising moderation in all things helps people to be more focused and more productive members of society.

Creating a work-life balance that works for you means that you maximise your time. You can work or study hard, but still enjoy the time you have with your family and friends, doing sports and other activities. […] For many working people, one way of achieving such a balance is to avoid taking work home. […] This provides a boundary between work and home, which can provide benefits, as it allows the stresses of work to stay at work, and not affect family time.

“I don’t like to see someone who does not have a hobby, sport or passion in life. Idleness is not only an attitude towards work; it starts with the mind.” His Highness Sheikh Mohammed bin Rashid Al Maktoum (May Allah protect him)

Page 132–133 · Reading 2

Eating a well-balanced diet has many benefits to your physical and mental wellbeing. What you eat affects how you feel and the amount of energy that you have. […] Eating healthily has the benefits of contributing to strong bones and teeth, keeping your blood pressure and cholesterol under control, and having a healthy heart. […] Healthy foods boost your cognitive abilities and make you more alert and focused.

A calorie is a unit of energy. When we consume food and drink, we increase the number of calories in our body. Exercise and activity burns up these calories. If we put more calories into our body than we use up, the excess is stored as body fat. Physical activity helps to maintain the calorie balance in the body and maintains a healthy body weight.

Regular physical activity supports heart, bone and muscle health and can improve sleep, concentration and mental well-being. These benefits involve several body systems; do not reduce them to a simple claim that more oxygen makes the brain release helpful chemicals.

Page 133–134 · Reading 3

Experts have shown that regular exercise can improve mood and helps you sleep much more soundly. Taking daily exercise can reduce stress, increases the likelihood of living longer and also helps to build your own self-confidence. This self-confidence can spread to all other aspects of your life.

People need to solve problems on a daily basis. However, problem-solving skills do not develop by themselves. To solve problems, we need to listen and think carefully, look at the different solutions that are possible and find workable solutions, which may involve making compromises. It is also important to think about other people’s opinions and needs when problem-solving.

Action Task: Write down a problem that you have. Think about why it is a problem. Brainstorm at least four possible solutions. Evaluate each of the solutions, focusing on the positives and negatives. Rate each solution from 0 (not very good) to 10 (very good). Put the solution into action. […] Evaluate the outcome, focusing on what worked well, what didn’t work so well and any changes that could be made to make the solution more effective.

Page 136–138 · Reading 4

“Do not underestimate your thoughts, because your energy, determination, success and even health, are directly related to positive thoughts.” His Highness Sheikh Mohammed bin Rashid Al Maktoum (May Allah protect him)

Hopeful thinking and supportive relationships can help people cope with difficulties. They do not guarantee health or success. Mental health depends on many personal, social and environmental factors; a person who is struggling is not at fault for failing to think positively.

Our happiness and success in life are essentially based on the relationships that we build with other people. Having positive and healthy relationships is a choice. Having and creating positive relationships with our family and friends includes listening to each other, trusting and respecting each other and making time to spend time together. […] Strong relationships also work as a support network when you are having difficult times in your life, as you can ask your family and friends for help.

Page 138–140 · Reading 5

Surrounding yourself with positive relationships allows you to share happy moments on a more regular basis and develop your sense of purpose and meaning. Like a ripple in the water, having positive relationships spreads out and touches everyone. These types of relationships create strong and healthy family bonds, which in turn create strong, positive communities. […]

Action Task: Creating strong relationships with friends is an important part of leading a healthy life. Make a list of six characteristics that you need to make friends and describe why each characteristic is important.

Being responsible in life means doing the things that need to be done when they need to be done, as well as the behaviour you choose. This develops self-confidence and a ‘can do’ attitude to everything in life, which helps us to feel good about ourselves. […] When you are responsible, people trust and depend on you because they know you are reliable. […] By showing this characteristic, you become a role model for others to follow.

“To hold others responsible for failure is a way of escaping one’s own responsibility. Responsibility is a heavy burden and a great honour at the same time. […]” His Highness Sheikh Mohammed bin Rashid Al Maktoum (May Allah protect him)

Page 140–142 · Reading 6

Personal hygiene involves practising daily routines that keep your body clean. This involves washing, oral care and hair care. The risk of harmful bacteria and germs can be reduced by washing your hands regularly and thoroughly after visiting the bathroom, using a tissue when you sneeze, and protecting cuts on your skin with plasters helps to avoid cross-infection. […] Brushing and flossing your teeth regularly keeps your breath fresh and reduces the risk of gum disease and tooth decay […]. Hygiene can support health. Access to water, disability, illness and other circumstances can affect routines; appearance is not evidence of a person’s worth or self-respect. […]

Thinking Task: What do you think are your three most important personal hygiene routines? Give reasons for your choices. Checkpoint: Why is it important to practise good personal hygiene? Why does good personal hygiene affect the way that other people perceive us?

Self-Assessment Task: Having looked at the benefits of leading a moderate life, choose the three benefits that you think are most important. For each of the three benefits, write a short description of why you think that they are the most important.
