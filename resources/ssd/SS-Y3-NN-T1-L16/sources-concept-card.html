CONCEPT CARD

Reading a map

A map shows the location of places. A grid system has lines that cross each other to form boxes. Each box is a coordinate. Letters across the top and numbers down the side give coordinates of locations.

Letters and numbers help us find places.

Page 172–173 · Reading 1

Do you know how to use a map? When do people use maps?

Warm up: Brainstorm. In groups, answer the questions. Activity 1: Are there any numbers in your address, like a flat number or a number on your street? Why do we use numbers in our addresses?

Page 175 · Reading 2

Most maps show the location of places. A very simple map shows pictures to indicate landmarks at different locations. For example, this map of an amusement park shows where different activities are in the park.

To find a place on a map of a larger area, a map has a grid system. A grid system has lines that cross each other to form boxes. Each box is a coordinate. Across the top of the grid are letters. Down the side of the grid are numbers. Together, the letters and numbers give coordinates of locations.

Page 175–178 · Reading 3

Activity 2: Can you locate these things on the map? A clown giving out balloons, the castle ride, the ferris wheel, the snack cart, the train ride, the bumper cars, the roller coaster. What else can you see?

Activity 3: Look at the grid and give the colour for each coordinate. The first one has been done for you.

Page 179–183 · Reading 4

Activity 4: The city map has gridlines to show coordinates. Use the map to answer the questions. (Hint: look at the point of each icon, not the picture.) (1) Where is the parking lot (P)? 2-C. (2) Where is the hospital (H)? (3) Where is the hotel (house)? (4) Where is the gas station? (5) Where is the restaurant (fork and knife)? (6) Where is the information centre (star)?

Activity 5: The map shows where different animals live. Use the map to answer the questions. Write sentences. Where do elephants live? They live in Africa and Asia. Where do pandas live? Where do kangaroos live? Where do penguins live? Where do polar bears live? Choose two more animals and write where they live.

Page 184–187 · Reading 5

Activity 6: Play with a partner, but do not let your partner see your map. Put only one name in each coordinate. Write the names of 5 people in a square in the map. Your partner will do the same. Then, take turns saying coordinates trying to find your partner’s people first. Whoever finds 5 people wins! For example: “She is not there.” or “You found her.”

Activity 7: On the map grid, draw a picture of your classroom. Put the front of the classroom at the top. Now, write the coordinates of your desk, the teacher’s desk, and a bookshelf. After that add 3 items of your own and give the coordinates of each item.

Let’s talk

① What is a grid system? ② What are across the top of the grid? ③ What are down the side of the grid?
