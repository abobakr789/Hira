📖 Book text

Student Book Vol. 1 · Original text from the Student Book.

Page 76–77 · Reading 1

What bias and discrimination have you faced? Activity 1: Complete the group activity to better understand how discrimination can make you feel. In order to be more tolerant, we need to understand bias, discrimination and prejudice. These attitudes can be destructive. How may the individuals in the image be discriminated against? Why?

Page 78 · Reading 2

Activity 2: “Don’t judge until you know them!” These words stayed in the mind of an Emirati professional after each trip abroad. In an interview she said that what we have heard and what we experience are always poles apart. She said people tend to make prejudiced comments about people of all nations, and that when you have direct experience you can distinguish between prejudice and reality. Travelling also taught her that many universal values and ideas bring people together. In Australia, a local family talked passionately about koalas and kangaroos, and she felt Emiratis would talk about falcons with the same passion. In the Netherlands, windmills reminded her of the story of Don Quixote, and many people of various nationalities said the same thoughts came to their minds.

Questions: (a) How does travel help people overcome their prejudices? (b) Do you agree that what we have heard and what we experience are poles apart? Why? (c) We know more about the world than at any other time in history. Has this knowledge increased or diminished our prejudices? Discuss.

Page 79–80 · Reading 3

Activity 3: Consider the situations in the book’s table. How comfortable would you feel in these situations? Rate yourself on the scale: 1 very comfortable, 2 comfortable, 3 neutral, 4 uncomfortable, 5 very uncomfortable. The situations are grouped in sections (for example, meeting someone of a different background, seeing a person with a disability, and being with older people). When a full section is completed, fill in the total score in the last column.

Then answer the reflection question in the book: What did I learn about my own prejudices? What surprised me?

Page 82 · Reading 4

Activity 4: Consider the quotes from H.H. Sheikh Mohammed bin Rashid Al Maktoum and from the late president Nelson Mandela, then compare the messages. In the first quote, Sheikh Mohammed says that what makes the UAE proud is not the height of its buildings but the openness and tolerance of its nation. In the second, he says the aim is to protect society from ignorance and hatred and to build a model of tolerance in the region. In the third, Nelson Mandela challenges everyone to take the ideals of tolerance and respect for others and put them to practical use in schools, communities and throughout our lives.

Task: Compare the messages in each of the quotes.

Page 83–84 · Reading 5

Activity 5: You are now going to role play different scenarios to see prejudiced attitudes in action. The book gives four short scenarios: a girl told by a coach she is not strong enough to swim long distances; a boy told he is too heavy to dance; a girl whose friend says a father should not look after a baby; and a deaf boy left out because explaining things to him takes too long. After you watch each scenario, discuss with your group: (a) What type of bias is this? (b) Criticise each scenario showing the impact it has on each person. (c) Consider another reaction to those types of discrimination.

Activity 6: How can you overcome bias and prejudice, and reject discrimination? With your group, write down three actions you can take to create a more tolerant environment. These will form the basis of your class charter.
