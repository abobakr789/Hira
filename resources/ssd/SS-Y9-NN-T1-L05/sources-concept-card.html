📖 Book text

Student Book Vol. 1 · Original text from the Student Book.

Page 51–52 · Reading 1

How can nations deal with challenges such as including people of determination into society and improving education systems? Why are human rights important? Activity 1: So far, you have discussed examples of global challenges. Define what can hinder dealing with these challenges by reading the words of Her Highness Sheikha Jawaher Mohammed Al Qasimi. The book says she calls funding humanitarian aid a thorny issue, even though governments quickly agree it is needed, and says we must start by accepting that we all have a responsibility to others; without that, solutions will only be short-term.

Questions: (1) In your opinion, why is funding aid often a “thorny issue”? (2) Do you agree with this? Why or why not?

Page 52–54 · Reading 2

Activity 2: Read about the education sector in the world, the challenges it faces, and how to deal with them at the global level. The book says there is an urgent need for greater progress in education. On current trends, it says primary education in Africa and West Asia will be mainstream by 2048, and preparatory and secondary education by 2062 and 2082, which is much later than 2030, the deadline for the Sustainable Development Goals. It says UNESCO Director-General Irina Bokova called for a fundamental change in how we see the role of education in global development.

The book says that in Sweden the Education Minister, Gustav Fridolin, described students leaving school before completing high school as one of the most important problems the government is trying to resolve. Sweden aims to give teachers more time for students, to increase the number of teachers and their salaries, and to provide a high level of education for students whose mother tongue is not Swedish.

Questions: (1) Write a paragraph about how important the education sector is in the development of nations, and how you think the UAE compares with other countries. (2) With a classmate, research the reality of education in Finland and Algeria, and link it to the country’s progress and the people’s daily lives.

Page 55–57 · Reading 3

Activity 3: What challenges do children in the world today face? To learn how the world is working to meet these challenges, read the UN report. The book says the UNICEF report “The State of the World’s Children 2013” focuses on children with disabilities and their inclusion, and that its main message is that they are “not problems” but sisters, brothers, daughters, sons and friends. It says inclusion is possible but needs a change in perception and vision, that people of determination have the same rights as others and can be agents of change, and that their voices must be heard in policies and programmes.

The book says the report recommends that they be encouraged and given access to take part alongside their peers, for example through universal design, such as ramps instead of stairs, audio books, automatic doors and low-floor buses. It describes Abu Dhabi’s support in education, employment, healthcare and social development, and describes the Khalifa Award for Education. Its current people-of-determination field recognises professionals who support learners and specialised institutions or centres, not an automatic payment to every person with a disability. The official field page lists AED 75,000 for an individual professional and AED 100,000 for institutions or centres; the textbook amount belongs to its older account.

Questions: (1) With a classmate, research other projects sponsored by the UAE dedicated to people with special needs, and compare them to relevant UN reports. (2) Write a short biography of an Emirati person of determination and of an American person of determination, focusing on elements that can inspire achievements in the UAE.

Page 57–59 · Reading 4

Activity 4: Did you know that promoting respect for human rights is one of the objectives of the United Nations? The book says it is one of the goals and distinguishing factors of the UN, and that in November 2013 the Secretary-General renewed the commitment of the Secretariat, its funds and programmes to uphold human rights. Early warning may be one of the organisation’s most important roles in preventing conflict. Tasks: research local and international government bodies and how they protect human rights; and explain the importance of social responsibility towards the members of UAE society and the global community by researching local human rights associations.

Activity 5: Read the text on China’s industrial challenges and the UAE’s technological thinking. The book says an OECD report found that China’s economy is moving from a system driven by investment and low-cost items to one driven by innovation, and that the government is investing heavily in research and development. The book says that in 2015 China announced a plan to become a society driven by technological innovation by 2020. It says executives at the Global Manufacturing and Industrialisation Summit in Abu Dhabi confirmed the UAE’s commitment to developing industry, and that the UAE’s varied industries, such as food, pharmaceuticals and metals, led to advanced activities such as building ships and some aircraft parts.

Tasks: (1) With classmates, discuss the importance of innovative technological thinking, and prepare a report showing the UAE’s global standing compared to a major European industrial country. (2) In a one-minute recorded voice message, state how you will play an active role in the UAE’s vision of the technological industrial revolution.

Page 60–61 · Reading 5

Activity 6: (1) Discuss the saying of the philosopher Socrates given in the book, bearing in mind what you have learned about meeting global ethical challenges. (2) Write your own saying about global ethical challenges as a motto for your future life and the lives of your classmates.

Activity 7: With your classmates, prepare and implement a project. The book says you can challenge many ethical problems, even with limited abilities, and that in this project you will work with your team to put a smile on the faces of others and to alleviate their suffering in all forms. The book says the project may be the first step in cooperating with local and global communities, and that you may improve the living conditions of the needy and the poor through financial or moral support, so assistance should not be limited to giving money and in-kind help.
