Opening questions and Activity 1

📖 Book text 2/5

I Have The Right To Play (Part 1)

Read the story.

Rajeef is my neighbour and classmate at school. He is a quiet, peaceful and good boy. He is always happy and smiling. He likes playing football, and always tries to talk to his classmates to make friends with them. But some of them don’t talk to him.

One day, we decided to play a football match in the playground. […] Rajeef went to join his team, but three boys stood in his way to block him. They didn’t want to let him play. […] Rajeef looked sad. He looked at me. He wanted someone to speak up for him, to take his side.

I went to the referee and strongly told him: ‘Either we all play without discrimination and without leaving out anybody – and Rajeef plays with us – or you stop the game and we don’t play’.

Student Book Vol. 1 · page 12 · Reading 2

Student Book p. 12 (selected passages). Part 2 of the story is in Lesson 2.

📖 Book text 3/5

Questions and Let’s Think

Answer together.

Questions: (1) How do you think Rajeef felt when his classmates refused to let him play with them? (2) What do we call the attitude of the classmates who refused to let Rajeef participate in the game? (3) If you were one of Rajeef’s classmates, what would you have done? Why? (4) What do you think the referee said?

Let’s Think: Let’s think together about the statement, ‘I have the right to play’, and discuss it.

Student Book Vol. 1 · page 14 · Reading 3

Student Book p. 14. Suggested answers: (1) sad, left out; (2) discrimination; (3) and (4) accept reasoned answers.

📖 Book text 4/5

People of Determination in sport

Read and answer.

Activity 3: Let’s read about people of determination taking part in world sports. In 2019, there were Special Olympics World Games in Abu Dhabi. Almost seven thousand men and women athletes of determination played. The athletes came from 170 countries. The athletes showed their athletic skills in twenty-two games.

(a) Why are these special games held and who are they for? (b) Which athletic games will the players compete in?

On April 18th, 2017, His Highness Sheikh Mohammed bin Rashid al Maktoum (May God protect him) started the National Strategy for Empowering People with Disabilities. He said they should be called “People of Determination” instead of “People with Disabilities”. […] This shows that people can do anything if they put their minds to it.

Student Book Vol. 1 · page 14–16 · Reading 4

Student Book pp. 14–16 (selected passages). Answers to (a) and (b) come from the text and the pictures; accept reasoned answers.

📖 Book text 5/5

Activity 4, fair or unfair

Decide and say why.

Activity 4: Decide if the situations below are fair or unfair. Write either ‘fair’ or ‘unfair’ on each of the lines below.

(a) Hessa was going on a trip with her classmates. She took ages deciding what clothes to wear, so was very late. Her classmates had to wait for a long time.

(b) Ruba plays the piano every day while her neighbour Walid is studying.

(c) A boy’s parents give their son twice as much pocket money as they give to his younger brother.

(d) My aunt came back from her vacation and got gifts for everyone except my younger sister.

Student Book Vol. 1 · page 17 · Reading 5

Student Book p. 17. Suggested answers: (a) unfair to the classmates who waited; (b) unfair to Walid; (c) unfair; (d) unfair. Accept reasoned answers.
