Chinese inventions

The book shows four inventions from ancient China: paper, movable type printing, ceramic pottery and silk. Each one used skilled work and changed daily life. Joseph Needham studied Chinese science and technology, and the book asks us to respect hard work and the skills of different trades.

Skills build civilization.

Student Book Vol. 1 · Original text from the Student Book.

Page 148–149 · Reading 1

What are some examples of technology developed in China? Why was the technology important? Activity 1: In 1947, Joseph Needham, the physician and scholar of the history of Chinese science and technology, gave a lecture on Science and Society in Ancient China. It was the start of a major effort to study the origins and influence of Chinese technology and science in world history. This project has led to the ongoing series of 9 or more volumes called Science and Civilization in China. Because of this initiative, people are more aware of advances in technology, engineering, science and medicine during all periods of Chinese history.

Questions: (a) Who was Joseph Needham? (b) What was his profession? (c) What is he famous for?

Page 150–152 · Reading 2

Activity 2, Invention 1: Paper making went through three steps. 1. Selecting and separating and then washing the raw materials. 2. Cooking and stirring the fibres. 3. Pounding, separating, and drying the paper fibres in a mould.

For each invention, answer: (a) What is the invention? (b) Do we still use it? (c) How has it changed? (d) Why was this an innovative technology and how did it make life easier or better for people?

Page 153 · Reading 3

Invention 2: The invention of blocks of type that could be used to repeat printing of words, individual letters and seals was developed in China using wooden blocks. The wooden blocks could be used to print on paper, silk, or other materials. The Chinese started using moveable type for printing books in 1040 CE. They carved a single word into a block, then put the blocks together to form pages of writing. Pages were printed to form a book. Illustrations from the Imperial Printing Office of the Qing Dynasty Court show the making and setting of movable type. First, one group of artisans made blanks out of blocks of wood. Another group carved the words out of the wood blocks. The blocks with words were placed in sorting trays. Finally, the blocks were taken from the sorting trays to form the text of a book or document.

Page 153–156 · Reading 4

Invention 3: Making ceramic pottery is an ancient type of technology. To make a ceramic pot, artisans first retrieved clay from the earth. They stirred and mixed the clay with water until it was the right consistency to use. The clay was shaped on a wheel. Most pots were painted and glazed to produce colour and add a shiny coating. Finally, the pots were heated at high temperature in a furnace to melt the glaze and produce a strong and beautiful pot.

Invention 4: Silk was one of the most valuable goods on the world market for many centuries. The Chinese were the first to develop elaborate spindles for home weaving and later for industrial production. Silk is made through a delicate process of unwinding the long fibres from the cocoon of the silkworm which lives in the mulberry tree. […] In the classical Chinese silk-reeling machine, the operator rotates the main reel with a crank and pulley. In modern times, the reeling machine is powered by electricity using a driving belt.

Page 157–159 · Reading 5

Activity 3: Write a short reflection on one of the inventions from ancient China. Discuss what the invention contributed to civilization. How did it improve society? What other innovations occurred because of it? Activity 4: Match the key words (mulberry tree, wooden blocks, paper, pottery) to the descriptions: a process of printing on paper; the shaping and baking of terracotta and clay made this possible; this invention allowed for the development of writing and making of scrolls; a rare tree found in East Asia in which the silkworm grows and is harvested. Activity 5: Reflect on how respect for hard work and the skills of different trades contribute to a civilized society.
