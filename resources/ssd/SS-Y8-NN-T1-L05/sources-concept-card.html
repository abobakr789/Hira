📖 Book text

Student Book Vol. 1 · Original text from the Student Book.

Page 46–47 · Reading 1

What does tolerance mean to you? What traditions do you have in the UAE that are different from other countries? Activity 1: In the previous lesson we learnt the meaning of social cohesion. Read the words of Sheikh Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan (may God have mercy upon him), then discuss the importance of human beings to communities and cohesion. In the quote in the book, he says that man is the basis of any civilised process and the pivot of every tangible progress, regardless of buildings, schools and hospitals, and that man is the spirit of everything. The book adds that Sheikh Zayed (1918–2004) was the president of the UAE, ruler of Abu Dhabi and founder of the UAE.

Page 48 · Reading 2

Activity 2: Let’s look at the achievements of Sheikh Zayed (may God have mercy upon him) in building social cohesion within the UAE in the face of great diversity. The late Sheikh Zayed recognised the importance of promoting internal harmony and social and national cohesion among the people of his country. He was known for interacting with his people. In the late 1920s and 1930s, Sheikh Zayed spent time among the Bedouin people so that he could understand the hardships of their life. After he became ruler of Abu Dhabi, he acted quickly to establish closer ties with his brothers in the other Emirates. On December 2, 1971, the rulers announced the establishment of the United Arab Emirates, and elected Sheikh Zayed the first president. This position earned him the title “The Founding Father”.

Questions: (a) How do you think the citizens from different tribes felt when the UAE was declared? (b) Explain the vision of Sheikh Zayed and discuss the importance of federation to foster social cohesion within the UAE.

Page 49–50 · Reading 3

Activity 3: Tolerance is an important part of promoting social cohesion and accepting multiculturalism. The United Nations strongly supports tolerance between different cultures and people. This is the centre of the Charter of the United Nations and the Universal Declaration of Human Rights. In 1996, the UN invited member states to observe the International Day for Tolerance on November 16. In the UAE, the Minister of State for Tolerance, Her Excellency Sheikha Lubna Al Qasimi, has stated that the aim of the National Tolerance Programme is an Emirati society that values tolerance and multiculturalism, while rejecting discrimination, hatred and intolerance. The programme values respect for religious and cultural diversity, dialogue and coexistence, integrity and transparency, effective communication, innovation and initiative.

Task: Conduct research on the UAE Charter of Tolerance and Coexistence and Peace. Discuss its aims. Suggest ways in which tolerance can be applied to our behaviour at the level of family, school and youth groups. Talk about an initiative by one individual or group towards another that shows tolerance. Then organise a Day of Tolerance in your school and agree on the activities.

Page 51–52 · Reading 4

Activity 4: Read about the concept of sub-community, which exists within a society. The culture of a society is the customs and traditions adopted by that society, and that culture is the product of all the cultures within each sub-community. Each sub-community consists of neighbourhoods, each neighbourhood of streets, each street of buildings, each building of apartments with families, and each family of individuals with their own direction. So society contains many sub-communities, each with its own values, customs, traditions and trends. This is called sub-culture. Through the culture of the individual, values and traditions are part of the general culture of society.

Tasks: (a) A neighbourhood is part of a sub-community. Carry out a study to identify whether your neighbourhood is made up of people of the same nationality or of several nationalities. Give an example of a culture you have acquired from where you live and describe an occasion when you practise it. (b) In what ways could several sub-cultures impact upon social cohesion? Analyse the pros and cons in the table. (c) Compare the traditions of the UAE with those of some other countries of your choice, focusing on holidays and festivals.

Page 52–55 · Reading 5

Activity 5: How has the social class system in ancient China affected the architecture? The book says Confucius was born in 551 BCE and died in 479 BCE, that he wanted to return society to integrity and honesty and to build a hierarchy of social classes, and that a strict system of rules for architecture followed the hierarchy. Tasks: draw a hierarchical structure for your extended family and for your school, with the duties of each person, and explain the impact of commitment to duties on social cohesion; name three Arab and foreign countries headed by a king and three headed by a president.

Activity 6: A French writer who lives in Abu Dhabi and teaches civilisational and cultural diversity says in the book that the mix of Arabic and foreign cultures and the flexibility between nationalities in the UAE may matter greatly for the convergence of civilisations. What is the importance of social cohesion to preserve Emirati culture despite the interaction and cultural diversity?

Activity 7: Many refugee children suffer difficult conditions after being displaced by conflict. They need psychological support and education. Tasks: research the conditions of refugee children and the roles of UNICEF, UNHCR and UAE NGOs; choose a project that provides educational support; collect donations such as schoolbooks, stationery and school bags; contact relevant NGOs in the UAE; write a report or make a film about your responsibility to others.
