Duties of sons and daughters

The book says children have important duties towards the parents who raised and supported them: respecting their opinions, being kind, listening and never scolding them. Doing chores builds skills and shows appreciation. The story of Zhao shows how a son cared for his father. Ethics and mutual respect are the basis of duty and responsibility.

📖 Book text

Story adapted from Student Book pp. 35–36. Medical correction checked with WHO, Poliomyelitis, 2 April 2025. Polio has no cure; vaccination prevents it. Accept different family-care choices without judgment.

Student Book Vol. 1 · Student Book readings with clearly marked health/safety adaptations.

Page 27–29 · Reading 1

What are children’s duties and responsibilities towards their parents or guardian? What do you do to help your parents or guardian? Activity 1: Children’s responsibilities and duties towards their parents start at home, but do they end there? Discuss this topic in class.

Activity 2: Children have important duties towards the parents who raised them, cared for them, and supported them financially. Parents spend long days and nights taking care of their children, putting their children’s needs even before their own. […] Therefore, it is the duty of children to be kind to their parents and to fulfill their obligations towards them. The most important obligations include: respecting their parents’ opinions and discussing controversial issues with them; being kind to them and never scolding them; listening to them and answering their questions; accepting their requests and trying to make them happy; showing kindness to them even after their death.

Page 29–31 · Reading 2

Think of a time when your parents made an important sacrifice for you. Write a story about something you have done to show your appreciation for your parents. Then present it to the class.

Activity 3: Is it your responsibility to help your parents by doing chores at home? Discuss with your classmates. Then match the skills with the tasks in the table. Tasks: washing the dishes, setting the table, helping with the shopping, keeping track of household spending, helping younger siblings with their homework, preparing desserts, repairing domestic appliances, sorting waste for recycling, making a list of missing items. Skills include: mastering delicate hand movements, understanding scientific concepts, awareness of the cost of goods, switching from one activity to another, developing environmental awareness, practising calculation, understanding household budgeting, helping and benefiting others.

Page 32–34 · Reading 3

Activity 4: Read this extract from the book The Birth of a Historical Leader. The late Sheikh Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan (may God have mercy upon him) grew up under the care of good parents. They raised him on the solid teachings and principles of their tribal surroundings. […] he received his education from a teacher at a small Al-Katateeb school, which taught children to read the Holy Quran and the principles of religion and morality, as well as some basic mathematics. […] He was very close to his father, and he always attended his meetings and discussions with tribal leaders and the people. […] The personality of Sheikh Zayed was significantly impacted by his mother, Sheikha Salama, since he was close to her and influenced by her guidance, which instilled into him love of the people and charity to the poor and needy.

Tasks: (a) List the obligations that Sheikh Zayed fulfilled towards his family during his early life. Did he follow the same principles in dealing with his people? (b) Research the possible reasons family and tribal bonds can become weaker, and suggest how to strengthen them.

Page 35–36 · Reading 4

Activity 5, adapted story: As a child, Zhao had polio. His father cared for him and took him to doctors by bicycle. Later, when Zhao was about to start university, his father became ill and was paralysed. Zhao brought his father to stay with him so that he could care for him, and found a part-time job to support them both. Health correction (WHO): polio can cause permanent paralysis. There is no cure for polio; vaccination can prevent it. The book's description of an eventual cure is not medical guidance. Questions: (a) How did Zhao show care for his father? Families may need different kinds of care and support. (b) Research how the UAE supports older people.

Page 37 · Reading 5

Activity 6: Write a paragraph that explains how ethics and mutual respect are the basis for a sense of duty and responsibility, whether among children, between children and parents, or between students and school. Present this text through a photo supported presentation to your classmates next week.
