CONCEPT CARD

Consequences of not living moderately

A life of excess can bring burn out, illnesses linked to unhealthy eating, problems from a sedentary life and poor sleep, harm from negative thinking and irresponsible choices, and illness from poor personal hygiene. Small changes help.

Excess has consequences; small changes help.

Page 143–145 · Reading 1

What are some healthy and unhealthy diet habits? What do you think you can do to make your lifestyle more active?

Leading a life that is not moderate, but full of excess and a lack of care for others has far-reaching and, in some cases, very dangerous consequences. […] Too much of anything is often not beneficial for your mental and physical health.

There is an increasing pressure in some countries for workers to work harder than ever, work longer hours, and work at the weekends. Working or studying hard are very important in life, but if people don’t have an appropriate balance of personal life and work or study, they can become exhausted and less effective. This is sometimes called ‘burn out.’ Burn out can be the result of stress, and if stress is not managed, it can lead to serious health issues.

Without proper rest, the body starts to weaken and the chances of becoming ill will increase. […] People become less productive and efficient in their working environments, and more negative in their personal lives.

Page 145–147 · Reading 2

If you eat unhealthy food, you will become unhealthy. […] It is not just about the kind of food that you are eating, but it is the amount as well. […] Eating healthily is not just about eating the right balance of foods. It is also about not eating certain types of food to excess.

Saturated fats are high in calories. They are found in cream, cakes, processed meats, cheeses, butter, and whole milk. Health experts note that too much saturated fat can lead to increased cholesterol and increases the risks of heart disease and strokes. […] Sugar can be present in cereals, sweets and chocolate, soft drinks, ketchup and in fruit. Reducing the amount of sugar in your diet can contribute to a healthier diet. For example, think about drinking water, rather than cans of soft drinks, and cutting back on chocolate.

A diet that is high in sugar, fat or salt can lead to chronic illnesses developing, such as diabetes or heart disease. […] once your body mass index (BMI) reaches 30 or above you are officially classed as obese. This increases the likelihood of other health conditions such as high blood pressure and certain cancers. The World Health Organisation’s 2014 report on diabetes estimates that there are 422 million adults living with diabetes. This is linked to being overweight or obese.

Page 147–148 · Reading 3

Mental health suffers as a result of a poor diet because brain cells need energy, something an unhealthy diet does not provide. This results in being unable to remember things, headaches and an inability to focus clearly. Many studies have shown that eating unhealthy food affects how we feel, and we are more likely to feel stressed and suffer from depression.

It is important that children develop healthy eating habits to maintain a healthy body weight. If these habits are not developed when children are young, then they are less likely to lead happy, healthy and successful lives.

Thinking Task: Generally, people who eat a lot of unhealthy food know that it is not good for them, but they eat it anyway. Why do you think this happens?

Action Task: Make notes in the table about everything you eat and drink in one week. At the end of the week, which side of the table has more entries? Share your findings with your group.

Page 148–150 · Reading 4

The term ‘sedentary’ means being inactive and not moving. It is the opposite of a healthy, active lifestyle. People who have a sedentary lifestyle do little or no physical activity and are often sitting or lying down and being inactive for most of the day. […] The increases in technology over the last 30 years have tended to encourage many more people to have a sedentary lifestyle.

As humans, we are designed to move […]. Sedentary behaviour, such as watching television or playing computer games, can increase anxiety. […] If we are inactive, the mind will follow this by becoming more forgetful and less focused, because there is less blood being pumped throughout our body and the brain receives less oxygen. Being inactive can also affect sleep patterns.

Getting an appropriate amount of sleep is important in maintaining overall health. Health experts note that proper sleep is important in helping the body repair itself. A lack of proper sleep can be associated with heart and kidney diseases, as well as high blood pressure, diabetes and strokes. A lack of sleep is also a factor in many accidents, including road accidents.

Action Task (p. 150): in four categories, school, home, travel and work, write two ideas each for small changes that reduce sitting or lying down.

Page 151–154 · Reading 5

Negative thoughts focus on the idea that the worst case scenario will happen in any situation. This can have a serious health impact because it can develop into depression, stress and anxiety. This way of thinking works against you, makes you feel worse, and reduces your self-confidence. […] Remember that you create your own thoughts, and therefore have control about what and how you think.

Action Task (p. 152): for five situations, such as giving a presentation, falling out with a friend who wants you to do something wrong, or getting a bad mark, write what a negative thinker and a positive thinker might say.

Being irresponsible means that you do not take time to think about the possible results of what you do. […] Examples of irresponsible behaviour might include being late for lessons, wasting time, distracting other students from their work or using other students’ possessions without asking first. Using inappropriate language, bullying and disrespecting the school’s equipment are also examples of irresponsible behaviour.

[…] some teenagers make poor choices when they feel stressed, want attention or are trying to impress their friends. […] Becoming involved with crime, experimenting with drugs and dropping out of school are all situations where people make poor choices, and have not considered the impact that these actions will have on themselves, their family and the wider community.

“[…] people do not hear your words as deeply as they feel your actions.” His Highness Sheikh Mohammed bin Rashid Al Maktoum (May Allah protect him)

Page 154–157 · Reading 6

If you do not have daily routines that help clean all your body, the consequences can range from problems with your family and friends, to more serious health issues. […] If a person doesn’t brush and floss their teeth, then they can develop bad breath, and gum disease. This disease attacks the teeth, and they start to decay and fall out.

Washing your hands regularly helps prevent the spread of bacteria and viruses. Every day, people touch all sorts of things such as door handles, cups and laptops. Without thinking, they then touch their face. Bacteria and viruses can be easily transferred and spread very quickly. If you don’t wash your hands regularly, then you are increasing the risk of catching the flu, or a cold, or a much more serious disease like some types of hepatitis.

Self-Assessment Task: (1) What is the difference between working hard and ‘burn out’? (2) What happens to your physical and mental wellbeing if you eat too much unhealthy food? (3) What are the consequences of a sedentary life? (4) How can negative thinking affect you? (5) What are possible outcomes if you are irresponsible and make poor decisions? (6) Why is personal hygiene important?
