CONCEPT CARD

Economy and globalisation

Globalisation is the increase in the connections between economies. An export is sold to a foreign customer and brings currency in; an import is bought from abroad and sends currency out. An economy is all the production and exchange in a place over a period. Trade agreements, such as the GCC customs union and bilateral agreements, reduce barriers to trade.

Globalisation connects economies through trade.

Page 10–12 · Reading 2

What the tourists are describing is one aspect of globalisation. Globalisation refers to the increase in the connections between economies around the world. These connections are increased by agreements made between governments which cover trade, the movement of money across countries and agreements about the movement of people across countries.

When a company sells goods and services to customers in another country, these goods and services are called exports. An export is the sale of a good or service to a foreign customer for which the domestic company receives an inflow of currency in payment for the goods and services.

Goods and services bought by domestic companies for use or sale in the country are called imports. An import is the purchase of a good or service from a foreign country for which a payment is made and leads to funds flowing out of the domestic economy.

Doing business with companies from different countries involves several processes. In the first instance, contact must be made between businesses and this might take place at a trade convention […]. Both businesses will need to build trust between them before eventually placing an order. This will require an invoice to be raised detailing the goods or services provided. Both companies’ banks must be involved as the payments between the two companies must be processed and this will often involve the need to change currency. […] Once the order is placed, the company which is producing the goods must arrange for the products to be produced to the specifications required and start to arrange the shipping. The products may be shipped by air, sea or land.

Discussion Point: Why do governments impose taxes on imported goods? What effect do import taxes have on trade between countries?

An economy can refer to a relatively small area, such as the economy of a village, or a larger area such as a town or city, a municipality, a country, a region, a continent and globally. What is common to each is that an economy is made up of people. […] This process of exchange is at the heart of what we mean by the word economy. The economy is all the production and exchange activity that takes place over a period of time in a particular place.

The textbook uses historical estimates: UAE production of about US$349 billion in 2016 and a global figure of about US$74 trillion. These are not current GDP values. Keep the year, currency and source with any comparison; do not mix totals from different years.

The amount received by a business for selling its goods and services is called its revenue. If a hotel business in the UAE sells 100 rooms per night to guests, at an average price of 500 AED per room, its revenue will be 50 000 AED per night.

Summary of the headings (our words). The barriers to globalisation: legal requirements (for example, toy safety standards), regulations (for example, cars built to drive on the left or right), laws relating to people (visas, employment taxes, working hours, contracts), and subcontracting and outsourcing (for example, brands that use contractors to make shoes).

Page 25–27 · Reading 6

A trade agreement is usually designed to allow goods, services, money and people to move freely across borders. Trade agreements may also reduce the paperwork needed, and in many cases remove taxes and duties on goods moving between countries.

The UAE is part of the GCC. The GCC is described as a customs union which means that the members of the GCC agree that goods traded between members are free of any duties or taxes, but any trade from outside the GCC is subject to a tax called a common external tariff (tax).

The UAE also has free trade agreements with individual countries. These are called bilateral trade agreements because they are an agreement between two countries. […] They are also a sign of the desire of the two countries to establish friendly relations and closer political ties.
