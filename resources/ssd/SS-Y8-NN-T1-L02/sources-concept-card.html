CONCEPT CARD

The role of communities

Social identity is how a person sees himself from the groups he belongs to. Families and groups pass on customs and educate children, and voluntary associations such as the Special Olympics help people learn and grow. The book also shows that some goals, such as sharing water or cleaning the land, need group work.

Why is having a social identity important? What role does education play in community? What values and duties are shared by your community? Activity 1: Who am I? Fill in the card and then tell your classmates your answers to the last four elements: my name, my gender, my age, my family, my school, the emirate where I live, my city, village or neighbourhood, my favourite hobbies, my nationality.

Page 20 · Reading 2

Activity 2: Read this text about social identity. Kelly Hannum is a researcher and assistant in the Global Leadership and Diversity Project. In her book “Social Identity: Knowing Yourself, Leading Others” (2009), she said that social identity includes personal elements from the groups that a person belongs to, whether the groups are related to age, gender, nationality, race, tribe, religion or social and economic status. Your social identity can determine the way you deal with others. Also, the social identity of the people around you affects how they think of you. So, social identity can be thought of as the individual’s view of his or her identity. This starts from the groups that he or she is a member of.

Express your opinion about the following statement: “Social identity is always a source of pride and honour.”

Page 21–22 · Reading 3

Activity 3: The family is a society that influences the child’s education and refines the child’s personality, from the book “A Cup of Coffee: The Emirates in the Memory of its Children, Part 3”. We highlight here the ways in which parents take care of their children, starting from direct supervision and taking children to groups, to paying careful attention to their behaviour and their movements outside the home. Since early childhood, children inherit customs, traditions, concepts, arts, and experiences. For example, some children make a game of making small sailing boats, using cuttings of metal sheets or of palm trees. These children go to the seaside in all emirates, especially in the summer, organising races for their boats and playing traditional sea games inspired by their ancestors. […] The Omani oasis of Khasab was visited in the summer months by many mothers and children, especially from Dubai and Abu Dhabi, while the men were away on seasonal diving trips.

Questions: (a) Fill the table with old-fashioned and newer games. (b) How do you organise your time while playing electronic games? What would happen if you did not belong to any digital community? Explain your answer. (c) On the map, show the location of Khasab.

Page 22–24 · Reading 4

Activity 4: The role of education does not depend only on the family and school, especially in cases of people of determination. Read the summary of Loretta Claiborne’s life story and an overview of Timothy Shriver’s book on the Special Olympics to discover the role that voluntary associations can play in education. Loretta Claiborne was born in 1953 in Pennsylvania, into a poor family of seven children raised by a single mother. She was born partially blind and had an intellectual disability. As a child, she endured bullying because of her differences, and by often having to run away from her bullies, she discovered the pleasure of running. She was introduced to the Special Olympics, which were started by Ms. Eunice Kennedy Shriver. The organisation started “Camp Shriver” for young people with intellectual disabilities to integrate with athletes without them. Claiborne crossed the finish line in 26 marathons, achieved a fourth-level black belt in karate, learned four languages and received two honorary doctorates.

Task: Write about three UAE athletes who participated in the Special Olympics.

Page 24–27 · Reading 5

Activity 5: No matter how great the individual’s achievements are in his or her society, some goals can be achieved only through group work. Text 1, Water is the Bedouin Dream: Sheikh Zayed bin Sultan (may God have mercy upon him) recognised the importance of agriculture for the city of Al Ain. The scarcity of water was a big problem and he decided to give up the share of the ruling family of the falaj water, so that water could be available for all and for free. He gathered the tribesmen and asked them to help him dig new expansions of the falaj system, and people joined in, and water became freely available for everyone. Text 2, A Campaign to Clean the Land: in February 2012, crowds arrived at Al Khatem from all over Abu Dhabi in response to a call by His Highness Sheikh Abdullah bin Zayed Al Nahyan, to show their belief in the importance of volunteering and their commitment to community participation, and to keep the environment clean.

Questions: (a) Analyse and compare the values and the motives that led the residents of Al Ain to help Sheikh Zayed and those that prompted people to participate in a cleaning campaign. (b) Research volunteering fields in the UAE, identify the field that fits your interests, and design a voluntary campaign. Activity 6: Choose a group that you belong to and write a ten-line paragraph about the reasons why you belong to it, the rules you comply with, your roles and duties, and the role the group plays for you and society.
