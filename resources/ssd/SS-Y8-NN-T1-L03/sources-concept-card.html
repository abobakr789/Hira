Page 28–29 · Reading 1

What role has the sea played in the development of the UAE? What are the required qualities of a responsible and reliable Emirati citizen? Activity 1: Proverbs reflect the culture of the people. What cultural aspects of Hawaii and the UAE do the proverbs in the book reflect? The book gives one proverb from the UAE, about a person who wants to be at the front of the ship, and one from Hawaii, about catching shrimps.

Page 30 · Reading 2

Activity 2: Read about the marine environment in the UAE and Africa. The sea has played an active and vital role in the history and development of the UAE. It has also had a significant social, cultural and economic influence. Everyone in the UAE who lived along the coast was involved in marine activities, whether it was diving, pearling, fishing or sailing to neighbouring countries for trade. Since ancient times, the people of the UAE have had close ties to the sea, which has given them a deep attachment to coastal life.

Ali bin Matar Al-Shamsi, an 80-year-old sea captain, says that Emiratis have a strong relationship with the sea, with historical and cultural dimensions. He says this connection has produced many social, cultural and creative traditions, customs and ceremonies. He adds that maritime professions need experience, bravery and patience.

Page 30–31 · Reading 3

The Role of the Sea in African Countries: for coastal populations across West Africa, the ocean is a way of life. It is also a major source of income and nutrition, especially for the poor. In countries such as Mauritania, Senegal, Guinea, Guinea-Bissau, Ghana, Liberia and Sierra Leone, the Atlantic Ocean provides a huge habitat for many different types of sea life. A World Bank report dated 5 June 2013 estimated that fisheries earned these countries $4.9 billion a year. Jamal Saghir, then World Bank Director of Sustainable Development for Africa, said fisheries supported about 10 million livelihoods in Africa and could generate at least $2 billion more annually with better governance. These are historical estimates, not current totals.

Tasks: (a) Conduct research on the lifestyles of people in the coastal areas of the UAE and Africa. Focus on the characteristics of each coast, the practices of each community and the challenges they face. Then compare and contrast. (b) Fill in the table with details of marine employment in the UAE and Africa.

Page 32 · Reading 4

Activity 3: Read about the Document of Conduct and Ethics of the Emirati Citizen. It was approved by the Cabinet in Union House on 27 November 2012. The document aims at bringing up a new Emirati generation that is aware of its responsibilities and duties towards its nation, family and community. It includes the most important characteristics, manners, values and skills which Emirati citizens should be distinguished with. The book describes a citizen who is loyal to the nation, observes laws and seeks to achieve its advancement and prosperity; who seeks to provide an honourable life to his family; and who is enriched with good ethics.

Tasks: (a) Create a short story or a feature film about the basic Emirati values that are passed down from one generation to another. (b) Conduct research on the unique values and behaviours of Chinese people. Compare your findings with the predominant values and behaviours of Emirati people.

Page 33–36 · Reading 5

Activity 4: Read the historical “UAE Vision 2021” framework, which described the qualities of a responsible and reliable Emirati citizen. Emiratis have the confidence and sense of responsibility to shape their future. A successful Emirati should be aware of his responsibilities towards others and share his successes with other members of society. Questions: (a) Why should people begin with themselves in practising values and being responsible? What value would you like to practise? (b) What responsibility do you generally take on when your family is away or during special occasions?

Activity 5: Read about the Dubai Forum, which paid special attention to global citizenship. It focused on the theme “How to make real global citizens”. Mr Sunny Varkey said education should help children from every country, culture and faith learn that there is more that unites than divides. The UNESCO table in the book names cognitive, emotional and social, and behavioural skills of a global citizen. Tasks: (a) Define the role of the UAE in enforcing the UNESCO initiative for Global Citizenship Education. (b) Develop a draft project at your school that promotes global citizenship.
