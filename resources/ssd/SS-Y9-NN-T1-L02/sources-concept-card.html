📖 Book text

Student Book Vol. 1 · Original text from the Student Book.

Page 22 · Reading 1

In what ways are the UAE and Japan working together to improve their countries? What role does the European Union play in the world? Activity 1: Look at the logos and organisations in the book: the Gulf Cooperation Council, the United Nations, the European Union and the Arab League. Which of these organisations does the UAE belong to?

Page 23–24 · Reading 2

Activity 2: Read the statements of Sheikh Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan, may God have mercy upon him, and President Jimmy Carter about international relations. The book says the UAE’s foreign policy strengthens its relations with all states according to the principles of equality, justice, mutual respect and love of good for all without exception. It says the UAE supported the issues of rights and justice in the UN and other institutions, which enabled it to take an important role in building relations based on peace and stability.

The book gives Sheikh Zayed’s words about a moderate foreign policy that advocates truth and justice, uses dialogue and understanding, respects international law and the Charter of the United Nations, respects good neighbourliness, sovereignty and territorial integrity, does not interfere in the internal affairs of other nations, and tries to resolve disputes peacefully. It adds that in 1978, at the White House, President Jimmy Carter said that human rights are the essence of America’s foreign policy.

Questions: (1) Compare Sheikh Zayed’s words with what President Carter said about foreign policy. (2) In your group, discuss the rights that you could claim as a member of the UN. Check your information online and support your answer with examples.

Page 25–26 · Reading 3

Activity 3: Read about the cooperation between Japan and the UAE as an example of collaboration between countries. The book says the Masdar Institute of Science and Technology and the University of Tokyo signed an agreement for academic and research collaboration, including exchange of researchers, joint research and seminars, and exchange of publications and materials. In 2014 the Masdar Institute hosted three PhD students from the University of Tokyo.

The agreement follows other projects, including a summer training programme with the Japan International Cooperation Center and research agreements with Mitsubishi Heavy Industries. The book says the Institute supports Masdar’s vision of helping the UAE and Abu Dhabi become a knowledge-based economy and find solutions to challenges such as climate change. The training programme includes introductory courses on Japanese culture and language, and many Japanese companies take part.

Questions: (1) If you had the chance, would you be interested in a two-month training programme in Japan? Give reasons. (2) Suggest four points on which to compare education in the UAE before and after technology in the classroom, and say how students benefit.

Page 26–28 · Reading 4

Activity 4: What is the nature of EU foreign policy? The book says the EU is a key player on the global scene, with security interests and responsibilities at regional and global levels, and that through its common foreign policy it speaks in a unified voice on the main international issues. It says the EU plays a key role in world peace operations, with soldiers, police officers and judges who help stabilise conflict-stricken regions and provide emergency relief after disasters.

The book says that on 1 December 2009 the European Council appointed Catherine Ashton as the highest representative for Foreign Affairs and Security Policy, a role she held until 2014. The goals of the common foreign policy include preserving the EU’s values, interests and independence; promoting democracy, the rule of law and human rights; maintaining peace and strengthening international security; and helping people facing natural and man-made disasters.

Task: In groups, choose an example of an international union in the world and list the benefits of this union and its impact on the foreign relations of countries. Then present your research to your classmates.

Page 28–30 · Reading 5

Activity 5: Read about customs agreements between two countries. The book says bilateral agreements on customs and technical and administrative cooperation are among the core elements of the Federal Customs Authority’s work. The Authority is the official federal customs authority and is a member of the World Customs Organisation and the World Trade Organisation.

The book gives reasons these agreements matter: they reduce customs violations that harm the economic, social and cultural interests of two countries; they help calculate and collect customs duties correctly; they help enforce actions against violations; they help stop illegal trafficking in narcotic drugs, which threatens public health and society; and they encourage trade and the exchange of experience between customs administrations. The book says the Authority’s motto is “Towards a secure society and fair trade”, and that it seeks to protect the community from counterfeit goods and to make trade easier.

Questions: (1) What is the role of customs in the economic cycle? (2) Conduct research with a partner about four countries that have a trade agreement with the UAE. What are the most important exports and imports with these countries?
