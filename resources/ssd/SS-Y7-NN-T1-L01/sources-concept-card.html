CONCEPT CARD

Rights and duties

A community, small like a family or large like a country, needs laws. A constitution is a set of rules and laws that confirms people’s rights and responsibilities. At school, students have the right to dignity and to express their thoughts, and the duty to attend, look after school property and respect others. In Japan, students also help clean their school.

Rights and duties go together.

Page 9–10 · Reading 1

What are your rights at school? What are your duties at school? What responsibilities and duties do students have in Japan? Activity 1: Look at the pictures. In your opinion, what are students’ rights and duties at school?

Activity 2: Laws are needed to keep order in communities. The community might be small, like a family, or large, like a country. The laws that govern a community usually originate from customs, traditions and general moral principles. There are also social agreements, known as a constitution. A constitution is a set of rules and laws. It confirms the rights of individuals, and their responsibilities within the community.

Page 10 · Reading 2

A school is a small community. Therefore, every school should have rules. These rules are usually created by the Ministry of Education or the school administration. These rules govern the relationships among students, and between students, teachers, and administration. In class, it is the students’ responsibility to have good behaviour towards their peers and their teacher. This helps create a friendly, familiar atmosphere within the classroom. These responsibilities help students to become responsible citizens and productive individuals.

Questions: (a) Write three examples about the relationship between the school and the students. (b) What are the duties and responsibilities that make you a responsible and productive citizen?

Page 11–12 · Reading 3

Access to education is an important part of a developed country. […] Similar to countries, academic institutions give their students basic rights and ask them to do general school-related duties. Rights that students benefit from in academic institutions include: the preservation of dignity; freedom from insults or discrimination based on their religion, gender, race or family background; participation in the educational process; freedom to express their thoughts and feelings; access to educational materials that meet quality standards; and freedom to ask about certain details that they do not fully understand during classes. Duties that students have include: committing to daily attendance during school hours; providing an explanation when absent; knowing which lessons they missed and making up for them; and committing to upholding general morals at school with teachers and classmates alike. Also, students must look after school property and the tools used by the class.

Questions: Work with your classmate to explain why you follow the moral ethics in the community. From your experience, describe a duty that your school has assigned you. Did this help clarify the concepts of rights and duties for you?

Page 13 · Reading 4

Through its educational vision and mission, the UAE Ministry of Education seeks to build and manage an innovative educational system in order to create a knowledge community with global competitiveness. With this in mind, it set a strategic plan for 2017–2021. This is a historical plan, not a current plan. Strategic goals include: ensure inclusive quality education including pre-school education; achieve excellent leadership and educational efficiency; ensure safe, conducive and challenging learning environments; strengthen the capacity for scientific research and innovation; establish a culture of innovation in an institutional working environment.

Tasks: Write about the rights and responsibilities that you have to follow as a citizen of the UAE. Write a report on the elements of the safe environment in your school. Suggest some improvements to create a safer environment.

Page 14–16 · Reading 5

Activity 5: The Japanese education system focuses on developing students’ sense of community and responsibility towards society. This starts with their school environment, such as looking after school facilities, teaching materials, school furniture and so on. Japan is known for its clean schools. […] It is also common in Japanese schools for students to sweep the floors of the classrooms and clean them at the end of the school day. […] Teachers often join them to work on achieving general cleanliness. Nobody looks down on students or teachers when they undertake this work. […] At the end of the school day, students gather and check whether they have fully accomplished the day’s tasks.

Tasks: (a) Identify the importance of school for students and teachers in Japan. Justify your answer using examples from the text. (b) Present what students do at the end of the school day and why you think it is important. (c) Organise a “Cleanliness at School Week” activity and create rules that apply to all students. Activity 6: Determine the responsibilities and duties of the security staff, the nurse, the headmaster and the bus driver at your school.
