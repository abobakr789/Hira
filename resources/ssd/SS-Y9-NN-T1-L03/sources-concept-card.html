Global ethical challenges

📖 Book text

Student Book Vol. 1 · Original text from the Student Book.

Page 31–32 · Reading 1

What are the main challenges we face in the world today? Why is it important to talk about climate change? Activity 1: What are the challenges for global communities in the 21st century? Look at the pictures and read the quotes in the book. How does each one make you feel? What do you think is the lesson or message? The book gives quotations from Ban Ki-moon, Secretary-General of the United Nations from 2007 to 2016, who says that climate change does not respect borders or who you are, so it needs global solidarity; from Benjamin Franklin, who says that we know the worth of water when the well is dry; and from the painter Pablo Picasso.

Page 33–34 · Reading 2

Activity 2: Environmental problems, and global warming in particular, are among the global challenges that Ban Ki-moon talked about. Read the text about polar bears feeding on dolphins in the North Pole. The book says that during research in the Svalbard archipelago in Norway in April 2014, a scientist from the Norwegian Polar Institute monitored and photographed a bear feeding on white-beaked dolphins. Polar bears usually feed mainly on seals. The scientist explained that these dolphins are part of the diet because, as the waters warm, new species are finding their way north.

The book says there were no earlier records of these dolphins there in winter, and that the significant reduction of sea ice could be the reason they came. As ice forms, dolphins can be trapped under it with only small openings to breathe. The scientist noted that a hungry old male bear most likely hunted them, and that five further cases were later reported. He said he did not think this signified a great change in the diet, but that bears are meeting species they had not met before.

Task: The North Pole is far from the UAE. Are you supposed to be interested in this issue? Conduct thorough research into the effects of the reduction of sea ice on the ecosystem. Then write a dialogue with a classmate who disagrees with you and present it to the class.

Page 34–35 · Reading 3

Activity 3: Why is climate change considered an ethical dilemma at the level of international relations? The book says climate change causes ethical challenges in the international community for three reasons. First, it is a global issue that matters to every nation: once greenhouse gases spread through the air, their impact on any place cannot be prevented. The book calls this a “Prisoner’s Dilemma”: all countries together want to reduce emissions, but each country alone prefers to keep emitting. Those affected are not equally affected: most of the severely affected countries and peoples are those that emitted the least.

Second, current emissions have a trans-generational impact. Carbon dioxide stays in the air for a very long time, so the present generation is responsible for preventing future harm, because it is unfair to ask future generations to bear the burden. It is also unfair to ask countries that have not contributed to emissions not to use economic activities that benefit them. Third, there are theoretical tools to help decide problems in international justice, trans-generational ethics and environmental justice.

Questions: (1) With your group, suggest actions that you and your classmates could do at home and at school to limit global warming. (2) Conduct research with your group about the most notable convention that the international community has established to limit the factors causing climate change. Identify one or more obstacles, which match the reasons in the text, to the signing of this convention.

Page 36–37 · Reading 4

Activity 4: The book says the reduction of freshwater in the Middle East and North Africa is a grave danger to economic growth, social cohesion, peace and political stability, and that it has been getting worse because of rapid climate change. The Middle East, and North Africa in particular, are the most vulnerable because water availability per capita is much lower than the normal range. The book says some countries of the Arabian Peninsula have almost no freshwater resources, but they do not depend on agriculture. It says rainfall is low and varies a lot by place and time.

Climate change affects water supplies differently across regions. Higher temperatures can increase evaporation and drought risk. Rising sea levels can worsen saltwater intrusion into coastal groundwater. IPCC AR6 gives scenario-dependent global mean sea-level projections for 2100, relative to 1995–2014: likely 0.28–0.55 m under very low emissions (SSP1-1.9), and 0.63–1.01 m under very high emissions (SSP5-8.5). These are projections, not a single forecast for every coast.

Homework: With teacher approval and adult supervision, demonstrate a safe, simple filtration method. Never drink the filtered water; filtering alone does not make water safe to drink.

Page 38–39 · Reading 5

Activity 5: Read the text on water desalination and its economic and environmental outcomes. The book says that turning seawater into freshwater is a lifeline for countries with limited freshwater, and that in the UAE almost all drinking water is desalinated. It says desalination needs a lot of energy and is not cost-effective, especially when oil and gas prices rise. The book says the Gulf region produces about 50% of the world’s desalinated water, that desalination costs are expected to increase, and that the salinity of the water returned to the sea is twice the basic salinity, which harms marine life.

The book says Masdar launched a pilot programme to test highly efficient desalination using renewable energy, with the aim of a commercially operational plant by 2020. The programme aims to reduce energy use by combining advanced desalination with renewable energy.

Task: What is the ethical problem resulting from desalination? Explain this using the definition of “ethical problem”. Express your answer by performing an acting scene in groups of three: one student plays a state that desalinates water, the second plays an activist in an environmental society, and the third plays a scientist in modern techniques for seawater desalination using renewable energy.
