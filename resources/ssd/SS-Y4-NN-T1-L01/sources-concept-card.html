CONCEPT CARD

Showing respect to others

Self-respect means respecting yourself. Respecting others means treating them politely and following rules. Respectful behaviour includes greeting politely, knocking before entering, being calm in an argument, and studying hard. When we respect ourselves, others respect us too.

When I respect myself, others respect me, and I respect them.

Words to know

self-respectfeeling that you are worth respect and acting in a way that shows itruleswhat we must or must not do
respecting otherstreating other people politely and kindlyvolunteerto offer to do something without being asked

Page 11–12 · Activity 2

Nemo and his classmates were waiting for the teacher to arrive in class. When the teacher arrived, the students stood up and greeted him politely. The teacher greeted the students in return and asked them to sit down again. Then the teacher noticed that Majid was not there, so he asked about him.

Michael raised his hand. He explained that Majid was sent to the headmaster’s office. He had to explain his behaviour towards another student in a different class. Majid and the other boy had been fighting earlier that morning. Michael explained that the other boy had put his bag in the corridor. Majid had moved it with his foot, leaving it in the middle of the corridor. Then some other students kicked the bag while they were passing. That was why Majid and the other boy started to fight. A teacher separated the boys and took them to the headmaster’s office. Majid was on his last chance because he had caused trouble lots of times before. So he would probably be expelled from the school. That would be a serious blow to him.

While the class was watching a film about life and language in New York City, Majid opened the door. He didn’t knock. He went directly to his seat next to Nemo. The students were surprised to see such rudeness in the classroom. The teacher said, ‘Let’s greet Majid. Since you didn’t respect the rules of entering the classroom, I must take action. classmates will volunteer to accompany you for a week, to help you to learn how to respect yourself and others, I’ll forgive you. I’ll also speak to the headmaster and make sure that you aren’t expelled. However, if nobody volunteers, you’ll have to leave the classroom immediately.’ Confused, Majid looked to his friends, waiting for someone to volunteer. Everyone remained quiet. It was clear that no other student wanted to spend time with him. Majid’s eyes moved from boy to boy, and then focused on Nemo, who was sitting next to him. He looked straight at him, silently asking for help. Nemo raised his hand and said to the teacher, ‘Sir, I’ll accompany my friend Majid throughout the week. I’m sure he will behave respectfully and not repeat his past behaviour.’ As the class ended, Nemo wondered how he was going to help his friend to learn to follow the rules and improve his behaviour.

If you were Nemo, which behaviours would you try to encourage Majid to change? What behaviours would you help Majid to adopt to prove to the teacher that he respects himself and others? Write sentences to answer the questions.

Work in groups. Read the two texts and identify examples of self-respect and respect for others.

Text 1. At the beginning of her visit to the UAE, Queen Elizabeth II toured the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque in Abu Dhabi. When the Queen entered the mosque’s courtyard, she took off her shoes and wore a cloak-like robe and a full-head cover designed specially for her visit.

Text 2. Al Sanaa is a set of customs and traditions, including general rules and ethics. It’s about dealing with others. Respecting the feelings of others. Finding the best way of communicating with people in order to improve social values. Al Sanaa is also about protecting cultural and social heritage. It tries to teach children to be good citizens, regardless of the nature, technologies and requirements of their age.
