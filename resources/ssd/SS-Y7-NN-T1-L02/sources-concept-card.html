Page 17–18 · Reading 1

What rights and responsibilities do members of a family have? What is the “Wadeema” Child Protection Law? What different types of families are there? Activity 1: Identify some of the qualities of an ideal father from the words of the late Sheikh Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan (may God have mercy upon him): “A real leader is one who takes care of, observes, follows up with, and asks about his people as if they are his family members.”

Activity 2: The family is the pillar of society. The way family members are raised is essential to building a generation capable of successfully withstanding all the difficulties of life. Therefore, parents should be keen on taking care of their children and promoting their self-confidence and the ability to assume responsibility. […] Parents should also guide their children to respect the common social customs and other people’s rights, abide by moral values and respect our customs and traditions, all for the good of the family and society equally.

Page 19–21 · Reading 2

The UAE acceded to the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child on 3 January 1997. The Convention was adopted on 20 November 1989. Some important articles: Article 24: You have the right to the best health care possible, safe water to drink, nutritious food, a clean and safe environment, and information to help you stay well. Article 28: You have the right to a good quality education. Article 12: You have the right to give your opinion, and for adults to listen and take it seriously. Article 13: You have the right to find out things and share what you think with others, unless it harms or offends other people. Article 39: You have the right to help if you’ve been hurt, neglected, or badly treated. Article 42: You have the right to know your rights! Article 2: All children have these rights, no matter who they are.

Tasks: (a) Identify the parties responsible for meeting children’s rights and write them in the second column. (b) Select the most appropriate responsibility for each item (meeting essential needs, moral support, education). (c) Discuss the goals of UAE organisations concerned with the Child Protection Law and the reasons for its inception.

Page 22–23 · Reading 3

How does the UAE protect children? What is the “Wadeema” Child Protection Law? UAE society welcomed the State’s efforts to provide full protection for children, through the declaration of the “Wadeema” Child Protection Law. It was named in memory of an eight-year-old girl called Wadeema, who died in 2012 in a crime that shook UAE society. […] The late Sheikh Khalifa bin Zayed Al Nahyan declared this Federal Law on 8 March 2016 and it entered into force on 15 June 2016. It highlights the UAE’s role in preserving children’s rights to live, survive and develop. It also protects the child from negligence, exploitation, and abuse. […] the law uses the term “best interest” in taking measures to protect the child.

Tasks: (a) Analyse the meaning of “best interest”, and clarify the services provided under this term. (b) Name some family activities that make you feel safe.

Page 24 · Reading 4

Activity 5: Read the following text by a woman about her late father. As well as being a loyal patriot until his last day, my father was a great parent. He was caring, loving and good-hearted. He loved his family greatly and looked at boys and girls equally. He used to gather us and discuss different life-related topics with us. He often took our opinions […] encouraged us to abide by the basics of morality, such as honesty and loyalty, and raised us on the values of love for the family, the country and loyalty at work. […] He was a great teacher, a great role model and will always be alive in our hearts.

Question: Consider the father’s behaviour in this story. How does it compare with what Sheikh Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan (may God have mercy upon him) did as an ideal father?

Page 25–26 · Reading 5

Activity 6: A family is a group of individuals related to each other by kinship and blood. Families come in different forms, including: the nuclear family, which includes the parents and their children (if they have any); the extended family, which includes grandparents, uncles and aunts, and can extend to three generations; the single-parent family; and the joint family. For generations, families have drawn trees that represent their members and illustrate their origins and kinship. The family tree goes from top to bottom, from the oldest to the youngest.

Tasks: (a) Look at the family tree example and then draw your own family tree. (b) Compare the characteristics of life within a nuclear family and an extended family.
