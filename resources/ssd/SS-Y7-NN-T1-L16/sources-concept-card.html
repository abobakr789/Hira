📖 Book text

Student Book Vol. 1 · Original text from the Student Book.

Page 183–185 · Reading 1

What do you know about Korea? What was Korea like during the Joseon period (1392 CE to 1910 CE)? What does “Renaissance” mean? Activity 1: The word Renaissance means “rebirth” or “reawakening.” It is a time when economies flourish, and cultures develop. Many different Renaissance periods have occurred throughout history. Korea went through a Renaissance period from about 1400 to 1900 CE. Artwork from the period shows how the culture was developing. A painting entitled Donggwoldo was painted around the year 1826 to 1831 during the reign of King Sunjo. The painting shows the two-palace complex near present-day Seoul: Changdeokgung Palace and Changgyeonggung Palace, located on the east side of the main Gyeongbokgung Palace. The painting shows the buildings from a bird’s eye view. It fits on 16 folded screens that extend to 576 cm wide and 273 cm long.

Page 183–185 · Reading 2

Questions: (a) What is a renaissance? (b) When did the renaissance in Korea happen? (c) What is Donggwoldo? (d) What does it show?

Page 186–187 · Reading 3

Activity 2: The Donggwoldo painting shows the palaces in Korea. Examine the painting, then answer the questions. (a) Describe the organization of the city. (What shape is it? Where are the houses? What else can you see?) (b) Circle the two palaces. (Clue: one is red and green.) (c) What do you see in the picture around the edges of the city? Why is it a benefit to the city?

Page 187–188 · Reading 4

Activity 3: Examine another view of the Donggwoldo painting and answer the questions. (a) What do you notice about the palace areas? (b) What natural formations are surrounding the buildings? (c) How do the natural formations help to protect the society? (d) What do you notice about how the city is organized? (streets, trees, structures)

Page 189–190 · Reading 5

Activity 4: People were concerned about public health at the time and discussed ways to improve it. One of the most important resources for any civilization is water. An excerpt from an editorial in the Korean newspaper The Independent (Tongnip sinmun), 30 April 1896, reflects those concerns. The writer says water is of more value than railroads, because instead of saving money it saves life, and suggests you can estimate the level of civilization of a people by the amount of water they use, comparing several countries.

Questions: (a) When was the newspaper article published? How many years ago was that? (b) What is water being compared to? Why? How does this show the importance of water? (c) What was the author implying about the relationship between water consumption and the level of development? How is that different from our modern perspective? (d) What does the reading suggest about the responsibility of good government to provide sanitation and promote public health for citizens?
