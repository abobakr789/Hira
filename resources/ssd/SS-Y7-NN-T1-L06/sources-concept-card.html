CONCEPT CARD

Risks and consequences

A risk is the chance that something bad happens, and a consequence is the result of an action. Hazards can be found at home and in the community, such as plastic waste in the environment. We can keep ourselves and others safe by spotting hazards, making careful choices and asking for help.

Stay safe, help others.

Unit questions: What is a risk? What are the consequences of taking risks? What actions should you take when you are at risk? How can you protect yourself from risk? Lesson questions: What is a risk? What is a consequence? How can you keep yourself safe?

Activity 1: Write a sentence that explains what (a) a risk and (b) a consequence are. You may use the word bank to help you: chance, danger, happens, punishment, take, result, outcome, action, unknown. Then write an example of a risk and a consequence in the table. Activity 2: Below is an image of a house. How many hazards can you spot? Make a list of them. Then decide which hazard is the highest risk to the household.

Page 63 · Reading 2

Activity 3: While there are hazards in our homes, there are also hazards in our local community. Look at the pictures in the Student Book. Discuss the risks in each of these situations. Then suggest a way to keep yourself and others safe from the danger in the pictures. For each picture, write the hazard and the risk.

Page 65 · Reading 3

Activity 4: Inappropriate practices can lead to damages or to dangerous situations in the local environment. In Kenya, volunteers have cleaned up 6,000 kg of plastic waste to try to reduce the amount of plastic dumped into the ocean. The effort was part of a global initiative called the International Coastal Cleanup. It takes place every year around the world. […] Experts say that 8,000,000 tons of plastic ends up in the ocean every year. […] More of it breaks down into tiny pieces and is eaten by birds, fish and other sea creatures. […] Sometimes, the plastic goes straight into the ocean from people who don’t pick up after themselves at the beach. […] By 2050, experts say, there will be more waste than fish in the world’s oceans.

Questions: (a) What are the risks of having plastic in the ocean? (b) What can people do to stop plastic ending up in the ocean? (c) Do you think there should be consequences for throwing away plastic carelessly?

Page 66–67 · Reading 4

Activity 5: Read this story about a woman who was attacked on her way home. A woman was attacked and robbed on her way home from work. The police in a town are searching for two suspects who allegedly assaulted and robbed a woman on her way home from work last week. It happened around 8:30 p.m. The victim told police that she was walking home from work alone when she was suddenly attacked from behind. […] The victim was transported to a nearby hospital where she received medical treatment and was released.

Questions: (a) How do you think the woman felt after she was attacked? (b) What actions could she have taken to keep herself safe? (c) Are there any dangerous areas in your community? (d) Suggest one way you could keep yourself safe on your way home.

Page 68–69 · Reading 5

Activity 6: Complete the following activity. A risk is: … A consequence is: … Write risks that can lead to dangers in the home and risks that can lead to dangers in the community. Then complete: I can keep myself safe by: … I can keep others safe by: …
