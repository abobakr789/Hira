📖 Book text

Student Book Vol. 1 · Adapted textbook extracts; verified corrections included.

Page 66–67 · Reading 1

What problems do you think you would face if you left the UAE? Activity 1: Do you know the meaning of the words in the table? Think of an example of each that you have heard or read about. The four terms in the book’s table are diversity, prejudice, tolerance and discrimination. For each term, write the meaning and an example.

Page 67–68 · Reading 2

Activity 2: A writer visited a beautiful garden. All the seeds had been sown and tended until they grew into flowers. The flowers are all very different, with different colours and sizes, and the seeds came from many parts of the world. The flowers are individual and unique, and yet they grow in unison, together creating a place of reflection, peace and harmony. Visitors from all around the world come to marvel at their beauty. The garden is also visited by many species, such as bees, butterflies and crickets. All are essential in maintaining the equilibrium that allows the garden to continue to provide its magnificent spectacle for many years to come.

Questions: (a) What do you think this text is about? Discuss with your partner. (b) With the extract “all are essential in maintaining the equilibrium” in mind, what similarities exist between nature and the society that we live in?

Page 68–70 · Reading 3

Activity 3: When people talk of prejudice, discrimination and intolerance, certain groups of people, such as people of determination, economic refugees and elderly people, spring to mind. However, the book says anyone can experience feelings of exclusion. A writer tells a story. The writer had a circle of friends, a family and a sense of belonging, but no job, and decided to leave to find a career elsewhere. On arrival in the new country, the writer discovered what it feels like not to be one of “us” and to become one of “them”. Qualifications did not guarantee a career. The language barrier and body language caused misunderstandings, and gestures thought polite were seen as rude. The writer tried hard to fit in, but at a child’s birthday party the food of the writer’s own country went uneaten. After several years, the writer felt always one of “them”.

Questions: (a) What emotions do you think the writer experienced before leaving? (b) Analyse the words “us” and “them” in paragraph 4. (c) If you were to immigrate to a different country, how would you plan your integration into the new culture?

Page 71–72 · Reading 4

Activity 4: Sometimes we expect that everyone should be, act, look and dress the same. We get uncomfortable when people are different. Read the scenario in the book. The friends called Tom, Andy and Jim talk about who ate the last piece of pizza. Tom jokes about Andy’s body and suggests that Andy could not keep up when they go swimming. Andy laughs along, but the book shows Andy’s private thoughts: Andy is hurt and ashamed, and goes home. Jim says Andy was upset. Tom says he was only kidding. The book then says that being different should not mean a person feels isolated from society.

Questions: (a) Think about a time you felt like an outsider. How did this make you feel? What did you think about other people? (b) Now think of a time you treated someone like an outsider. Why did you behave this way? How do you think that person felt? (c) How can we help people who feel like outsiders?

Page 72–75 · Reading 5

Activity 5: A speaker in a TEDx talk told a childhood story about a classmate who shouted “you throw like a girl” in a game of softball. The speaker, in fourth grade, first thought it sounded great and only later realised it was meant as an insult. The speaker was born in the Netherlands to diplomat parents, spent childhood in 12 different countries, and remembers boys saying she and a friend could not play football, which made them want to play every recess. Tasks: (a) Deduce a definition of the word “stereotype”. (b) Intolerance often leads to stereotypes. Listen to your teacher’s instructions to do the walking debate about common stereotypes.

Activity 6: You are going to create a diverse community! Draw a different person on each of four sheets of paper. Think about hair colour, eye colour, clothing, job, background, general appearance and social group, and try to have four different social groups. On the back of each sheet, write a short paragraph saying who this person is, where they are from and how they may be discriminated against or what prejudice may exist about them. Be creative.

Let’s talk

① What does the book say about the flowers in the garden? ② What feeling did the writer in Activity 3 have, in the book? ③ What does the book say being different should not mean?
