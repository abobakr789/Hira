CONCEPT CARD

What is a community?

A community has three elements: individuals, an area and relationships, says the book. Primary groups such as family are joined automatically, and secondary groups such as school are joined by choice. People belong to groups for support and security, and communities change, for example after the discovery of oil in the UAE, says the book.

We belong together.

Page 7–11 · Reading 1

Unit questions: What is a community? Are there different groups within the community? What are the factors that affect the formation of the personality of the individual? What are the factors that affect the building of community culture? What is meant by social cohesion? How can societies achieve it? What is meant by global citizen? What is the role of the country in teaching the principles of global citizenship? Lesson questions: What different types of communities are there? What are the advantages of belonging to a community group?

Activity 1: Write down the types of groups you recognise in the images and the nature of the relationships between the members. Then write down the names of some of the groups that you belong to, identify the type of relationship between you and each of them, and write the name of the largest of these groups.

Page 12 · Reading 2

Activity 2: The basic part of any community is the individual. A community is a number of individuals living in one area. They have a shared culture, job, political system, or faith. Three elements describe a community: individuals, area and relationships. Being in a community is not only for human beings. Some animal groups also need their communities to survive. A group is a social unit made up of at least three people. They are connected by relationships. There are two types of groups: primary groups and secondary groups. Primary groups are groups of belonging. This is because the individual belongs to them automatically. These groups are united by very strong emotional relationships, such as those between family members. Secondary groups are those that individuals join by choice. They are called reference groups. A school is an example of a reference group. Relationships between members of these groups are often less emotional. […] Membership of a secondary group is usually temporary.

Questions: (a) Write some examples of human values that govern the relationships between group members and contribute to the continuity of the group. (b) From the text, identify some of the benefits that an individual gains from belonging to the community group.

Page 13–14 · Reading 3

Activity 3: What are the most important reasons why an individual belongs to a group? Read the article on the concept of social belonging by the German sociologist “George Simmel”. The idea of social belonging is one of the main ideas that decides a person’s relationship with the group. Humans are social beings who live together and depend on one another. Therefore, the core of the individual’s relationship depends on his or her need for support. […] Individuals can fulfil personal and social desires that they cannot normally fulfil on their own. Individuals have a sense of security and calm within a group that they accept and which accepts them. Individuals follow the standards and values of the group to which they belong. Individuals’ cultural heritage prepares them for positive interactions with other members of the community.

Questions: (a) Choose a group that you belong to. Describe to your classmates the benefits you get from belonging to this group. (b) Can individuals react to a crisis they are going through in their primary group in the same way that they would react to a crisis within a secondary group? Discuss and support your opinion with examples. (c) Identify the educational means provided by the Emirati community to help promote community belonging.

Page 14 · Reading 4

Activity 4: Historical events may occur in a country and change its social structure. Oil discovery in the UAE has led to important changes to its community systems and relationships. Family relationships used to rely on the extended family or tribe to decide the position of a person. This has changed. Now, there are different factors that decide a person’s status. Education is the most important thing today when deciding a person’s position. Also, the introduction of nuclear families, and the change of family roles and values because of the openness to the outside world. With the discovery of oil, the UAE became a modern, developed state. This caused a great increase in the number of employees. Because of very little local workers, people from other countries came to work in the UAE. These people (more than 200 nationalities) have played an important role in the development of the UAE.

Task: Research the Industrial Revolution and its impact on European social structures.

Page 15–17 · Reading 5

Activity 5: Not only does the social structure of a community change, but its scope may also change as the community expands. Read the quoted text adapted from the National Archives of the UAE. The late Sheikh Zayed (may God have mercy upon him) emphasised the importance of the federation when he became ruler of Abu Dhabi on 6 August 1966. […] The importance of the federation and the need to work in cooperation with the other Emirates have been the pillars of his mandate. […] He had a firm conviction that it could be established, based on the mutual ties between the different Emirates and the history and the heritage they have shared for centuries.

Questions: (a) Define your duties towards your country in terms of your role in each of the following groups: family, school and neighbourhood. (b) With one of your classmates, think about reasons that may increase an individual’s sense of belonging to a community. Activity 6: Write a ten-line paragraph outlining the impact of development on the growing number of groups to which individuals belong. Pay particular attention to the impact of social media.
