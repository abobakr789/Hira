CONCEPT CARD

The fall of the Roman Empire

The book says the Roman Empire was the largest empire in the world in the early centuries CE. The Pax Romana kept the peace for over two hundred years, but it did not benefit everyone equally. The empire was attacked by the Goths, the Huns led by Attila, and others. The Goths stormed Rome in 410 CE, and Attila died in 453 CE. The Western Roman Empire began to decline.

Page 100–102 · Reading 1

What caused the fall of the Roman Empire? How did the Roman Empire grow as large as it did? How did the Roman Empire distribute resources across their lands?

Activity 1: The red area on the map shows the ancient Roman Empire at its peak. Locate and label the area where the modern-day countries in the box would be on the map: France, Germany, England, Turkey, Italy, Egypt, Morocco, Greece, Spain, Algeria. Question: What is the Roman Empire bordered by? How did it help the empire spread?

Page 104 · Reading 2

In the early centuries of the Common Era, the Roman Empire was the largest empire in the world. It stretched north to England where Hadrian’s Wall was built and east to the River Euphrates in Iraq. The Roman Empire brought many different peoples and cultures together under Roman rule. […] For over two hundred years, the tight control of the Romans kept the conquered people from fighting. This period is called the Pax Romana. The Pax Romana did not benefit all of its people equally. Only male citizens who owned land could participate in the government. […] The common people had few privileges, yet were heavily taxed. Over time, the common people grew more and more dissatisfied.

Page 104–105 · Reading 3

In addition to internal conflicts, the Roman Empire was frequently attacked by tribes outside their borders. Romans referred to people who were not Roman as Barbarians because their ways, customs, laws, and outward appearances were not like the Romans. Around 376 CE, tribes, including the Goths, came from the southeast to the edge of the River Danube. […] In the Battle of Hadrianapolis, sometimes called Adrianopolis, in 378 CE, the Roman emperor Valens was killed. In 410 CE, the Goths stormed the city of Rome and carried away a great deal of the city’s wealth. […] Within a decade, the Huns, led by Attila, conquered the eastern territories of the Empire. […] The Hun dominance started to decline when their leader, Attila, died in 453 CE. […] Gradually, the Western Roman Empire began to decline. Date check: Attila ruled from 434 to 453 CE (Britannica), not within ten years of 410.

Page 107–111 · Reading 4

Activity 3: Match the group of people with the correct fact about each one. Groups: Franks, Goths, Romans, Huns, Vandals. Facts: captured most of Western Europe; took control of northern Africa; came to the edge of the Danube River; were often attacked by Germanic armies; were led by Attila.

Activity 4: Write the names of each of the kingdoms in the correct places on the map: Goths, Visigoths, Huns, Vandals, Suevi. Activity 5: Using information from the text, chart the causes or effects of each event: most Roman citizens could not vote and were taxed heavily; the Goths declared war on the Romans; Attila the Hun died in 453 CE. Activity 6: Some of the events that helped create the Pax Romana (27 BC to 180 CE) are listed. How do you think the events contributed to keeping the Roman Empire peaceful?

Page 112–113 · Reading 5

Activity 7: The way Rome allocated land resources made people unhappy. There were two types of land: (a) land that was excellent for farming, (b) land that was good for mining for iron. How would you allocate land? Explain your answers. Groups: upper class, lower class and farmers, soldiers and veterans.

Activity 8: How did Rome’s division of resources help cause the fall of the empire? Discuss and write notes.
