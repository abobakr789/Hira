CONCEPT CARD

Thinking about crime

People who commit crimes face consequences such as arrest, charges and prison, and victims can suffer for a long time, so support is important. The book also says irresponsible behaviour does not always have to be a crime, and that rules have consequences in school and in society.

Rules and support protect us.

Words to know

📖 Book text

Student Book Vol. 1 · Original text from the Student Book.

Page 106–107 · Reading 1

What consequences are there for people who commit crimes? What impacts can crime have on victims? Activity 1: If someone commits a crime, he should expect consequences. What could be the consequences of the actions you see in the images in the Student Book?

Page 108–109 · Reading 2

Activity 2: There are always consequences when people are arrested for committing a crime. Two men were arrested as they were trying to break into an automated teller machine (ATM) in an industrial area, police said. […] They were arrested within minutes, thanks to the quick response of police patrols. […] The pair confessed to the police that they had planned to steal money from the machine after breaking it open using tools. […] The duo was referred to the public prosecution to finish the investigation after charging them with attempted theft. The deputy director praised public awareness and cooperation which have helped the police prevent crimes and ensure safety and stability in the emirate.

Questions: (a) What crime were the men arrested for? (b) What do you think will happen next? (c) How effective were the police in arresting the criminals? (d) Find out about a crime that happened recently and write the details below.

Page 110–112 · Reading 3

Activity 3: Many crimes lead to criminals serving time in prison. Prison is used as a form of punishment in countries around the world. Austin, 22, says he had a fairly normal life until he was 20 and wanted to become a painter. He chose the wrong group of friends, and it was petty crime at first: shoplifting and minor vandalism. “I can’t blame anyone but myself,” he says. In prison he got into fights, until a prison guard called Ray took an interest in his drawings and told him about the art classes in the prison. Now he has served his time, has learned his lesson, and studies art at night in college while working in a store. He plans to exhibit his paintings.

Questions: (a) List the advantages of imprisonment as a form of punishment. (b) List the disadvantages of imprisonment as a form of punishment. (c) Did imprisonment achieve its aim in Austin’s case?

Page 113–114 · Reading 4

Activity 4: Answer the questions below to create a leaflet for a Crime Victims’ Helpline. People who commit crimes are eventually punished. But their victims can continue to suffer daily from the consequences of the crimes done against them. (a) Do you think the victims of crime need support? Explain your answer. (b) Name three effects of crime. (c) List three things that this organisation could provide. (d) There is no slogan on this leaflet. Compose a suitable slogan for the organisation.

Page 114–117 · Reading 5

Activity 5: Irresponsible behaviour doesn’t always have to be a crime. Irresponsible behaviour can simply be bad behaviour, and it happens in school and society too. If a school rule is broken, there are consequences for the students who break them. School rules include: follow the instructions and rules of the school; behave responsibly so you don’t put other people or yourself in danger; take care of buildings and property; arrive on time; show a positive attitude towards learning; show commitment to the heritage and culture of the UAE by behaving honestly and with dignity; and show respect to all teachers, staff, fellow students and members of the community.

Questions: (a) Who makes the rules in school and society? (b) What happens when rules are broken in your school? (c) Do you think we really need rules? (d) What happens when rules are broken in society? Activity 6: Think of three crimes. Consider the impact of each crime on the victim and suggest a protective measure against it. Complete the table: Impact on victim, Impact on others.
