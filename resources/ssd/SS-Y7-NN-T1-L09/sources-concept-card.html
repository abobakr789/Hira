CONCEPT CARD

Protection against crime

A crime breaks the law, and a victim is a person who is harmed. The book says crime can take place online, so we should think critically, use long and different passwords, give websites the minimum information and ask a knowledgeable adult. It also says family is like a wall of protection.

Stay alert, protect others.

📖 Book text

Student Book Vol. 1 · Original text from the Student Book.

Page 92–95 · Reading 1

How can you protect yourself from crime? How can you protect others from crime? Activity 1: Think of as many crimes as you can. Write them down and place them in order of most serious to least serious. Activity 2: Examine the images in the Student Book and complete the activity. (a) Suggest a way the person in each picture could better protect themselves from crime. (b) Choose one image and write a story about what is happening in the picture.

Page 96–97 · Reading 2

Activity 3: The role of the family is an important part of Emirati culture; it means unconditional and endless love, care, respect and support. […] Emirati families take the form of the extended family, which means that parents, children, grandparents, and in-laws often share one household. […] Family is like a wall of protection. It gives you a feeling of safety and makes you aware that, no matter what, there is always someone there who will offer you support. […] The bonds of a family are more important than all other issues and they can help overcome any obstacles that life might bring.

Questions: (a) How do UAE family relationships affect a person’s sense of security? (b) Imagine that one of your family members was in a difficult situation. How would you and other family members react?

Page 98–100 · Reading 3

Activity 4: Crime doesn’t only take place in our communities, it can also take place online. For example, if people are not careful, they can have their bank details stolen or their computers hacked in order to get the user’s personal information. […] To protect yourself from online crimes, there are a few things you can do. The most important is to think critically. Does an offer look too good to be true? It probably is. […] Don’t go roaming to strange sites on the web, stick to ones you know and trust. If you’re not sure about a site, ask a knowledgeable adult. Don’t use the same passwords for every site, and make your passwords long and difficult to guess. If a website asks you for personal information, think hard before you enter it. […] If you think you’ve made a mistake, don’t be afraid to tell. The Dubai Police Force has launched a campaign warning of the dangers of online crimes.

Questions: (a) Why should you not click on a link from a friend if it doesn’t sound like them? (b) Why does the writer say “ask a knowledgeable adult”? (c) Why should you not use the same passwords for every site? (d) The poster contains the hashtag #YourSecurityOurHappiness. What do you think this means? Key fact (the book’s claim): the UAE has one of the lowest crime rates in the world.

Page 101–104 · Reading 4

Activity 5: Crime is a global problem. Crimes against humanity have been common throughout history. An example of a crime against humanity is mass killing of a particular sect of a population. In Rwanda in 1994 mass killing of the Tutsi population took place. Most of the people that carried out the killing were Hutus. The book then shares Valentina’s story, written by a visitor who met her as a girl of 13 who had survived a massacre, and met her again three years later as a 16-year-old who had survived her injuries and now lives with an aunt and two other orphans. The man who led the killers has confessed some of his crimes.

Questions: (a) What happened to Valentina? (b) What do you think she did to protect herself? (c) What consequence could the old man face? (d) Put yourself in Valentina’s shoes. In what way do you think the events of 1994 still impact her life today?

Page 105 · Reading 5

Activity 6: Complete the table. Identify three crimes you have heard about and write how you can protect yourself and others from these crimes. Use three columns: Crime, Protect myself, Protect others.
