CONCEPT CARD

Justice in leadership

Leaders practise distributive justice when they give everyone a fair chance. In the story, the teacher used equitable participation: James and Wael competed for coordinator. The book also asks about fair jobs, free education and health services, and supporting people of determination to work.

Fair leaders give everyone a chance.

Words to know

benefitsgood things that people receivevacancya job that is open for someone to take
leadera person who guides a groupdeterminationnot giving up when things are hard
election programmea plan a candidate presents to win a voteannouncementa public message telling people something
equitable participationeveryone has a fair chance to take partinjusticeunfair treatment

📖 Book text

Student Book Vol. 1 · Original text from the Student Book.

Page 51–52 · Reading 1

How can leaders practise distributive justice? How do people feel when they are treated unjustly?

Activity 1: In front of you there is a box filled with stationery, including pencils, ballpoint pens, colouring pens, erasers, geometric rulers, and other items. Share these items among your classmates without regard to age, position within the class, and needs or possessions. Was it possible? Was everyone satisfied? How can you ensure a fair distribution of benefits?

Page 52–53 · Reading 2

Activity 2: Read the story about James who felt unjustly treated in class. James was unhappy that Wael had been the coordinator of the class for four months. […] He always worked hard to help his classmates, even though he had not been given an official role by the teacher. […] The teacher proposed that James compete with Wael for the post of coordinator. Each candidate devised an election programme […]. The teacher said that the position of coordinator would be based on the principle of equitable participation in the class.

Questions: (a) Imagine that Wael was angry about James’ wish to run for the position of coordinator. Would his anger be justified? Why or why not? (b) How should Wael see things to help him accept James’ wish?

Page 54 · Reading 3

Activity 3: Read the text from the jobs page of a municipality website. As part of our overall efforts to improve the municipality, we are seeking to develop the city into one of the most advanced cities in the world in terms of the excellence of services provided to residents. […] we are looking for qualified and talented people who will work hard to provide the highest standards of service. […] Ibrahim and Yusuf have been friends since childhood. Ibrahim spent six years at university studying civil engineering, while Yusuf has devoted his time and effort to developing his motor racing skills. […]

Questions: (a) Imagine you are in charge of this municipality. How could Yusuf and Ibrahim help the municipality? (b) What real contributions could Yusuf and Ibrahim make?

Page 55 · Reading 4

Activity 4: A country has issued the following decisions: (a) Provide free education from kindergarten to secondary school. (b) Provide free health services for all. (c) Making sure 30 percent of government positions are filled by women. Discuss these decisions with your classmates. Do you agree with them?

Page 56 · Reading 5

Activity 5: Read the following texts about people of determination. Saif Saeed, who has a mental disability, has proven his ability to work. He works for the Desert Group, a local company that Saif says provides the appropriate working environment to enable him to work productively. […] Aisha Bilal, a teacher, worked with dedication and skill to overcome the many challenges resulting from being paralysed since childhood. Aisha says, ‘The support I received from my family enabled me to continue and succeed.’ […]

Some people refuse to allow people of determination to participate in employment, arguing that they are not able to physically or mentally perform roles effectively. Based on what you have read, write a 10-line letter addressing the importance of supporting all people to work.

Page 57 · Reading 6

Activity 6: Consider some injustices in the world. Start building a project that addresses these issues. You will present your ideas to the class. (a) Think of some examples of prejudice or discrimination in your school or community. (b) Find others in your class who are interested in the same issue and create a group for the project. (c) Identify the problem that you would like to resolve. (d) Decide on the outcome you would like to achieve. (e) Provide a list of the resources you need. These might be human and/or material. (f) Decide who will complete which task and when.

Let’s talk

① Why was James unhappy? ② What principle did the teacher use? ③ What does Aisha say helped her succeed?
