CONCEPT CARD

The geography of East Asia

East Asia has natural resources like mountains and rivers and man-made ones like the Great Wall and the Grand Canal. The Yellow and Yangtze are China’s longest rivers, says the book. In 1937 most of the region was under colonial powers, and after the war industrialization and urbanization transformed cities such as Osaka and Chongqing.

Land, water and change.

Words to know

colonialismone country ruling and controlling anothercanala man-made waterway
sustainabilityusing resources so they do not run outurbanizationthe growth of cities
independencebeing free from outside controlrangea line of mountains
industrializationgrowth of factories and industryinfrastructureroads, bridges and services a place needs

📖 Book text

Student Book Vol. 1 · Original text from the Student Book.

Page 120–123 · Reading 1

East Asia has some of the most important resources in the world. Some of the resources are natural resources, like mountains, rivers, minerals, and others. Some of the resources are man-made, like the buildings, bridges, dams, and others. One of the most famous man-made structures is the Great Wall. The Great Wall was built in stages from 200 BCE and 1644 CE. It was built to protect China from invaders from the North. It stretched for over 21,196 km. […] The Yellow (or Huang) River and the Yangtze River are China’s longest rivers and are connected by the ancient Grand Canal.

Questions: (a) What are some important features in China? (b) Look at the map and list the capital cities you see.

Page 124–126 · Reading 2

Activity 2: Use maps to help you fill in the blanks with the names of the major rivers and seas in or near China, using the words in the box: Yellow (Huang) River, Chongqing, Seoul, East China Sea, Taipei, Taiwan, Guangzhou, Tokyo, North Korea, Shanghai, Beijing, Long River, South Korea. The Yangtze River empties into the East China Sea near the major port city of Shanghai. […] Canals constructed during the Yuan Dynasty in the 1300s connected the Yellow River to the modern capital city of Beijing. The Korean peninsula is separated into two countries: North Korea (the Democratic People’s Republic of Korea) and South Korea (the Republic of Korea), whose capital is Seoul. To the east of Korea is the Sea of Japan and the island country of Japan, whose capital is Tokyo.

Activity 3: (a) What is the large mountain range in southwestern China? (b) Which country is north of central China? (c) Which three countries are northeast of China? (d) What is the tallest mountain on earth? Activity 4: Label the map using the words in the box: Himalayan Mountains, India, South Korea, Mongolia, North Korea, Mu Us Desert and others.

Page 127–129 · Reading 3

Activity 5: List two things you know about China that you did not know before this lesson. Activity 6: In the past, canals in China were built near the rivers to irrigate farm fields. Over time canals became important transportation routes with the ability to transport barges and boats with goods, food, animals, and people. Important canals in ancient China included the Grand Canal that connected the two longest rivers in China, the Yellow River and the Yangtze River. Another canal built during the Qin Dynasty was the Lingqu Canal in southern China. The Grand Canal is one of the greatest engineering feats of the ancient world. It is the longest canal in the world. […] Construction began on the Grand Canal in the 5th century BCE with construction and maintenance continuing ever since. […] By the 13th century, it had been expanded to over 2,000 km of artificial waterways, linking China’s river basins. Today, it connects the capital city of Beijing in the north to Zhejiang province in the south.

Questions: (a) What was the largest canal in the world? (b) When was it built? (c) List at least three ways that canals were important to the people in ancient China.

Page 129–133 · Reading 4

Activity 7: Use the maps and texts from this unit as well as your own research to add notes to the mind maps: three major rivers, a mountain range, capital city, features in China, major cities, seas bordering China, and countries in east and southeast Asia. Activity 8: At the start of World War II in 1937, many parts of East Asia and Southeast Asia were controlled by European, American, and Japanese colonial powers. […] The map shows that only Thailand and the Republic of China were independent when the war broke out. […] In 1937, Japanese armies invaded China. Chinese of all ages fought against the Japanese occupation. […] The war raged for eight years. Eventually, the United Kingdom and its Commonwealth countries of Australia, India, New Zealand and Canada, called the Allied forces, entered the war. The United States joined the Allied forces, using Hawaii, which is the closest state to Southeast Asia.

Questions: (a) When did World War II start for people in China? (b) Which countries were colonized? (c) Which countries were not colonized at the time? (d) Which state of the United States of America is closest to Southeast Asia?

Page 133–134 · Reading 5

Activity 9: Look at the images of Osaka, Japan, and Chongqing, China, and reflect on how industrialization and urbanization have transformed the region since the end of World War II in 1945. Write notes. Activity 10: Use the words in the box to fill in the gaps. We find evidence of the development of careful design and city planning in both ancient cities and modern cities. Cities along the coast served as ports that allowed trade by sea. Transportation between coastal cities required roads. Governments also needed to provide infrastructure such as clean water and sewers. Well-planned cities feature public spaces, commercial areas and residential neighbourhoods. When cities grow, we find the establishment of vital services such as public hospitals and public schools.
