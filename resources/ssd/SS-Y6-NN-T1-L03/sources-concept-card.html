CONCEPT CARD

Distributive justice

Distributive justice is sharing resources fairly. In the car story, the children had to agree on a fair solution. In the parable, the governor says each blind man was partially right: they did not wait to know the whole truth. The book also asks how health care can be funded fairly, from taxes, insurance, direct payments and donations.

See the whole truth before judging.

📖 Book text

Student Book Vol. 1 · Original text from the Student Book.

Page 32–33 · Reading 1

What is the best way to solve a dispute over resources? How can fair and just distribution be used to solve problems?

Activity 1: Read the story below. Then, in your group, identify a solution to ensure that everyone is happy at the end. Zang’s family decided to go on a trip. They were going to the car when Rafa ran to sit beside the window. Yan ran behind him and sat next to the other window. May was upset because she had to sit in the middle. Their father refused to leave until the children were all happy with where they were sitting. He had them get out of the car and their mother told them to agree on a solution that worked for everyone.

Page 34 · Reading 2

Activity 2: Now, let’s think about justice at the societal level. Read the story of ‘The Elephant and the Blind Men’. An Indian parable tells the story of six blind men who heard that an enormous elephant would be brought to their town to take part in an annual celebration. […] The governor agreed and asked each of them to describe the elephant in one sentence after touching it. […] The first man said, ‘The elephant looks like a rope.’ Surprised, the second man said, ‘Where is the rope? It is exactly like a wall.’ The third man laughed and said, ‘A rope and a wall! How is that even possible when the elephant is nothing but a big spear?’ […]

Page 35–37 · Reading 3

[…] ‘Every one of them was right about the description of the part of the elephant they touched. […] each of the six was partially right; the only mistake they made was not waiting to know the whole truth. We also, without exception, often make this mistake. We speak about part of the truth and we think that this part is the whole truth. Then we hold on to it and become stubborn about it. We argue with those who disagree and hold different opinions, simply because we have failed to see the truth from all sides.’ When people heard the governor’s wise words, they nodded and became humble.

Question: In your opinion, was the governor being fair or unfair towards the six blind men when he made them describe the elephant in front of other people? Discuss this with your group and provide reasons for your answer.

Page 37–38 · Reading 4

Activity 3: Now, let’s think about justice around the world. Read the text about the decreasing birth rate in Germany. In Europe, and particularly in Germany, there has been a decline in the number of births. […] Young working people are increasingly deciding against having large families because many of them see this as having a bad impact on their career. […] Mothers who want to return to work often find themselves in an unhappy situation. If there are no places available in public nursery schools, then new mothers’ salaries are spent on private nursery schools. […]

Question: In your group, discuss whether Germany could achieve a better system for new mothers. Write down your ideas.

Page 40 · Reading 5

Activity 4: Look at the image. It represents justice. (a) What do the different parts of the image represent? (b) In groups, create a design representing distributive justice in society.

Page 41 · Reading 6

Activity 5: Read the text about funding and distributive justice in health systems throughout the world. […] Types of healthcare systems vary widely throughout the world. […] all healthcare systems are funded by one or more of the following resources: 1. Public taxes imposed by the government. 2. The social health insurance system. 3. Private health insurance. 4. Direct payment of costs by those who receive healthcare. 5. Grants and donations given by charitable institutions and individuals.

Questions: (a) What do you think is the best way to fund a healthcare system? (b) What are the challenges that prevent countries from achieving justice in the healthcare sector? (c) Do some research into the Emirati healthcare system. Has distributive justice been achieved? Present your findings in written or electronic form.

Let’s talk

① What did the father do about the car seats? ② What did the governor say the six men did wrong? ③ Name two ways healthcare is funded.
