Celebrating Great Attitudes

Unit 2: Valuing Diversity · Lesson 5

Student Book Vol. 1 · pages 96–107

Welcome the class. Today we celebrate diversity and think about the attitudes that include everyone, using only the Student Book texts. Ask the lesson questions from the Student Book (p. 96) and collect ideas without correcting. Activity 2 tells real celebrities’ experiences of discrimination, including racist abuse: describe it calmly, do not repeat abusive words, never ask a pupil to speak for a group, and follow the school safeguarding policy if a pupil shares a worry. The celebrities’ stories, the Tolerance Sign date (27 October 2016), the 200 nationalities claim and the calendar of international days are the book’s claims: teacher check, and do not add details from memory. Personal tasks (your attitude to differences, how equipped you feel) are answered in pupils’ own words: accept reasoned answers and do not judge. The “Find Someone Who” sheet in Activity 1 is in the book: use it only with questions that every pupil is comfortable answering. Research and creative tasks are teacher-checked.

📖 Book text 2/5

Activity 2, celebrities who faced discrimination

Read and answer.

Activity 2: Does anyone have the right to question our existence, our beliefs, our rights, or even the way we look? No! Sadly, most of us will face some form of discrimination during our lives, celebrities included. The book gives three examples. First, the famous American talk show host Oprah Winfrey was shopping in a store full of expensive handbags and asked to see a bag worth AED 140,000, but the shop assistant refused, assuming she could not afford it. (a) In what way is this prejudice or discrimination?

Second, the book says Muhammad Ali won the Olympic gold medal for boxing in Rome in 1960. The day after he returned to the United States, a restaurant owner in his hometown refused to serve him because he was black, and Ali threw his gold medal in the river to protest. (b) Do you think that was a sensible way to fight back? Third, the book says that on a visit to India in 2007 the former Australian cricketer Andrew Symonds faced abusive taunts from a crowd. (c) Do celebrities have better ways to fight discrimination than ordinary people?

Student Book Vol. 1 · page 98–100 · Reading 2

Celebrating Great Attitudes
Student Book pp. 98–100 (summarised). These are real people’s stories told in the book: teacher check, and do not add details. The racist abuse is described without the abusive words: do not repeat them. Questions (b) and (c) are opinion: accept reasoned answers.

📖 Book text 3/5

Activity 3, the Tolerance Sign in Dubai

Read and plan.

Activity 3: Read the article about an initiative to promote tolerance. The book says the “Tolerance Sign” announcing Dubai as the capital of the world for tolerance was launched on Thursday 27 October 2016, in an event that saw four doves released into the sky at the base of Burj Khalifa. The hand sign of a dove and the hashtag Tolerance_Unites_Us were announced and translated into different languages to spread the message of tolerance. A celebrity presenter was appointed ambassador for the Mohammad Bin Rashid Award for Tolerance, and a social media influencer pledged to promote the values of tolerance and coexistence. The secretary-general of the award said it carries a message about a country where more than 200 nationalities coexist peacefully, and that the initiative aims to eliminate violence, extremism, hatred and discrimination.

Task: With your group, create a diary of events to celebrate the days in the book’s table: 16 November, International Day of Tolerance; 2 December, UAE National Day; 3 December, International Day of Persons with Disabilities; 10 December, Human Rights Day; the third Monday in January, Martin Luther King, Jr. Day; 9 May, Europe Day; and 21 May, World Day for Cultural Diversity for Dialogue and Development. What actions can you take to promote diversity and tolerance?

📖 Book text 4/5

Activities 4 and 5, celebrating diversity

Read and create.

Activity 4: Celebrating Diversity. The book suggests five ways: (1) find out about games or sports from another culture; (2) visit a museum, or explore museum websites; (3) learn a language, because language is a big part of culture; (4) read traditional stories, since many folk tales from around the world have similar themes, which shows that we all have things in common; and (5) try the cuisine of other cultures, which helps us understand our international friends better. The book says today we will share diversity through music, traditional food and clothing, sharing stories and teaching each other about values.

Questions: (a) What did you learn about other cultures? (b) What can you do to promote respect for other people’s culture? Activity 5: Diversity Notice Board. The UAE is one of the most diverse places on Earth, and it is important that all of us who live here embrace this diversity to attain a life of peace and happiness. We are going to create a diversity notice board to remind us of this important day of celebration. Use your imagination and let’s get creative!

📖 Book text 5/5

Activity 6, embracing the foreign

Read and reflect.

Activity 6: Life would not be a journey if there was not an explorer living inside us. Who would want to visit the same place over and over? We all want to see new places, meet new people and have new experiences. The book says that there would be no journey if we did not embrace the “foreign” and the “different”, and that the greatest explorers wanted to discover something foreign. It asks: if you take the word foreign out of our vocabulary, what would you replace it with? The foreigner you fear is a best friend, brother or mother to someone else. If your mind is free from fear, you will realise we share the same characteristics and dreams. Maybe there is no “foreign” and maybe we are all the same.

Questions: (a) Interpret your feelings about your attitude to differences. (b) How do you feel you are equipped to deal with issues of prejudice, discrimination and bias in your own life?

Student Book Vol. 1 · page 106 · Reading 5

Celebrating Great Attitudes
Student Book p. 106 (summarised). Both questions are personal: pupils choose what to share; accept reasoned answers and do not judge. Gently note that people are also different, so “we are all the same” is the book’s poetic idea: pupils can discuss it.
