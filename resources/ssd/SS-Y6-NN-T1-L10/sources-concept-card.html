CONCEPT CARD

Disease prevention

The UN Sustainable Development Goals are 17 goals aiming for a better world by 2030. Goals 1, 2 and 3 address poverty, hunger and good health, and Goal 6 concerns clean water and sanitation. Good health requires access to clean water, which is a basic human right. Everyone has a part to play: governments, communities and people like you.

📖 Book text

Student Book Vol. 1 · Original text from the Student Book.

Page 92–93 · Reading 1

Who is responsible for preventing disease? How can countries work together to improve global health? How do global health goals improve the lives of others?

Activity 1: Think about how you want to see the future of the planet. Activity 2: Read the article United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) about the world’s plans for a brighter future. By the year 2030, the world will be a better place. […] The UAE is among the 193 nations that support the 17 goals which aim to improve the living standards and life expectancy of people all over the world. The initiative especially hopes to make a difference to people whose lives are at risk from hunger, poverty, disease and lack of sanitation or clean water. Clarification: 2030 is the SDG target date, not a guaranteed outcome. The UN reports that progress is uneven.

Page 94 · Reading 2

The goals cover a range of global issues which include Climate Change, Peace, Justice and Strong Institutions, Life on Land, and Life below Water. However, among the 17 goals, goals 1, 2, and 3 address eliminating poverty and hunger, and promoting good health and well-being, which are closely linked. Goal 6 concerns clean water and sanitation. Poor health often means that children have to drop out of school. This means that they can’t get well-paid jobs when they grow up. This leads to poverty and malnutrition, which then leads to poor health and the cycle continues. For the goals to be reached, everyone needs to do their part: governments, communities, and people like you.

Questions: (a) Whose lives do the SDGs aim to improve? (b) Who will be involved in helping to achieve the goals? (c) What part does a healthy lifestyle play in achieving the global goals? (d) What do you think the world will be like in 2030?

Page 95 · Reading 3

Facts and Figures: (a) Malnutrition is the biggest contributor to disease in the world. (b) Almost half of child deaths are linked to malnutrition. (c) Children born into poverty are almost twice as likely to die before the age of five as those from wealthier families.

Activity 3: Create a checklist reflecting on your own health and what you can improve. Divide your list into three categories: (a) Physical activities, (b) Diet, (c) Hygiene.

Page 96 · Reading 4

Activity 4: Read the text about access to clean water and answer the following questions. Good health requires access to clean water. It is a basic human right. Yet millions of people die each year because they do not have clean water and good sanitation. […] For example, the UAE supports global efforts to bring clean water and sanitation to some of the most remote areas in the world. Sheikh Zayed (may God have mercy upon him) believed that water was the most essential element in life. He therefore supported finding and conserving water in the UAE.

Questions: (a) Explain why water projects are life-changing. (b) Research how Sheikh Zayed found and conserved water. (c) Name a foundation that is working to improve global health.

Page 97 · Reading 5

Activity 5: Now it is time for your group to present your campaign on the topic you chose in the previous lesson. Answer any questions your classmates may have and ask questions about the other campaigns too.
