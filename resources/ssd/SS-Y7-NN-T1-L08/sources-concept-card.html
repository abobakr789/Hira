CONCEPT CARD

First aid and the Red Crescent

First aid is first help for a hurt or ill person. Get adult and emergency help promptly. For an unresponsive person, normal breathing determines recovery position or CPR. For choking, check for effective coughing before intervention. Babies need a different procedure. UAE ambulance: 998. Practical skills need qualified training.

Page 80–81 · Reading 1

What are some basic first aid skills? What is the Emirates Red Crescent? Activity 1: Complete the following activity. There are many different incidents that require first aid. Would you know how to perform first aid if one of your friends was injured? (a) First aid is: … (b) Why is it important to know first aid?

Page 82–84 · Reading 2

Activity 2: The Emirates Red Crescent is a volunteer humanitarian organisation. It was established on 31 January 1983 and was internationally attested as a member of the International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies in 1986. […] In times of peace: organising awareness programmes, providing first aid, protecting and controlling epidemics, paying attention to social issues, providing various humanitarian assistance for vulnerable, needy people and victims of accidents and disasters. In times of war: transferring and treating the wounded, providing first aid and relief to victims, protecting civilians, and searching for missing persons and reuniting separated families. […] Our values: transparency, credibility, humanitarian partnership with civil society institutions, and encouraging voluntary work as a value in itself.

Questions: (a) Give some real-life examples of Red Crescent work. Say how the Red Crescent’s intervention alleviated people’s suffering. (b) Design a poster for the Emirates Red Crescent outlining the work that it does. Key fact: Red Crescent Day is 8th May.

Page 85–87 · Reading 3

Activity 3, corrected safety summary: First check whether the person can cough effectively, speak or breathe. Encourage an effective cough. If they cannot cough effectively, speak or breathe, shout for help. In the UAE ask a helper to call 998. For a conscious adult or child over 1 year with severe choking: give up to 5 back blows, checking after each; if not cleared, give up to 5 abdominal thrusts. Repeat the back-blow and thrust cycle while help is coming. If they become unresponsive, call 998 and start CPR, following the call handler. Babies under 1 year need chest thrusts, never abdominal thrusts. Abdominal thrusts are also unsuitable during pregnancy; other methods are needed. This is discussion, not practical training. Never practise blows or thrusts on classmates. Questions: What signs suggest severe choking? Why is the baby procedure different?

Page 87–89 · Reading 4

Activity 4, corrected safety summary: Make sure it is safe to approach. Check for a response. If the person is unresponsive, shout for help and ask a helper to call 998, or call on speakerphone. Follow the call handler's instructions. Open the airway and check for normal breathing for no more than 10 seconds. Occasional gasps are not normal breathing. If breathing normally, use the recovery position and keep monitoring. If not breathing normally, start CPR and have a helper bring a defibrillator if available. Questions: (a) Why is checking normal breathing important? (b) Explain how to get adult and emergency help. Discuss the recovery position; any practical training must be led by a qualified first-aid instructor. Do not practise CPR on classmates.

Page 89–91 · Reading 5

Activity 5, discussion scenarios: (1) A player collapses and is unresponsive but breathing normally. (2) A woman falls on stairs and points to her injured leg. (3) An adult clutches his chest and collapses. (4) A friend appears to choke while eating. For every scenario, first consider safety, get an adult and call 998 when emergency help is needed. For an unresponsive person, check normal breathing and follow the call handler. For choking, first assess whether coughing is effective. Do not move an injured person unnecessarily. Activity 6: Write a different scenario and explain how to get suitable help. This is a discussion task, not a substitute for first-aid training.

Activity 6: Think of a scenario that hasn’t been mentioned in this lesson. Write the scenario and what first aid you should give in that situation.
