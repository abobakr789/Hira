📖 Book text

Student Book Vol. 1 · Original text from the Student Book.

Page 86–87 · Reading 1

How can governments and non-governmental organisations financially help people in need? What can we do to financially help people in need? Activity 1: We all know people who appear to be wealthier than us, and people who appear to be less wealthy. How do you feel when you see a very wealthy person, and when you see people who are less wealthy? The book says our feelings depend on our attitude to wealth. It gives a statement by Sheikh Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan that wealth is not money but lies in people, and that this is why resources are directed to building the individual and serving the nation. What does this quote mean to you? Share your thoughts with the class.

Page 87–89 · Reading 2

Activity 2: Read “Personal Responsibility and Social Responsibility”. The book says we are motivated by different things, such as doing well at school, helping our family or promoting a cause, and it is our personal responsibility to live by our values. It says we must also think beyond ourselves: social responsibility means being committed to improving society, and may even require sacrifice, putting society’s needs ahead of your own benefit. The book says socially responsible people are proactive: when they see social injustice, they take action, and their moral code influences their actions.

The book says a common form of social responsibility is using your money for the good of others, such as giving to a charity or an NGO. It says you must pick the NGO carefully, based on the transparency of its goals and operations, so that your money is used for good works.

Questions: (1) Think of a situation where you would put the benefit of society ahead of your personal benefit. (2) Why is it important that a person takes social responsibility? (3) Explain the statement that a socially responsible person is proactive and willing to make the world a better place for everyone around him, and briefly suggest an action you would take against social injustice.

Page 90–91 · Reading 3

Activity 3: Read about organisations. The book says individuals are limited in what they can do because they have limited resources, so big change requires big efforts, and organisations can make an impact. It says Non-Governmental Organisations (NGOs) are important contributors to development projects in Africa and other underprivileged regions. They are not-for-profit groups that exercise financial transparency about aid and donations, have clear objectives with results in annual reports, and have projects to end poverty and hunger by providing food, water, health care and education.

The book says corporate social responsibility is an emerging trend: companies accounting for environmental and social consequences in their costs. A manufacturing plant, for example, may cause pollution to save on filters, but a responsible firm restructures its operations in an environmentally friendly way. The book says this is not yet regulated by governments, so firms must volunteer; programmes range from awareness campaigns to donating part of profits, or producing safe products.

Questions: (1) The text mentions areas where NGOs help the poor in Africa. Propose an idea for a sustainable project to help children in Africa have a better life. (2) How would you persuade the CEO of a small or medium enterprise that it pays to incorporate social responsibility into their operations?

Page 91–94 · Reading 4

Activity 4: Read “Money for Good: Government Level”. The book says reducing inequality and helping poorer nations is a collection of efforts from governments, organisations and individuals. It says countries fall into developed and developing groups, and that the biggest responsibility lies with developed countries, which have more wealth and resources. It says the UAE contributes billions to fight poverty as part of its humanitarian vision and is a role model for its commitment to sustainable development. The book asks: think of a resource, other than money, that is abundant in the UAE and could help the poor of the world. How would it help?

Activity 5: The book says that in 2000 the UN hosted the Millennial Summit, where all 191 UN members committed to a 15-year plan to achieve eight Millennial Development Goals by 2015, including eradicating extreme poverty and hunger, universal primary education, gender equality, reducing child mortality, improving maternal health, combating diseases, environmental sustainability and a global partnership. It says that in 2015 studies showed 21 million lives were saved, with the biggest successes in reducing child mortality and fatal disease outbreaks, and that in January 2016 the UNDP introduced 17 Sustainable Development Goals, adding peace and justice, climate change and reduced inequality.

Questions: (1) How should the UNDP go about achieving the goals? (2) The first goal is to eradicate poverty: is it realistic? Explain. (3) Goal 10 is reducing inequality: define inequality and say why reducing it is important. (4) As a student, do you share responsibility in helping achieve those goals? Explain.

Page 94–95 · Reading 5

Activity 6: Read about small NGOs that have a big impact. The book says microfinance organisations are small banks that give microcredit to people who usually do not qualify for loans from big banks. It says they give loans as small as AED 2000 to help people start small businesses, which have sometimes transformed lives. They were criticised at the beginning as risky, but the book says the default rates proved to be very low and the debtors were glad to repay the loans.

Question 1: Now that you know about microfinance, think of a business idea that a debtor who borrows AED 4000 could implement. The book then gives a statement by Sheikh Mohammed bin Rashid Al Maktoum, saying that Sheikh Zayed’s name has become synonymous with generosity and giving around the world, that he did not like talking about his philanthropy, and that he instilled a love of giving in his people, making the Emirates a global humanitarian platform. Question 2: What examples do you know that show Sheikh Zayed’s sense of social responsibility?

Let’s talk

① What does the book say socially responsible people do when they see injustice? ② How many members joined the Millennial Summit, in the book? ③ How small can microfinance loans be, in the book?
