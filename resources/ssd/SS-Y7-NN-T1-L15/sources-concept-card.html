CONCEPT CARD

Culture through art

In 1770 the court painter Xu Yang painted twelve silk scrolls of the Qianlong Emperor’s Southern Inspection Tour. They show everyday life along the Grand Canal in detail, because Suzhou was his hometown, says the book. We can use such art as evidence about the past.

Art keeps the past alive.

📖 Book text

Student Book Vol. 1 · Student Book readings with museum-verified factual updates.

Page 176–178 · Reading 1

Art in China: the Qianlong Emperor’s Southern Inspection Tour. In 1770, Xu Yang, a Qing dynasty court painter, painted a series of twelve large silk scrolls of the Emperor Qianlong’s tour of his country. Qianlong ruled from 1736 to 1795, one of the longest reigning emperors in Chinese history. These scrolls provide a richly detailed view of the social and economic life along the Grand Canal at Suzhou in China. The original tour was made in 1751 (Metropolitan Museum of Art). Xu Yang began a draft in 1764 and completed the silk version in 1770, in time for the emperor’s sixtieth birthday. […] Great attention was paid to capturing details of everyday life. The paintings are detailed because Suzhou, the city shown in these scrolls, was the hometown of the artist Xu Yang.

Page 176–178 · Reading 2

Questions: (a) Who is Xu Yang? (b) What did Xu Yang paint? (c) How can Xu Yang’s paintings help us understand about the city of Suzhou?

Page 179–181 · Reading 3

Activity 2: Look at the scroll from the Qianlong Emperor’s Southern Inspection Tour (Scroll Six: Entering Suzhou along the Grand Canal, dated 1770). Describe the scene, making sure to answer the questions. (a) What is the scene? (b) Are there any special man-made or natural land features? (c) What transportation is being used, if any? (d) What are people doing? (e) Are there any other interesting elements in the painting?

Page 182 · Reading 4

Activity 3: Look at the scroll again. In groups, discuss the questions and write notes. (a) What do you think the houses were made of? (b) Why do you think the buildings were built close to the canal? (c) What do you see at the top of the painting? What does this say about the location of the town?

Page 176–182 · Reading 5

Using the book’s texts and the scroll, discuss: what can a painting tell us about the past that a written text may not? What details of everyday life did the artist choose to show? Why might the emperor have wanted a record of his tour?
