CONCEPT CARD

Standards of distributive justice

Four standards help us share fairly: eligibility, equality, authority and need. Sarah had 500 AED to divide among three sisters. Water justice means finding a fair way to share water. Amartya Sen asks us to think about equality in terms of people’s capabilities.

Use clear standards to share fairly.

Words to know

equalityeveryone receiving the samescholarshipmoney given to a student to help pay for study
authoritythe power to decidecapabilitieswhat a person is able to do, also called competencies
needsomething a person must havemeritbeing worthy because of achievement

Page 43–44 · Reading 1

How would you describe distributive justice? What does sharing have to do with distributive justice? What is the connection between fairness and distributive justice?

Activity 1: Three plants of different sizes are placed in front of you. You have one litre of water. Each plant is thirsty and has not been watered for a week. How would you distribute the litre of water between them? What criteria did you use? What criteria did your classmates use? What does this tell us about distributive justice?

Page 44–45 · Reading 2

Activity 2: Read the text about Sarah’s distributive justice dilemma during her family’s preparation for Eid. Here comes Eid again. It is a time for families to come together and enjoy the holiday. […] Sarah was delighted when her parents asked her to go with them to Dubai Mall […]. When they got to the clothes store, Sarah’s parents asked her to choose clothes for herself and her two sisters up to a total value of 500 AED. Sarah was confused. She started thinking about how she should divide this amount to buy clothes for the three of them in a way that was fair for everyone.

Page 45–46 · Reading 3

Sarah started thinking: ‘Perhaps I should buy myself the most expensive dress because I’m more deserving of my father’s money. Or should I choose the most expensive dress for Maha as she is the eldest? But then there won’t be enough money left to buy two more dresses! And if I buy Yara, our younger sister, the dress that she liked last time we went shopping, there won’t be enough money for anything else because it’s so expensive. There is another option. Perhaps if I looked around more, I might find three dresses for the same price. Then there’d be no difference between us.’

Questions: (a) In your group, discuss the options considered by Sarah to distribute the 500 AED between her and her sisters. Categorise these options according to these justice criteria: Eligibility, Equality, Authority, Need. (b) If you were in Sarah’s position, how would you distribute the amount? Explain why your distribution would be fair.

Page 47–48 · Reading 4

Activity 3: Read the following text about water justice. Water is essential to human life. Water security means having enough water to meet human needs. Whilst ‘water justice’ means finding a fair way to share water. However, access to clean water is not always equal. It is affected by both governments and the environment. How can we ensure the fair and equal distribution of water between different regions and countries?

(a) In your group, discuss the photos and make some notes. Consider the relationship between the two areas pictured. Think about how resources might be distributed. Keep in mind the criteria for distributive justice that we learned. (b) Did each member of your group agree on the specific distribution method? What aspects did you agree on? What did you disagree on?

Page 48–49 · Reading 5

Activity 4: Read the following passage about the economic philosopher Amartya Sen. Amartya Kumar Sen was born on November 3, 1933, in India. He is an economist and philosopher, and a lecturer in the United Kingdom and the United States. In 1998, Sen received the Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences. He has made contributions, particularly in the field of social and economic justice. Amartya Sen invites us to think of equality based on individual capabilities, also known as competencies. He argues that given the different capabilities among individuals, equality is necessary and ineffective when it comes to achieving fairness.

(a) As a class, discuss the case presented below. Base your discussion on Amartya Sen’s idea about equality. (b) There are two ‘department manager’ vacancies in a major company. As a class, discuss who should receive each of the jobs.

Page 50 · Reading 6

Activity 5: Read the information from two university websites about scholarships. Then, answer the questions. The University of Iowa in the USA offers various scholarships each year to its students based on financial need, academic merit, or, in particular, academic performance. […] Citizens of the United Arab Emirates, or residents and nationals of the Gulf Cooperation Council, are eligible for scholarships. There are certain conditions necessary to receive a scholarship […].

Questions: (a) In what ways do the universities distribute the scholarships with justice? (b) What solution could the university offer in order to be just towards both the wealthy students and those who suffer from financial difficulties?
