📖 Book text

Student Book Vol. 1 · Original text from the Student Book.

Page 8–9 · Reading 1

Why is it important to be moral? In what ways does the UAE promote tolerance? Activity 1: The book gives a quotation by the writer Ernest Hemingway and asks you to analyse the concept of an ethical action, then suggest some actions you have done or could do that are moral actions. In the quotation, he says that what is moral is what you feel good after.

Activity 2: Read the passage about morality and discuss the points it makes. The book says morals are deep-rooted values that result in actions, positive or negative, and that they play a significant role in building societies. They are the source of individuals’ actions, they develop group empathy and regulate relationships, and this reinforces societal ties, cooperation, solidarity and strength. Morals are also fundamental because laws and rules are based on them, and they protect societies from destruction and dissolution. The book adds that nations’ morals minimise disputes and conflicts among people and are a requirement for the sustainability of nations.

Page 10–11 · Reading 2

The United Nations works for international peace and cooperation. It has 193 Member States. The Holy See and the State of Palestine are non-member observer States, so not every state is a UN member. Article 2 of the Charter includes sovereign equality, fulfilling Charter obligations in good faith, settling disputes peacefully, refraining from the threat or use of force, and assisting UN action under the Charter. It does not require consultation about every action a country intends to take.

Activity 2A: Write about two ethical behaviours governing each of four groups: children in a public park, football fans at the stadium, students in extracurricular activities, and parents’ behaviour in front of their children. Activity 2B: Imagine you are in a position where one of your classmates acts in an immoral manner. Write a ten-sentence dialogue illustrating the situation, and your reaction.

Page 12–14 · Reading 3

Activity 3: Read and discuss a section of UNICEF’s appeal about the most significant threats to the lives of millions of people in four countries. The book says that on 28 March 2017 UNICEF launched an emergency appeal to raise $255 million to respond to the urgent needs of children in the northeastern areas of Nigeria, Somalia, South Sudan and Yemen, providing food, water, health, education and protection services. Most funding goes to nutrition programmes and medical care for children with malnutrition, and also to drinking water, sanitation and hygiene advice, to protect against potentially fatal diseases and to respond to the threat of famine.

The book gives figures for each country, for example emergency health care for 3.9 million people in Northeast Nigeria, support for 1.7 million children under five in Somalia, life-saving aid for 145,000 people in South Sudan, and more healthcare facilities and volunteers reaching hard-to-reach communities in Yemen. It adds that UNICEF calls for the support of children and the protection of their human rights.

Activity 3A: Compare the situations of children in one of the countries mentioned with children in the UAE, in terms of healthcare, education and protection from danger. Activity 3B: UNICEF seeks funding for programmes to fight hunger and malnutrition. Carry out research about the UAE’s contribution to the fight against hunger and malnutrition.

Page 15–17 · Reading 4

Activity 4: Read what was written in Al Ittihad newspaper about tolerance and peace in the UAE, then answer the questions. The book’s text says the approach of tolerance, peacefulness, civil coexistence and respect for beliefs adopted by the UAE stems from the legacy of its people, and that it contributed to the UAE’s presence as a country that seeks peace and supports efforts to maintain it. Tolerance, love and peace are the principles it depends on. The text adds that the UAE is working on boosting humanitarian values shared by nations of the region, based on tolerance, acceptance and respect of others, and on trust and the bonds of brotherhood and friendship among all societal groups. It is cited as Al Ittihad newspaper website, 25 April 2017 (edited).

Questions: (1) Write about the way Sheikh Zayed, may God have mercy upon him, ruled the country. Describe his morals in dealing with citizens and residents and support your argument with examples. (2) The value of tolerance builds community and protects people from extremism. Define four values that facilitate the relationship between citizens and residents, showing their importance in building a cohesive society. (3) The Egyptian poet Ahmed Shawqi said that nations live as long as they keep their morals. Paraphrase the lines and compare them to an essay in a newspaper.

Page 17–21 · Reading 5

Activity 5: Read the passage about the National Programme for Tolerance and discuss the points that follow. The book says the United Nations General Assembly declared 1995 the Year for Tolerance, and that in the same year UNESCO Member States adopted the Declaration of Principles on Tolerance. The UNESCO-Madanjeet Singh Prize was also established to promote tolerance and non-violence, and it is given every two years at a ceremony marking the International Day of Tolerance, to people or organisations who have developed initiatives in a spirit of tolerance.

The book says that on 8 June 2016 the Cabinet, headed by Sheikh Mohammed bin Rashid Al Maktoum, adopted the National Tolerance Programme presented by Sheikha Lubna bint Khalid Al Qasimi, Minister of State for Tolerance. It is based on seven pillars: Islam; the UAE’s Constitution; Zayed’s legacy and the ethics of the UAE; international conventions; archaeology and history; humanity; and common values. The programme was launched on 26 October 2016 and works within five main subjects, including strengthening the government’s role, the role of the family and young people, preventing intolerance and extremism, inspiring scientific and cultural ideas, and contributing to international efforts. It supports UAE Vision 2021, with initiatives such as the Week of Tolerance and the Emirates Centre for Tolerance.

Tasks: (a) Write a research paper about tolerance as mentioned in the United Nations Charter, the Universal Declaration of Human Rights and the UNESCO Declaration of Principles on Tolerance, and present it. (b) Analyse the role of tolerance in facilitating international relations. (c) Study the National Programme for Tolerance and prepare a poster. Activity 6: The book says dealing with international humanitarian problems is an international moral imperative. Launch this as a hashtag on a social media platform and ask classmates and teachers to comment.
