CONCEPT CARD

Recognising other’s points of view

A point of view is the way a person sees something. The two seagulls had different views about a home. Samantha and Jackie had different ideas for a dream house. We can understand others by listening and look for an agreement that makes both happy.

We understand others when we listen to their point of view.

Page 23 · Activity 1

Key Vocabulary: sympathy, empathy, point of view.

This activity will help you understand how different problems can be seen and solved in different ways.

Distribute yourselves equally over the four corners of the classroom. Imagine you are on a boat. You should move in parallel, otherwise the boat will sink. Take a selection of objects and pass them to the person facing you, in order to maintain the balance of the boat.

Did you manage to prevent the boat from capsizing? How?

Page 23–24 · Activity 2

Read the story The Two Little Seagulls and note their different opinions. Then do the activities that follow.

One winter, two little seagulls met on a branch of an old olive tree.

The first seagull shook its tail and said, ‘I am tired of moving around and have lost hope of finding a warm place to stay. As soon as we get used to one home, the cold weather and the rain force us to fly away in search of a new home.’

The second seagull laughed and said, ‘We all feel that way! We seagulls are always on the move and our homes are never permanent.’

The first seagull said, ‘I dream of a homeland and an identity. You have no idea how much I want to have a permanent home and address.’

Page 24 · Activity 2

The first seagull was quiet for a few seconds. Then he continued, ‘Look at this tree. I think it’s more than a hundred years old. It is firmly rooted as if it is part of the earth. It may die of sadness if it’s moved to another place because it wholeheartedly loves its homeland.’

The second seagull said, ‘I do wonder about your way of thinking! Are you comparing a seagull to a tree? You know that every creature has its own special nature, making it different from other creatures, so why do you want to change the laws of life and the universe? We birds fly and travel, crossing forests, seas, mountains, valleys and rivers. Our natural homeland has no borders.’

The first seagull replied, ‘I understand. I understand, but I need an identity, an address and a homeland. I don’t think you understand me.’

The second seagull looked around and saw a black cloud coming their way, and shouted, ‘Come on! Come on! Let’s go before the storm and rain reach us! We have wasted a lot of time.’

The first seagull replied, ‘Listen to me. What do you think about staying in this tree? It seems strong enough to survive the storm.’

The second seagull replied firmly, ‘That’s enough daydreaming. I will fly away and leave you.’

Page 25 · Activity 2

1. Rewrite the conversation between the two seagulls to show understanding of both viewpoints. First seagull: I am tired of moving around and have lost hope of finding a warm place to stay. Second seagull: ______

2. Which seagull do you agree with? Explain why, giving reasons for your opinion. I agree with seagull: ______

Page 26 · Activity 3

Sometimes, because of different interests or ways of looking at things, friends can argue with each other. Read what happened between Samantha and Jackie. Then do the activities that follow.

In a school in Miami, USA, the art teacher asked Samantha and Jackie to design a dream house together. The two girls thought of different ideas. Samantha wanted a big house with a garden and pool. Jackie preferred an apartment in a tall building overlooking the sea.

3. How can Samantha and Jackie come to an agreement that they are both happy with?

4. Work in pairs. Discuss your ideas with your partner. Do you agree with each other? Try to come up with an idea that you are both happy with.

5. When people don’t agree on something, how do you work out who is right and who is wrong?

Page 27–28 · Activities 4 and 5

Activity 4. Work in groups. What do you think about the two views of rain? Give reasons for your answer.

Rain is a blessing to humans, animals and plants. It brings life to everything.

At certain times, rain can be a curse on people. It can cause floods, car accidents or drowning.

Activity 5. Work in groups. Make a list of the reasons you think social media is: a good thing; a bad thing. Remember to support your ideas with examples. When you are ready, take turns debating with another group. One group will argue it is a good thing, the other will argue it is a bad thing.
