📖 Book text

Student Book Vol. 1 · Original text from the Student Book.

Page 110–111 · Reading 1

What African rivers, mountains and physical geographic features can you name? What resources in Africa can you name? The book’s map lists the Orange River, the Zambezi River, the Nile River, the Niger River, the Sahara Desert, the Kalahari Desert, the Great Rift Valley, Mount Kilimanjaro and the Cape of Good Hope. Warm up: When you think of African resources, what comes to mind? Many of the resources that we use in our everyday lives originate in Africa. List them in the mind map in the appropriate categories: fuels, minerals, foods, raw materials for textiles, precious stones, and special animals and landscapes from Africa.

Page 112–115 · Reading 2

Africa is surrounded by two major oceans, the Indian Ocean on its east and the Atlantic Ocean on its west. Along Africa’s north coast is the Mediterranean Sea. Africa is joined to the Asian continent at the Suez Peninsula, and the Suez Canal connects the Red Sea along the northeast coast of Egypt and Sudan. The book says it was built in the late 19th century to allow ships to pass from the Mediterranean Sea to the Red Sea. Africa contains two major deserts. The Sahara Desert is the world’s largest desert and covers more than 4,800 kilometres from the coast of Western Sahara to the Nile River Valley in Egypt. The other desert is the Kalahari Desert in southern Africa. The Sahel borders the Sahara and stretches down into grassy savannah lands where raising crops and grazing animals is possible. Timbuktu and Djenne are located in the Sahel. The West African coast is divided among various countries; Nigeria is rich in oil resources and is a major exporter of petroleum products, and Lagos is a port city and the biggest city in Nigeria.

Activity 1: Find the Sahara Desert in the map at the beginning of the lesson. List five countries in North Africa that border the Sahara Desert. Activity 2: Use the map to identify major rivers in Africa: the Nile, Congo, Zambezi, Orange, Niger and Senegal. List one city or feature that is nearby or on each river.

Page 116–119 · Reading 3

The Rift Valley and East Africa: the Rift Valley of eastern Africa was created by plate tectonics. Movements of the earth over millions of years caused a split or “rift” in the earth’s crust. Splits in the rock formations revealed layers of geological rock formations that date back hundreds of thousands and millions of years. Geologists and physical anthropologists found ancient human remains in the Rift Valley at Olduvai Gorge in Tanzania and Lake Turkana in Kenya.

Activity 3: Find the Great Rift Valley on the map. List the countries that the Rift Valley runs through. Activity 4: Write one or two sentences describing each picture. Identify the main land formation in each description from the box: fields with farmland, mountain, river valley with waterfall, flat plain.

Page 122–123 · Reading 4

Central Africa is dominated by the Congo Basin. In it are large tropical rain forests as well as one of Africa’s principal river systems, the Congo River. It is an area rich in mineral resources and rare metals. Southern Africa is separated from the Congo Basin and the Katanga Plateau by the Kalahari Desert. It is a rich agricultural and mineral area, with gold and diamond mines. The book’s photographs show a container ship on the Congo River and Johannesburg, the biggest city in South Africa.

Activity 5: Circle the correct answer. You may use the map at the beginning of the lesson. (1) The Sahel is found in West Africa, East Africa, Central Africa or Southern Africa. (2) The Sahel is a grassland savannah, a desert, a coastal plain or a region of little human activity. (3) The Rift Valley is known for diamonds and gold, early signs of human habitation, or tropical rainforests. (4) The Blue Nile and White Nile are two tributaries that join at which capital city: Khartoum or Addis Ababa? (5) Dakar is the capital city of which country: Ethiopia, Nigeria or Senegal? (6) Lagos is the biggest city in which country: Sudan, Senegal or Nigeria?

Page 125 · Reading 5

Activity 6: European explorers and merchants established settlements along the coast of Africa to ship products to sell in other parts of the world. What are some of the products you think were shipped? Write sentences describing products or natural resources found in Africa that would have been new to Europeans in the 17th century.
