📖 Book text 1/4

What are Climate Changes?

Read. Then answer the questions.

Read about climate change. Then, answer the questions that follow.

What are Climate Changes? Climate is the weather measured over a long period of time. Climate changes are noticeable changes in the climate pattern that take place over periods of time.

Sometimes outside forces can have a big impact on the climate.

Humans have had an impact on climate changes by building factories that pollute the air, and cutting down trees in places like the rainforest. This has had an impact on the local climate. Other major events that can affect the climate include volcano eruptions and changes in the Sun.

Scientists have observed that our planet’s surface is getting warmer and getting less precipitation in the recent past. This is called global warming. Greenhouse gases are especially dangerous. Some of the effects of these changes are the melting of the glaciers and rising sea levels caused by global warming.

📖 Book text 2/4

The Greenhouse Effect

Read about the greenhouse effect.

The Greenhouse Effect. The greenhouse effect is the warming caused by greenhouse gases trapping heat near Earth’s surface.

The atmosphere surrounds the planet Earth. It protects us from some dangerous rays that come from the Sun. Sunlight warms Earth’s surface, which then radiates heat. Greenhouse gases absorb and re-emit some of this heat. Some of the energy is reflected back into the space. Some is absorbed and radiated as heat. The heat that stays in the atmosphere warms up the Earth.

This temperature is needed for the plants to grow, and living creatures to survive. Greenhouse gases, including carbon dioxide (CO2), naturally absorb and re-emit heat. Adding more of these gases strengthens this effect and raises Earth’s average surface temperature.

Humans contribute to this effect as well. Burning fuels in factories and vehicles releases extra carbon dioxide into the atmosphere. Burning fossil fuels adds greenhouse gases and contributes to rising average temperatures. Fossil fuels formed from organisms that lived long ago. Fossil fuels include coal, petroleum (oil) and natural gas.

Adapted from Student Book pp. 161–162, with scientific corrections (NASA).

Climate Changes
Read the text (Student Book p. 161–162). A diagram shows the atmosphere, the Sun rays and heat trapped. Ask: Why do we need some warmth? (For plants to grow and living creatures to survive.)

📖 Book text 3/4

How do we know? Do we care?

Read about evidence and our planet.

How do we know that the climate is changing? We know what earth’s past climate was like by studying things that have been around for a long time. For example, scientists can study what earth’s climate was like hundreds of years ago by studying the insides of trees that have been alive since then. Another way to tell what the climate was like in the past is to study sediment cores and ice cores. Sediment is matter that settles to the bottom of a liquid.

Scientists can go back millions of years by looking at the samples. Sediment cores come from the bottoms of the lakes and oceans. Ice cores are drilled from deep — sometimes miles — below the surface of the ice in places like Antarctica. Scientists look at these samples and read the layers, studying what the surface had gone through in a particular period.

Do we care if Earth is getting warmer? Yes, we care! After all, imagine the earth as our spaceship. It carries us on a 583-million-mile cruise around the sun every year. It even has its own “force field.” Earth has a magnetic field (an invisible shield) that protects us from killer radiation and brutal solar wind. The earth has all the air, water, and food humans, animals, and plants need for its life-support system.

Just like the astronauts on a large space voyage, we need to monitor all our “ship’s” vital functions and keep our earth “ship shape.” This is the only spaceship we have and we can’t change the spaceship. We have to improve it, to make sure it’s running ok.
