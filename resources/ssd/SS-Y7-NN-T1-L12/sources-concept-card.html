Page 135–138 · Reading 1

The book’s timeline: Xia Dynasty 2100 BCE to 1600 BCE; Shang Dynasty 1600 BCE to 1050 BCE; introduction of the chariot 1300 BCE; Zhou Dynasty 1046 BCE to 256 BCE; Laozi and Taoism 604 BCE; life of Confucius 551 BCE to 497 BCE; Warring States Period 403 BCE to 221 BCE; Qin Dynasty 221 BCE to 206 BCE; the Great Wall 220 BCE to 1644 CE; Han Dynasty 206 BCE to 220 CE; introduction of Buddhism to China about 100 BCE to 100 CE. […] Confucius was one of the world’s great ancient philosophers. His teachings were written down by his students as the Analects. The Qin Dynasty was ruled by China’s first emperor, Qin Shihuangdi, who died in 210 BCE. […] The best known and most substantial portions of the Great Wall were built during the Ming Dynasty, 1368 to 1644.

Questions: (a) Who was Laozi? (b) What did Confucius teach? (c) When did the Qin Dynasty exist? (d) What is the Great Wall?

Page 139–141 · Reading 2

Activity 2: Look at the images and answer the questions. Image: a bell from the Eastern Zhou Dynasty (475 BCE to 221 BCE). (a) What do you think the bell was used for? (b) What does this bell show about the development of metalworking in ancient China? Image: the end of the Grand Canal in Beijing (1900). (c) How do you think workers dug the canal? (d) Why do you think the Grand Canal was extended to Beijing?

Page 141–143 · Reading 3

Activity 3: Look at the images and answer the questions. Image: the Grand Canal, a UNESCO world heritage site, in Hangzhou, China. (a) Why do you think the bridge in Hangzhou is arched so high above the canal? (b) Why is the Grand Canal known as the longest artificial river in the world? Image: the terracotta army, part of the first Qin Emperor’s mausoleum, in Xi’an. (c) What does the terracotta army indicate about the Qin Emperor’s view of his power?

Activity 4: Write the meaning of the words in the table. Use a dictionary if you need to: mausoleum, terracotta, world heritage site, dynasty.

Page 144–145 · Reading 4

Activity 5: Confucius was a teacher and a philosopher in ancient China. The teachings of Confucius emphasized moral behaviour of people founded on the oldest and most respected traditions of Chinese society. His teachings were written down by his students and are collected together in a work called The Analects. Read some excerpts of Jen, meaning humaneness. In one, a student asks whether there is one word which may serve as a rule of practice for all one’s life, and the teacher answers with reciprocity: what you do not want done to yourself, do not do to others. In another, the teacher says virtue is not left to stand alone, and those who practise it will have neighbours.

Questions: (a) Make a note of any words you do not know and research their meaning. (b) Choose one quote from Jen (humaneness). What do you think it means?

Page 146–147 · Reading 5

Activity 6: Possible interpretations of each of the quotes are listed. Match each with a possible interpretation. Examples include: each person is known by the friends he or she keeps; it is better to die with dignity than to live in shame; do to others as you want others to do to you. What other interpretations can you think of? Activity 7: Write the word from the box next to the information: first Qin Emperor, Confucius, humaneness and virtue, Analects. Information: essential characteristics of a civilized person; his tomb had thousands of terracotta warriors; the title of a collection of sayings by Confucius; a philosopher who lived in China in the 5th century BCE.
