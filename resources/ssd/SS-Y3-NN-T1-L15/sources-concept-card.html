📖 Book text

Student Book Vol. 1 · Original text from the Student Book.

Page 158–161 · Reading 1

How can we create clean energy in the UAE? How can people make farming better in the UAE?

Activity 1: Is sunlight a natural resource? Why or why not? Is wind a natural resource? Why or why not? Work in groups.

Page 161 · Reading 2

The world has many people but has few natural resources. We need to use them without harming the earth. Different countries are using their natural resources to get clean energy. Lots of wind comes over the Atlantic Ocean. Companies in Ireland are building wind turbines near the coast. Wind makes the wind turbines turn, which creates electricity. This electricity is used for businesses and homes. Wind energy does not pollute.

Science check: Wind turbines generate electricity without burning fuel. Building and operating them still has environmental effects, including noise and effects on wildlife.

The UAE gets a lot of sunlight. Two world leading solar plants are in the UAE. In Abu Dhabi, there is Noor Abu Dhabi. In Dubai, there is the Mohammed bin Rashid Al Maktoum Solar Power Park. These two plants make electricity in a clean way for the UAE.

Page 162 · Reading 3

A resource that we must use better is farmland. When cities grow, people often put new buildings on farmland. This destroys the land and we cannot grow food on it. There have been many improvements in farming in the UAE. In 1971, there were about 4,000 farms. In 2011, there were about 36,000 farms. Modern irrigation systems put water right next to the plants so that no water is wasted.

Science check: Efficient irrigation reduces water loss; it does not eliminate every loss. Results depend on design and management.

Page 163 · Reading 4

In 1999, 32% of farms in the UAE used a modern irrigation system. In 2011, 91% of farms used a modern irrigation system. A recent idea is indoor farming using vertical farming. Vertical means going up rather than across. The plants are on shelves inside the greenhouse. The farmer can control the temperature and the water used. Little or no soil is needed. The farm produces 1,000 to 1,500 kilograms of food every day. This saves farmland, soil, and water.

Reading carefully: The daily harvest figure describes the farm in this reading. It is not the output of every vertical farm; output depends on the crop and farm size.

Page 164–171 · Reading 5

Activity 3: Use information from the text to answer the questions. Why do we need to use natural resources in a clean way? What country uses wind turbines? How does a wind turbine make electricity? What natural resource is the UAE using to create electricity? Why do modern irrigation systems save water? What kind of new farm did the reading talk about?

Activity 4: Make graphs that explain changes in farming in the UAE. Draw a graph to show the percentage of farms that use a modern irrigation system in 1999 and 2011.

Activity 5: Choose your favourite new idea for using resources from this lesson. Draw the resource being used. Describe why you like the idea and how it will help people.
