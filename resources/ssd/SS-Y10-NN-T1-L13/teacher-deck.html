📖 Book text 2/6

Why study history? Learning from the past

Read and explain.

Why study what happened in the past at all? How can it benefit us today or in the future? Do events that happened hundreds or thousands of years ago influence our lives in the present day? […] Academic fields need to have a purpose and our study of them needs to have a practical use for us.

“Those who cannot remember the past are condemned to repeat it.” The Spanish philosopher and poet George Santayana wrote this famous quotation in his book The Life of Reason: The Phases of Human Progress in 1905-1906. What do you think he meant? One interpretation of the quote is that if we do not understand what our past mistakes are and learn from them, then we will make the same mistake again.

In order to understand our current social, cultural, and political environment, we need to understand how it has developed through history […]. The job of the historian is to discover these historical facts and preserve them to give us a better-rounded picture of the evolution of global civilisation. Perceptions change over time. For example, in ancient times, a civilisation expanded into new territories by conquering and, often, enslaving the people who were living there. […] Now, an unprovoked military advance into another country is considered a violation of human rights and a violation of international law.

📖 Book text 3/6

Branches of history, and palaeontology

Read and list.

How do we study history? What tools and procedures do historians use? What do scientists do to discover, interpret, and preserve historical objects and texts? The study of history has several branches. Scientists and historians work in specific fields of activity to make sense of the past.

Palaeontology looks the deepest and furthest into the past. It is concerned with discovering and cataloguing plant, animal, and human fossils from millions of years ago. Palaeontologists travel all over the world to locate fossils, gradually developing a picture of what life was like at different points in time. […] Bones and other organic matter may be processed using radiocarbon dating. The radiometric dating process examines the level of certain chemical particles in bones and fossils to determine how long they have existed. This process helps scientists narrow down the period in which animals or ancient people lived.

📖 Book text 4/6

Archaeology

Read and explain.

This field is specifically concerned with the development of human civilisation and culture. Archaeologists travel to areas where ancient civilisations are known to have existed and dig for human artefacts, such as dishes, tools, building materials, and weapons.

After discovering these artefacts, the job of the archaeologist is to classify them into various time periods and civilisations of human history. Archaeologists use actual human products to support and extend existing textual information and provide some context to textual descriptions and explanations of historical events.

In this way, the impacts of events such as the eruption of the volcano Mt. Vesuvius in 79 CE can be evidenced in a more direct and accurate way. […] The ruins of Pompeii, preserved in lava from the eruption of Mt. Vesuvius 2000 years ago and now a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

📖 Book text 5/6

Anthropology and modern technology

Read and apply.

Archaeology is essentially a branch of anthropology, the study of humanity. Anthropologists look at how humans have evolved through time from a social as well as a physical perspective. They examine and classify human artefacts to understand human societies and behaviours.

Anthropologists look at how human communication has developed by studying the evolution of languages, a branch of anthropology called philology. They also study human cultures and social groups, to gain a clearer understanding of how civilisations emerge, flourish, spread, and decline. Some other areas of anthropological research include art history, demographics, and cultural psychology.

It is important for historians and scientists from multiple areas of specialisation to coordinate their efforts to identify gaps in knowledge. […] For example, virtual reality simulators can be used to turn ancient ruins into active, living communities. Satellite imagery can show us new areas to dig, and in incredible detail. Ground-penetrating radar machines can pinpoint the exact locations of buried objects in a way that saves more time and money than ever before.

Let’s talk

💬 Let’s talk – answer with a full sentence.

1. Why do we study history?

To understand how our present developed and to learn from the past.

2. What does an archaeologist do?

Digs for human artefacts and classifies them into time periods.

3. Name one modern tool historians use.

Satellite imagery, virtual reality simulators or ground-penetrating radar.
