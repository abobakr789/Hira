Silver

1: ⚔️ Badr (2 AH): About 313 Muslims faced a much larger Makkan army and won. «Allah had already given you victory at Badr while you were few» (3:123). · 🌟 Its meaning: Badr gave the Muslims confidence and showed Allah's support. · 🏹 Uhud (3 AH): The archers were ordered not to leave the hill. When some left to collect spoils, the Makkan cavalry attacked from behind (3:152). · 💔 Its lesson: Many Muslims were killed, including the Prophet's ﷺ uncle Hamzah: obedience and discipline matter. · 🕳️ The Trench (5 AH): A huge confederate army came to destroy Madinah. On Salman al-Farisi's advice, the Muslims dug a trench and the Prophet ﷺ dug with them. · 🌬️ The end of the siege: After weeks of siege, Allah sent «a wind and armies you did not see» (33:9) and the confederates withdrew. · 🏰 Khaybar (7 AH): The fortresses of Khaybar had become a base for those plotting against Madinah. The Muslims besieged and took them one by one. · 🚩 Ali: The Prophet ﷺ gave the banner to Ali, and the final fortress fell (Bukhari). The people of Khaybar were allowed to stay and farm the land.
2 (2 marks): Any two: obey the leader · discipline · do not be distracted by worldly gain · patience after loss.
3 (3 marks): In 7 AH the Muslims marched to Khaybar, a fortified oasis that had become a base for those plotting against Madinah. They besieged the fortresses and took them one by one; the Prophet ﷺ gave the banner to Ali and the final fortress fell. The people of Khaybar were allowed to stay and farm the land in return for a share of the harvest.
4 (3 marks): A large confederate army came to attack Madinah in 5 AH (1); on Salman al-Farisi's advice the Muslims dug a trench, and the Prophet ﷺ worked with them (1); after a long siege, a strong wind and Allah's help made the confederates withdraw (33:9) (1).

Gold

1–2 (model answer): After years of persecution Allah gave permission to fight in defence (22:39), so the battles were a response to wrong done to the Muslims, not aggression. They protected the community's freedom to worship, for example defending Madinah at the Trench. Some battles responded to broken treaties and plotting, such as Khaybar, so the Prophet ﷺ fought to keep the community safe — always under rules that forbade harming women and children.

Diamond

Indicative content: Agree: verse 39 — prophets «fear none but Allah»: persecution tested and strengthened faith (Bilal's «Ahad, Ahad»; Sumayyah's sacrifice); hardship bound the believers together and prepared them for Madinah. Other view: the opposition also caused suffering and loss — Sumayyah was killed and some Muslims had to leave for Abyssinia; verse 38 shows that strength comes from following «the established way of Allah», not from persecution itself; Allah's help and the Prophet's ﷺ leadership kept them firm. Conclusion: a justified judgement, e.g. opposition revealed and deepened their faith, but it was faith in Allah, not the opposition itself, that made them strong. Mark with the Level 1–3 descriptors (Level 3 needs the text, another view and a justified conclusion).
