Silver

1: 🏛️ Nature: The third pillar: 2.5% of savings held above the nisab for one lunar year, paid every year. · 🧼 Purification: «Take from their wealth a charity by which you purify them» (9:103): it cleans wealth and the heart from greed. · 👥 The eight groups: The poor, the needy, collectors, reconciling hearts, freeing captives, debtors, the cause of Allah, travellers (9:60). · 🤲 Purpose: Wealth belongs to Allah; Zakah reduces poverty and narrows the gap between rich and poor. · 🖐️ Nature: «One fifth» (8:41): Shi'a Muslims pay 20% of their surplus income after a year's expenses. · 📚 Two shares: Sahm al-Imam: for religious work, scholars and schools; Sahm as-Sadat: for poor descendants of the Prophet ﷺ. · 🧭 Paid through: Usually given to a qualified religious scholar (marja') to distribute. · 🎯 Purpose: Purifies wealth, supports religious learning and cares for those in need.
2 (2 marks): Any one: it is commanded by Allah · it purifies wealth · it helps the poor · it removes greed · for reward.
3 (3 marks): Khums means 'one fifth' and is paid by Shi'a Muslims based on Qur'an 8:41. It is 20% of surplus income left after a year's expenses. It is divided into the share of the Imam for religious work and the share of the poor descendants of the Prophet ﷺ.
4 (3 marks): Zakah is the third pillar and is compulsory (1); it is 2.5% of savings above the nisab held for a lunar year (1); it is given to the eight groups in 9:60, such as the poor and the needy (1).

Gold

1–2 (model answer): Muslims believe the Ummah is like one body, so they help Muslims in need wherever they are, sending Zakah to famine and war zones. Supporting those in need also saves lives and reduces poverty around the world, which is a duty in Islam. Giving sadaqah to all people, Muslim or not, shows the mercy of Islam and brings people together.

Diamond

Indicative content: Agree: verse 39 — prophets «fear none but Allah»: persecution tested and strengthened faith (Bilal's «Ahad, Ahad»; Sumayyah's sacrifice); hardship bound the believers together and prepared them for Madinah. Other view: the opposition also caused suffering and loss — Sumayyah was killed and some Muslims had to leave for Abyssinia; verse 38 shows that strength comes from following «the established way of Allah», not from persecution itself; Allah's help and the Prophet's ﷺ leadership kept them firm. Conclusion: a justified judgement, e.g. opposition revealed and deepened their faith, but it was faith in Allah, not the opposition itself, that made them strong. Mark with the Level 1–3 descriptors (Level 3 needs the text, another view and a justified conclusion).
