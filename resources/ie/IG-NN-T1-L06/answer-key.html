Silver

1: ⚖️ Equal before the law: When a noble woman stole, he refused to let her off: «If Fatimah, the daughter of Muhammad, stole, I would cut off her hand» (Bukhari). · 💰 Zakah: A fixed share of wealth goes to the poor as their right, not a favour. · 🚫 No riba: Interest that exploits the needy was forbidden; fair weights and measures were commanded. · 🧕 The weak: He cared for orphans, slaves and widows, and freed and honoured former slaves such as Bilal. · ❤️ Life, property, honour: In the Farewell Sermon: «your blood, your property and your honour are sacred to one another» (Bukhari). · 🌍 No racism: No Arab is better than a non-Arab, nor white than black, except by taqwa (Farewell Sermon, Ahmad). · 👧 Right to life: The Qur'an condemned burying baby girls alive (81:8–9). · 🕊️ Freedom of belief: «There shall be no compulsion in religion» (2:256).
2 (2 marks): Any two: zakah for the poor · forbidding riba · fair weights and measures · care for orphans.
3 (3 marks): In Madinah he agreed the Charter of Madinah, which gave Jews and other groups rights and duties as one community. He was kind to people of other faiths, for example visiting a sick Jewish boy. He respected them, standing up for a Jewish funeral because «it is a soul».
4 (3 marks): When asked who deserves the best treatment he said «your mother» three times, then «your father» (1); this shows mothers have the greatest right to kindness (1); serving parents is a way to Paradise and part of worship (1).

Gold

1–2 (model answer): His equal treatment before the law shows Muslims believe society must apply rules fairly to rich and poor, which builds trust. His teaching on zakah shows that wealth carries a duty to others, so a Muslim society works to remove poverty. His treatment of other faiths in the Charter of Madinah shows that a Muslim society should protect the rights of minorities, so people can live together in peace.

Diamond

Indicative content: Agree: verse 39 — prophets «fear none but Allah»: persecution tested and strengthened faith (Bilal's «Ahad, Ahad»; Sumayyah's sacrifice); hardship bound the believers together and prepared them for Madinah. Other view: the opposition also caused suffering and loss — Sumayyah was killed and some Muslims had to leave for Abyssinia; verse 38 shows that strength comes from following «the established way of Allah», not from persecution itself; Allah's help and the Prophet's ﷺ leadership kept them firm. Conclusion: a justified judgement, e.g. opposition revealed and deepened their faith, but it was faith in Allah, not the opposition itself, that made them strong. Mark with the Level 1–3 descriptors (Level 3 needs the text, another view and a justified conclusion).
