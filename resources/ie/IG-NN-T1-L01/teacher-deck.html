[At-Tahrim 66:1–3]

(1) O Prophet, why do you prohibit [yourself from] what Allah has made lawful for you, seeking the approval of your wives? And Allah is Forgiving and Merciful.

(a) Birth and early influences

Spec 1.1 a

⏱ 8 min

He was born in Makkah, in the Year of the Elephant (about 570 CE), into Banu Hashim of Quraysh.

👶 Orphan

His father Abdullah died before his birth; his mother Aminah died when he was six.

🧓 Guardians

His grandfather Abd al-Muttalib cared for him, then his uncle Abu Talib, who protected him all his life.

🐑 Shepherd

He worked as a shepherd as a young man — learning patience and care for the weak.

⛺ Bahira

On a trade journey to Syria with Abu Talib, the monk Bahira saw signs of prophethood in the boy and told Abu Talib to protect him (Tirmidhi).

(a) Work, marriage and family

Spec 1.1 a

⏱ 8 min

💼 Trader

He traded honestly for Khadijah and became known as al-Sadiq al-Amin (the truthful, the trustworthy).

💍 Khadijah

Impressed by his honesty, Khadijah proposed marriage; he was 25. She was his only wife until her death.

👨‍👧 Children

With Khadijah: al-Qasim, Zaynab, Ruqayyah, Umm Kulthum, Fatimah and Abdullah. Later, Ibrahim was born to Mariyah. The sons died in childhood.

🏠 At home

«He used to be in the service of his family» — A'ishah (Bukhari). «The best of you is the best to his family» (Tirmidhi).

(b) Why his early life mattered

Spec 1.1 b

⏱ 8 min

🤝 Trust

Even opponents knew he never lied, so they could not easily reject his message as a lie.

💛 Compassion

Being an orphan helped him understand the poor, orphans and the weak.

As husband and father he showed Muslims how to live family life with kindness.

Point → develop → link

⏱ 8 min

(c) Explain the importance of the Prophet's ﷺ relationship with his wives and children. (6)

Point 1 + why

He treated his wives with kindness and helped at home, as A'ishah reported. This is important because it shows Muslims that serving one's family is part of good character, not beneath a leader.

Point 2 + why

He loved his children, especially Fatimah, and grieved for those who died. This matters because it teaches Muslim parents to show love and mercy, and that sadness is not a lack of faith.

Point 3 + why

Even Allah's guidance in 66:1–3 shows his family life was a model. This is important because the Sunnah of the home guides Muslims today: «the best of you is the best to his family».

Plan a (d) answer: June 2023, Q1(d)

12 marks · four elements

⏱ 6 min

Assess the view that Muhammad's (pbuh) early life was of great importance in preparing him as a messenger of Allah. Refer to Qur'an 66:1–3.

✅ Agree (with the text)

❌ Another point of view

His honesty earned the titles al-Sadiq al-Amin, so people could trust his message.

Prophethood is a choice and gift from Allah, not something earned by experience.

66:1–3 shows his family life was guided and gentle — experience as a husband prepared him to lead and teach.

Revelation (from age 40) and Jibrail's teaching shaped his mission more than his early life.

⚖️ Conclusion: His early life prepared his character and won people's trust, but it was Allah's choice and revelation that made him a messenger.

Model (d) answer · Level 3

Read the tags

⏱ 6 min

Text + agree

Many Muslims agree. His early life built the character a messenger needs: he was known as al-Sadiq al-Amin, so even his enemies could not call him a liar. Qur'an 66:1–3 shows his care for his wives and his gentleness, «he made known part of it and ignored a part», qualities he later used to lead his community.

Reason

As an orphan raised by Abu Talib he understood the weak, and as a trader he learned honesty and how to deal with people. These experiences explain why his teaching on orphans and fair trade was so practical.

Other view

However, others argue that prophethood is not prepared by human experience. Allah chooses His messengers, and it was the revelation in the cave of Hira, not his childhood, that made him a prophet.

Reason

Also, many people have similar early lives without becoming prophets, so experience alone cannot explain his mission.

Conclusion

Overall, his early life was important in preparing his character and making people trust him, but it was Allah's choice and revelation that made him the Messenger of Allah. So the statement is partly true.
