Silver

1: 👶 Orphan: His father Abdullah died before his birth; his mother Aminah died when he was six. · 🧓 Guardians: His grandfather Abd al-Muttalib cared for him, then his uncle Abu Talib, who protected him all his life. · 🐑 Shepherd: He worked as a shepherd as a young man — learning patience and care for the weak. · ⛺ Bahira: On a trade journey to Syria with Abu Talib, the monk Bahira saw signs of prophethood in the boy and told Abu Talib to protect him (Tirmidhi). · 💼 Trader: He traded honestly for Khadijah and became known as al-Sadiq al-Amin (the truthful, the trustworthy). · 💍 Khadijah: Impressed by his honesty, Khadijah proposed marriage; he was 25. She was his only wife until her death. · 👨‍👧 Children: With Khadijah: al-Qasim, Zaynab, Ruqayyah, Umm Kulthum, Fatimah and Abdullah. Later, Ibrahim was born to Mariyah. The sons died in childhood. · 🏠 At home: «He used to be in the service of his family» — A'ishah (Bukhari). «The best of you is the best to his family» (Tirmidhi).
2 (2 marks): Any two: al-Qasim · Zaynab · Ruqayyah · Umm Kulthum · Fatimah · Abdullah · Ibrahim.
3 (3 marks): As a boy, Muhammad ﷺ travelled with his uncle Abu Talib on a trade caravan to Syria. The Christian monk Bahira noticed signs of prophethood in him. He advised Abu Talib to take good care of the boy and protect him.
4 (3 marks): He took him into his care after Abd al-Muttalib died (1); he took him on trade journeys such as the one to Syria (1); after the revelation he protected him from Quraysh even though he did not accept Islam (1).

Gold

1–2 (model answer): As a trader for Khadijah he was honest, so he became known as al-Amin; this mattered because people trusted him when he later brought the message. His travels and business taught him how to deal with all kinds of people, which helped him later lead a community. Trade also showed him unfair practices such as cheating, so his later teachings on fair weights and honest trade came from real experience.

Diamond

Indicative content: Agree: verse 39 — prophets «fear none but Allah»: persecution tested and strengthened faith (Bilal's «Ahad, Ahad»; Sumayyah's sacrifice); hardship bound the believers together and prepared them for Madinah. Other view: the opposition also caused suffering and loss — Sumayyah was killed and some Muslims had to leave for Abyssinia; verse 38 shows that strength comes from following «the established way of Allah», not from persecution itself; Allah's help and the Prophet's ﷺ leadership kept them firm. Conclusion: a justified judgement, e.g. opposition revealed and deepened their faith, but it was faith in Allah, not the opposition itself, that made them strong. Mark with the Level 1–3 descriptors (Level 3 needs the text, another view and a justified conclusion).
