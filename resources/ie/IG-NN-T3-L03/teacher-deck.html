Key terms

Use them in every answer

⏱ 4 min

Sawm

Fasting from dawn to sunset: the fourth pillar.

Ramadan

The ninth month, in which the Qur'an was revealed (2:185).

Suhur

The meal before dawn.

Iftar

Breaking the fast at sunset.

Taqwa

God-consciousness: the purpose of fasting (2:183).

Fidyah

Feeding a poor person for each day missed by those who cannot fast.

Qada'

Making up missed fasts later.

Laylat al-Qadr

The Night of Power, in the last ten nights of Ramadan.

[Al-Baqarah 2:183–185]

(183) O you who have believed, decreed upon you is fasting as it was decreed upon those before you that you may become righteous -

(184) [Fasting for] a limited number of days. So whoever among you is ill or on a journey [during them] - then an equal number of other days [are to be made up]. And upon those who are able [to fast, but with hardship] - a ransom [as substitute] of feeding a poor person [each day]. And whoever volunteers good [i.e., excess] - it is better for him. But to fast is best for you, if you only knew.

(185) The month of Ramaḍān [is that] in which was revealed the Qur’ān, a guidance for the people and clear proofs of guidance and criterion. So whoever sights [the crescent of] the month, let him fast it; and whoever is ill or on a journey - then an equal number of other days. Allāh intends for you ease and does not intend for you hardship and [wants] for you to complete the period and to glorify Allāh for that [to] which He has guided you; and perhaps you will be grateful.

(a) Nature, role, significance and purpose

Spec 3.3 a

⏱ 8 min

🌙 Nature

No food, drink or marital relations from dawn to sunset for the month of Ramadan: the fourth pillar.

🎯 Purpose

«…that you may become righteous» (2:183): taqwa and self-control.

🤝 Significance

Feeling hunger builds empathy with the poor; families and mosques share iftar.

✨ Reward

«Whoever fasts Ramadan out of faith and hoping for reward, his past sins are forgiven» (Bukhari, Muslim).

4IS1 · 3.3 Sawm

Link to the Qur'an: Ramadan is the month the Qur'an was revealed (2:185).

(b) Those who are exempt

Spec 3.3 b

⏱ 8 min

🤒 The ill

May break the fast and make it up later (2:184).

✈️ Travellers

May break the fast and make it up later (2:185).

👴 The elderly / chronically ill

Who cannot fast feed a poor person for each day (fidyah, 2:184).

🤰 Pregnant or breastfeeding

May leave the fast if it harms them or the baby, and make it up.

🩸 Menstruation

Women do not fast and make up the days later (Muslim).

🧒 Children

Not required before puberty, but often practise.

«Allah intends for you ease and does not intend for you hardship» (2:185).

(c) Laylat al-Qadr for Muslims today

Spec 3.3 c

⏱ 8 min

📖 The night of revelation

The Qur'an was first revealed on this night (97:1).

⏳ Better than 1000 months

Worship on it is worth more than a lifetime of worship (97:3).

🤲 Forgiveness

«Whoever stands in prayer on Laylat al-Qadr out of faith and hoping for reward, his past sins are forgiven» (Bukhari, Muslim).

🕌 Today

Muslims seek it in the last ten nights: extra prayer, Qur'an, du'a and i'tikaf in the mosque.

We do: a (c) Explain question (6 marks)

Point → develop → link

⏱ 8 min

(c) Explain the importance of Laylat al-Qadr for Muslims today. (6) — June 2025

Point 1 + why

Laylat al-Qadr is the night the Qur'an was first revealed (97:1). This is important today because it reminds Muslims to reconnect with the Qur'an by reading and studying it.

Point 2 + why

It is «better than a thousand months» (97:3). This motivates Muslims to spend the last ten nights in extra prayer and du'a, seeking a reward greater than a lifetime.

Point 3 + why

Those who pray on it with faith are forgiven their past sins (Bukhari, Muslim). This gives Muslims today hope and a fresh start, strengthening their faith.

Plan a (d) answer: June 2024

12 marks · four elements

⏱ 6 min

«All Muslims should observe the fast during Ramadan.» Assess this statement. Refer to Qur'an 2:183–185.

✅ Agree (with the text)

❌ Another point of view

2:183: «fasting is prescribed for you» — it is the fourth pillar and compulsory.

2:184–185: the ill and travellers are excused; the elderly feed the poor instead.

It builds taqwa, self-control and empathy with the poor.

«Allah intends for you ease»: children, pregnant and menstruating women are excused.

⚖️ Conclusion: Fasting is compulsory for every adult Muslim who is able, but Allah Himself excuses those for whom it would be a hardship, so 'all' is too strong.

Plan in a T-table first (3 minutes in the exam). Every side needs reasons; the text must appear.

Model (d) answer · Level 3

Read the tags

⏱ 6 min

Text + agree

Many Muslims would agree. Qur'an 2:183 says «fasting is prescribed for you», and it is the fourth pillar of Islam, so every adult Muslim is commanded to fast.

Reason

Fasting builds taqwa, «that you may become righteous», teaches self-control and helps Muslims feel the hunger of the poor.

Other view

However, the same passage gives exemptions: «whoever is ill or on a journey» makes up the days later, and those who cannot fast feed a poor person (2:184).

Reason

Verse 185 says «Allah intends for you ease and does not intend for you hardship», so children, pregnant women and women during menstruation are also excused.

Conclusion

Overall, fasting is compulsory for every adult Muslim who is able, but Allah Himself excuses those for whom it would be a hardship. So the word 'all' makes the statement too strong.
