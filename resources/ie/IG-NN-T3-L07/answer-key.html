Silver

1: 📅 Origins: Celebrated on 1 Shawwal, at the end of Ramadan, the month of the Qur'an (2:185). · 💰 Before the prayer: Zakat al-Fitr is paid so the poor can celebrate too. · 🕌 Activities: Takbir, the Eid prayer then the khutbah, new clothes, greeting «Eid mubarak». · 👨‍👩‍👧 Family: Visiting relatives, gifts for children, shared meals — fasting on Eid is forbidden (Bukhari). · 📅 Origins: Remembers Ibrahim's willingness to sacrifice his son, and Allah's ransom «with a great sacrifice» (37:102–107). · 🕋 Timing: 10 Dhul-Hijjah, the day after Arafah, while pilgrims are at Mina. · 🐑 The sacrifice: A sheep, goat, cow or camel is sacrificed; the meat is shared with family, friends and the poor. · 🕌 Activities: Eid prayer and khutbah, takbir, visiting family, charity.
2 (2 marks): Any two: paying Zakat al-Fitr · the Eid prayer · listening to the khutbah · takbir · visiting family · gifts.
3 (3 marks): It is celebrated on 10 Dhul-Hijjah with the Eid prayer and a khutbah. Muslims sacrifice an animal, such as a sheep, and share the meat with family, friends and the poor. They visit family, give gifts and charity and say the takbir.
4 (3 marks): It remembers Ibrahim's willingness to sacrifice his son when Allah commanded him (1); Allah ransomed the son «with a great sacrifice» (37:107) (1); it is celebrated on 10 Dhul-Hijjah during Hajj (1).

Gold

1–2 (model answer): The Eids are a way of thanking Allah after completing worship: Eid al-Fitr after Ramadan and Eid al-Adha during Hajj. They unite the Ummah, as Zakat al-Fitr and the shared meat let the poor celebrate too. They also remember events of faith — Ibrahim's sacrifice and Allah saving Musa — teaching obedience and sacrifice.

Diamond

Indicative content: Agree: verse 39 — prophets «fear none but Allah»: persecution tested and strengthened faith (Bilal's «Ahad, Ahad»; Sumayyah's sacrifice); hardship bound the believers together and prepared them for Madinah. Other view: the opposition also caused suffering and loss — Sumayyah was killed and some Muslims had to leave for Abyssinia; verse 38 shows that strength comes from following «the established way of Allah», not from persecution itself; Allah's help and the Prophet's ﷺ leadership kept them firm. Conclusion: a justified judgement, e.g. opposition revealed and deepened their faith, but it was faith in Allah, not the opposition itself, that made them strong. Mark with the Level 1–3 descriptors (Level 3 needs the text, another view and a justified conclusion).
