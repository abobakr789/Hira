The noble hadith

Read & understand

Tamim ad-Dari (may Allah be pleased with him) reported that the Prophet ﷺ said:

«الدِّينُ النَّصِيحَةُ»، قُلْنَا: لِمَنْ؟ قَالَ: «لِلَّهِ، وَلِكِتَابِهِ، وَلِرَسُولِهِ، وَلِأَئِمَّةِ الْمُسْلِمِينَ وَعَامَّتِهِمْ»

“Religion is sincere advice.” We asked: “To whom?” He said: “To Allah, His Book, His Messenger, the leaders of the Muslims and their common people.”

Reported by Muslim

The narrator

Tamim ad-Dari, a noble Companion who became Muslim in 9 AH.

“To whom?”

The Companions’ question teaches us good manners in learning.

Teaching style

A short statement first, then explanation, to catch attention.

Key words

Word treasure

النَّصيحَة

Nasihah

Wanting good for others sincerely, in word and action.

الْخالِصَة

Khalisah

Pure – free from deception and showing off.

الدّين

Ad-Din

Islam: belief, worship, dealings and manners.

أَئِمَّةُ الْمُسْلِمين

A’immat al-Muslimin

Leaders and scholars who guide the Ummah.

عامَّتُهُم

‘Ammatuhum

Ordinary Muslims, other than leaders and scholars.

الْغِشّ

Al-Ghish

Showing good while hiding harm – the opposite of nasihah.

Arabic gems in the hadith

Islamic Education meets Arabic

The root ن ص ح (n-s-h)

Nasaha ath-thawb: to sew a garment and mend its holes – the adviser gently “mends” his brother’s faults.

Nasaha al-‘asal: to purify honey from wax – so advice must be “pure” (khalisah) from deception.

A powerful sentence

«الدِّينُ النَّصِيحَةُ»

Both words are definite (“the religion is the advice”), which shows advice is the very heart of the religion – like the hadith «الحَجُّ عَرَفَة» “Hajj is ‘Arafah”.

Question: why didn’t he say “advice is part of the religion”?

“Part of the religion” would make it one small piece; the definite form makes it the essence that enters every part of the religion.

The five areas of sincere advice

Building understanding

1

لِلَّهِ

To Allah

Believing in Him, worshipping Him sincerely and obeying Him.

2

لِكِتابِهِ

To His Book

Believing in it, reciting it, reflecting on it, acting on it.

3

لِرَسولِهِ

To His Messenger ﷺ

Believing him, loving him and following his Sunnah.

4

لِأَئِمَّةِ الْمُسْلِمين

To the leaders

Obeying them in good, helping them and praying for them.

5

لِعامَّتِهِم

To ordinary Muslims

Guiding them, loving good for them, never deceiving them.

Notice the order: advice starts with Allah, then widens until it includes all people.

From the Companions

Advice is a pledge

Jarir ibn ‘Abdillah (may Allah be pleased with him) said:

«بايَعْتُ رَسولَ اللهِ ﷺ عَلى إِقامِ الصَّلاةِ، وَإيتاءِ الزَّكاةِ، وَالنُّصْحِ لِكُلِّ مُسْلِمٍ»

“I pledged allegiance to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ to establish prayer, give zakah and give sincere advice to every Muslim.”

Agreed upon (al-Bukhari and Muslim)

A pledge, not an option

The Prophet ﷺ made advice part of the pledge itself.

Next to prayer and zakah

Mentioned with the greatest acts of Islam.

“To every Muslim”

Near or far, known or unknown to me.

Question: why did the Prophet ﷺ join advice with prayer and zakah in one pledge?

Model answers

Checking our understanding

1 Explain

The Prophet ﷺ made advice the religion itself: it is its pillar, like ‘Arafah is the greatest pillar of Hajj. The religion of a deceiver is not upright.

2 Infer the guidance

Advice is the pillar of the religion · it covers our relationship with Allah and people · good manners in asking · short statement then explanation.

3 Infer the areas

To Allah · His Book · His Messenger · the leaders · ordinary Muslims. Advice to Allah benefits the servant himself.

4 Explain the effects

Individual: correcting mistakes, stronger faith. Society: unity, less deception. Silence is deception when you can wisely prevent harm.

Reflect & critique

Correct and justify

“Advising someone in front of everyone works better than advising privately.”

✗

Advice should be private; public correction hurts and embarrasses.

“Part of advising leaders is obeying them in good and praying for them.”

✓

This is part of «وَلِأَئِمَّةِ الْمُسْلِمِين».

“If my friend rejects my advice, I have failed.”

✗

The adviser’s duty is to convey, not the result: “…but you do not like advisers.”

“Whoever sees a classmate cheat in a test and stays silent has failed in nasihah.”

✓

“Whoever deceives us is not one of us” – advise wisely and gently.

Advice and national identity

Our values, our nation

«…the leaders of the Muslims and their common people» includes sincere advice to the nation’s leaders and to society, as part of belonging and loyalty.

In the UAE this appears in respecting the law, praying for the leaders and taking part positively in serving the nation.
